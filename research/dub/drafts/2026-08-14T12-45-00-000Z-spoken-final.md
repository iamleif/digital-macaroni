# Dub review: plain-language final

Dub makes short links, but shortening a web address is not a good reason to pay for it. Plenty of free tools can do that.

The useful part is what happens after the link has been shared. Dub lets a creator or marketing team keep the same short address, change the page behind it, and see where the clicks came from. On higher plans, it can also connect a click to a signup or sale and run an affiliate program.

That makes Dub useful when a link has become part of the business. It is unnecessary when someone only wants a cleaner address to paste into one email.

## The redirect can save an old campaign

Imagine a company prints `brand.link/sale` on boxes, puts it in a YouTube description, and adds it to hundreds of social posts. The sale page later changes.

With Dub, the public short link can stay the same. The company changes its destination in one place, and future visitors go to the new page. This is called a redirect. It avoids editing old posts or accepting that printed material now sends people to a dead page.

Users mention this control as a practical benefit. One Trustpilot reviewer praised being able to update links from the dashboard. Others like the speed, folders, custom domains, and clear reports.

The click report can answer useful questions. It can show which social platform sent visitors, where those visitors were located, and which campaign link they used. One creator said this helped show sponsors that most clicks came from the United States.

## Free is enough to find out if this matters

The Free plan allows 25 new links and 1,000 tracked events each month. It includes one user, three custom domains, QR codes, API access, and 30 days of data.

That is enough for a creator or small company to use real campaign links. The key test is not whether the short address looks good. Change the destination after sharing it. Check whether the report answers a business question. If neither action matters, a paid plan will not add much.

Pro is $30 a month. It raises the limits to 1,000 new links and 50,000 tracked events, keeps data for one year, and supports three users. It also adds more ways to control a link, such as sending phone users into an app.

Business is $90 a month. It raises the limits again and adds sales and signup tracking, tests between two pages, event notices, and an affiliate program. Advanced is $300 for much higher use. The live page also shows a 14-day trial and a 10% yearly discount.

## Sales tracking is not automatic

Dub can show that a person clicked a link and later signed up or bought something. That is more useful than a click count because it shows which campaign led to money.

The setup needs work on the website and server. Dub's guide requires an analytics script and code that sends the signup or sale back to Dub. Sales tracking is available on Business and above.

This is where a simple link tool becomes a technical project. A marketer should ask a developer how the click will be saved, how a sale will be reported, and how the result will be tested. Paying $90 does not complete that connection.

## Affiliate complaints need to be separated

Dub can give partners their own links, record sales, calculate rewards, collect tax details, and send payouts. Business supports up to $2,500 in partner payouts each month and charges a 5% payout fee.

Trustpilot has 22 reviews at 3.7. Most are five-star reviews, but two sharp complaints pull the score down. One person reported missing payout paperwork and no reply for ten days. Dub replied that it could not find the support ticket. Another person said a promised reward was cut. Dub said the outside company running that program had changed the reward.

These are not the same problem. Dub controls its platform and support. The company using Dub controls its own reward rules. An affiliate should save the program terms and contact the program owner when the amount changes.

The complaints still show why partner programs need careful testing. A link can record the right sale while paperwork, program rules, or support creates a separate problem.

## Who should use Dub

We recommend Dub to creators and marketing teams that publish links in places they cannot easily edit. The ability to keep the address and change the final page is simple, useful, and easy to test on Free.

We also recommend looking at Business when a company needs to prove which links lead to signups or sales. Do that only after a developer has scoped the tracking work.

We do not recommend paying for Dub if the business needs a handful of ordinary links and rarely looks at the reports. In that case, the free plan or a simpler tool is enough.

Dub is not valuable because it removes characters from a URL. It is valuable when the company needs control over that URL after other people start using it.
