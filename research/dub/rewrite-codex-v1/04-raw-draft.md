Dub is a link manager for people who need to control a web address after sharing it. A creator or marketing team can change where a short link sends people and keep a record of each click. Higher plans connect those clicks to signups, sales, and partner rewards.

We recommend Dub when a link appears in videos, printed material, old posts, or paid campaigns. The public short address can remain the same even after the destination page changes. That control is worth far more than making a URL shorter.

The free plan is enough for light use and a proper trial. A paid plan makes sense when reports guide campaign spending or links directly affect revenue.

## A lasting link is the main reason to use Dub

A company may share the same campaign link across packaging and social posts. If the sale page moves, Dub can send future clicks to the new page without changing the public address. This is a redirect.

That job sounds small until the old link sits in places the company cannot edit. A dead page can waste years of traffic. Dub puts the destination in one dashboard, along with folders and custom domains for keeping links organized.

Fast link creation, easy destination changes, and clear reports receive consistent praise. One creator used the location data to show a sponsor that most clicks came from the United States. That is a useful business answer, while a raw click total may say very little.

The Free plan included 25 new links and 1,000 tracked events each month. It also had one user, three custom domains, QR codes, API access, and 30 days of data. Use it with a real campaign. Change the destination after sharing the link, then check whether the report answers a question the team cares about.

Skip a paid plan if neither action has value. A basic shortener can handle links that are temporary and rarely measured.

## Paid plans buy volume and longer memory

Pro was $30 per month when the evidence was collected. It raised the limit to 1,000 new links and 50,000 tracked events, kept one year of data, and supported three users. It also added more ways to direct people based on the device they use.

Business was $90 per month. It added signup and sales tracking, tests between two pages, event alerts, and partner programs. Advanced was $300 for much higher use. The pricing page also listed a 14-day trial and a yearly discount.

Pro is easiest to justify when a team creates many links and needs a longer history. Business is for a company that will act on sales data or pay partners. The feature name alone does not create that value. The tracking has to be set up correctly, and someone must use the result.

Self-hosting is also possible because Dub's core code is public. A small set of large-company features has a separate commercial license. Running the code yourself adds hosting and maintenance work, so it is a technical choice rather than a cheaper default.

## Sales tracking needs developer work

Dub can connect an early click to a later signup or purchase. This is more useful than counting visits because it can show which campaign led to revenue.

The connection is not automatic. Dub's setup guide calls for a script on the site and code on the server or in the app. That code sends the completed signup or sale back to Dub. Sales tracking starts on Business.

A marketer should ask a developer how the original click will be saved and when a sale will be sent. Test a normal purchase, a refund path if it applies, and a visitor who returns later. Check the totals against the store or billing system.

The $90 plan buys access to the tools. It does not finish the connection. Leave if the business cannot assign the setup or cannot trust the numbers after comparing them with its own records.

## Partner payouts add another party

Dub can give each partner a link, record sales, work out rewards, collect tax details, and send payouts. Business supported up to $2,500 in monthly partner payouts and charged a 5% payout fee.

A few Trustpilot complaints involved missing payout paperwork, slow support, or a changed reward. At least one reward dispute came from the outside company that ran the partner program. Dub provided the software, while that company set the reward.

Partners should save the program terms and know which company owes the money. Program owners should test the sale record, tax form, approval, and payout before adding many partners. Dub support still matters when the platform or paperwork fails.

We recommend Dub to creators and marketing teams whose links remain public for a long time. Free is generous enough to prove the redirect and reporting value. Pro suits higher volume and longer history. Business is worth considering only when sales tracking or partner payouts have a clear owner.

Dub is unnecessary for a handful of ordinary links that no one checks later. It becomes useful when an address must survive page changes and its clicks affect a business decision.
