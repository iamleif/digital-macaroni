# Claap review: plain-language final

Claap records meetings and short videos of your screen. It then writes a transcript and summary, stores the recording, and lets coworkers comment at the exact point they mean.

That combination is useful for a remote team. A product manager can explain a change in five minutes instead of booking a half-hour call. A teammate can watch later, click the moment that is unclear, and leave a question beside it. Someone who missed a customer meeting can read the summary and open only the part that matters.

We would consider Claap when a team already spends too much time repeating visual explanations or writing meeting notes. We would not buy it just to record more meetings. The recording needs to replace a call, settle a question, or create follow-up that people use.

## The useful part comes after recording

Making a screen video is not unusual. Claap's advantage is keeping the video, transcript, summary, notes, and team feedback in one place.

Time-stamped comments are a good example. Instead of writing that the button near the end of a six-minute demo is wrong, a designer can leave the note at the second the button appears. The person making the change sees the right screen and the comment together.

The same idea helps with meetings. A teammate who was absent does not always need to watch an hour. They can read the summary, search the transcript, and open the section where a decision was made.

This is why Claap's public feedback is positive. G2 shows 4.6 from 406 reviews, with easy recording and time savings among the most common strengths. Product Hunt feedback also praises transcripts, sharing, and team use.

The tool still needs a habit around it. Someone must give each recording a clear name, put it in the right place, and write the final decision where the team expects to find it. A folder full of videos does not become useful documentation by itself.

## Slow processing can spoil the handoff

Processing and loading delays are the complaint we would take most seriously. G2 groups dozens of comments around slow performance, loading, recording problems, and delays. Longer or higher-quality videos appear more likely to expose the problem.

That matters because Claap is often used as a handoff. If a person records an update before leaving work, the next person needs it to be ready. Waiting for the upload, transcript, or player weakens the reason for recording it.

Do not judge this with a one-minute sample. Record a meeting that is as long as the team's normal call. Then record a real screen explanation with the usual camera and display settings. Time how long both take to become watchable and searchable.

Playback is part of the same test. Ask a teammate on a normal home connection to open the link, jump between sections, and leave a comment. A video that records easily but loads badly for viewers has not solved the problem.

## AI notes save time only when they are checked

Claap can turn the transcript into a short recap and action items. Paid plans add better summaries, custom prompts, meeting details, and sales features such as follow-up emails and CRM updates.

These tools can remove the first round of note writing. They should not get the last word when a meeting includes a price, date, customer promise, product name, or person responsible for a task.

During the trial, choose four facts from a real call before looking at the summary. Check whether Claap captured each one correctly. Then check whether every action has the right owner and date. If someone must replay the whole meeting to repair the note, the summary did not save much time.

Recording also needs a clear privacy rule. The team should know when a call is recorded, who can open it, how long it stays, and what should never be recorded. Claap offers private areas and stronger controls on higher plans, but software settings do not replace consent and a retention policy.

## Basic is enough to prove the idea

Basic allows ten created videos per person, three uploaded videos, and 300 recorded minutes in total. Recordings stay for three months. It includes transcripts, a basic summary, browser recording, and a shared workspace.

That is enough for a focused test. It is not a lasting free plan for someone who records every week, because the ten-video limit is total rather than monthly.

Pro allows unlimited recordings, 1,000 recorded minutes a month, ten uploads a month, 500 AI credits for each license, and two years of storage. It also adds better summaries, video editing, private channels, and more connections to other tools.

Business removes the recording-time limit and adds sales work such as coaching, CRM completion, follow-up emails, and reports. Claap's current page did not expose readable public prices for Pro or Business during our research. Confirm the price and required licenses before choosing it. A workspace can mix free and paid licenses, so people who only watch may not need the same plan as people who record.

## Who should use Claap

We recommend trying Claap when a team wants to replace some meetings with short screen explanations, or when people regularly miss calls but need the decisions afterward. The combination of video, transcript, summary, and comments is practical.

We would skip it when the team already has a meeting recorder it trusts, or when people rarely watch recorded updates. We would also stop if a normal-length video takes too long to process or the important facts in the summary need heavy repair.

The trial is simple. Record one real meeting and one real screen explanation. Time the processing, check four facts, and ask a teammate to find one decision and leave one comment without help.

If that replaces a call or makes the follow-up easier, Claap has done something useful. If it only creates another library to search, it has added work instead.
