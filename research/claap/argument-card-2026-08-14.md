# Claap private argument card

- Buyer: a team that already records meetings or sends screen explanations
- Job: make a recorded conversation useful to people who were absent and turn it into clear follow-up
- Thesis: Claap is worth considering when a short recording replaces a meeting or keeps feedback beside the exact moment; it is not useful if recordings become an unwatched library
- Main reason to buy: recording, summary, and time-specific comments live together
- Main reason to hesitate: processing delays and weak organization can erase the time saved
- When it is unnecessary: the team can explain the work more clearly in a short message or already has a trusted meeting recorder
- Decision rule: record one normal-length meeting and one real screen explanation, time processing, check four critical facts in the summary, and have a teammate find one decision without help
- Evidence limit: research-based; no hands-on test
