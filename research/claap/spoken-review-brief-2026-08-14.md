# Claap spoken review brief

## What is it?

Claap records a meeting or a short video of your screen. It writes a transcript and summary, then lets coworkers comment at the exact part of the video they mean.

## Who will find it useful?

Remote teams that spend too much time repeating product updates, design feedback, sales-call notes, or training. It helps only when people actually watch the recording or use the follow-up.

## What is good?

Recording and feedback stay together. Someone can miss a meeting, read the summary, jump to the relevant moment, and ask a question without another call.

## What is wrong?

Processing and playback delays come up often enough to test. A large video library can also become hard to search and maintain. The summary must be checked when names, numbers, dates, or promises matter.

## What does it cost?

Basic gives each person ten videos and 300 total recording minutes. Pro allows unlimited recordings but caps recorded time at 1,000 minutes a month. Business removes that time limit and adds sales tools. Claap did not show readable public Pro or Business prices during research, so confirm the quote before planning a rollout.

## Our answer

Record one real meeting and one real screen explanation. Time how long each takes to become watchable. Check four important facts in the summary and ask a teammate to find one decision without help. If that saves a call and the follow-up is accurate, Claap is useful. If the recording only adds another place to look, skip it.
