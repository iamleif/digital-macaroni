# Wrike writer's notebook

## Research scope

- Research-based review. Digital Macaroni did not use Wrike.
- Current official plan and feature pages were checked on August 14, 2026.
- Evidence includes three large review platforms, two current community talks, launch coverage, and an older hands-on review.

## Reader and job

- Primary reader: a team with work arriving from several departments or clients.
- Main job: turn each request into assigned work, move it through review, and show managers what is late or blocked.
- Failure cost: people keep private spreadsheets, work is copied between tools, and managers stop trusting the reports.

## Product facts

- Free costs $0. Team is $10 per user each month and Business is $25, with displayed prices billed yearly.
- Team supports 2 to 15 users. Business starts at 5 users and supports up to 200.
- Subscriptions are sold in seat groups. Business and higher plans are annual.
- Wrike includes tasks, forms, custom fields and statuses, proofing, dashboards, reports, automation, workload planning, integrations, and AI features across different plans.

## Repeated patterns

- Forms, proofing, custom steps, and reports are useful for creative and service teams.
- Wrike works best after someone designs the setup and teaches the team how to use it.
- The number of choices can make simple task work harder than it needs to be.
- Seat groups, annual terms, add-ons, and upgrades are a real source of frustration.

## Feature translation

- Request form: a page where a coworker or client gives the details needed to start a job.
- Workflow: the steps a task follows, such as new, working, waiting for review, and done.
- Proofing: comments and approval placed on the image, video, or document being reviewed.
- Resource planning: seeing who has too much work and who has room.
- Cross-tagging: showing the same task in more than one project without making a copy.

## Conflict ledger

- Easy task creation and difficult company setup concern different levels of use.
- Broad features save tools only when the team uses them; unused options become clutter.
- Strong product praise and pricing anger concern different parts of the purchase.

## Missing evidence

- No live workspace, migration, outside guest, mobile use, automation failure, or report check.
- No support request or renewal quote.
- No measured time saved after adoption.

## Spoken language bank

- “A request arrives, becomes a task, gets reviewed, and appears in the same report.”
- “Wrike needs an owner.”
- “Do not pay for a large system to keep a small to-do list.”
- “Use the real seat count, not the per-user headline.”
