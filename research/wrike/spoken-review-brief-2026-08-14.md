# Wrike spoken review brief

Wrike gives a company one place to collect work, assign it, review it, and see what is late. It is more than a shared to-do list.

It is useful when a request comes from another team or a client. A form can collect the details. Wrike can then create the work, send it through the right steps, keep comments on the file, and place the result in a report.

That depth is good for a team with messy work. It is too much for five people who only need owners and due dates.

Wrike also needs someone to decide how the account works. If every team creates its own fields and statuses, the shared reports will not mean much.

Before buying, build one real process and hand it to normal users. Ask a client to submit a request and review a file. Then price the exact number and type of seats, since Wrike sells paid accounts in groups.
