# Saner.AI writer's notebook

## Research scope

- Research-based review; no product test
- Supabase packet refreshed against current pricing, App Store, and Reddit on 2026-08-14
- Product Hunt is strongly positive; the App Store currently shows 3.0 from 13 ratings
- Main limit: the product changes quickly and the public samples are small

## Reader and job

- Primary reader: someone with scattered notes and tasks who wants help deciding what to do next
- Saner.AI speaks directly to people with ADHD
- Cost of failure: more duplicate tasks, more cleanup, and another system that must be remembered and managed

## Product facts

- Brings notes, tasks, email, calendar, Google Drive, and Slack into one assistant
- Lets a person dump thoughts into the app, turn them into tasks, and ask questions across saved material
- Current page lists Free, Starter at $8 monthly, and Standard at $16 monthly, with a $12 early-user offer shown for Standard
- Free includes 100 notes and 30 AI messages each month

## Experience records

### Brain dump became reminders

- Source: App Store
- Result: user praised the app for making sense of messy thoughts and turning them into reminders
- Consequence: this is the clearest version of Saner.AI's promise

### Daily planning felt proactive

- Source: App Store
- Result: user liked being told what to do each morning and praised email support
- Consequence: the assistant can be useful when it reduces the need to plan from a blank page

### Task edits created more work

- Source: Reddit
- Context: user subscribed after trying the product for ADHD support
- Result: changing a task often created a duplicate; tasks could not be deleted through the assistant; subtasks were missed; web and mobile completion did not stay in sync
- Consequence: the product can add cleanup to the exact job it promises to simplify

### Mobile task controls failed

- Source: App Store
- Result: slow screen, controls not available, editing a task caused a crash
- Consequence: test basic task actions before connecting the rest of a personal system

## Repeated patterns

- Quick capture and asking questions about personal notes are the strongest parts
- Turning a brain dump into a first task list can be genuinely helpful
- Editing, deleting, moving, and syncing tasks have produced the most damaging complaints
- The ADHD message raises the standard: the product must reduce cleanup, not merely move it

## Feature translation

### Personal knowledge assistant

- What it does: searches notes and connected accounts when the user asks a question
- Who cares: people who remember having saved something but cannot remember where
- When it does not matter: the user's notes already live in one simple, searchable place

### Brain dump to tasks

- What it does: turns a messy paragraph into separate reminders or tasks
- Main limit: duplicate or incomplete tasks create more work than writing the list by hand

### Proactive planning

- What it does: suggests what to work on instead of waiting for the user to build a plan
- Main limit: the suggestion is useful only when the underlying tasks and dates are correct

## Conflict ledger

- Saner.AI can help one person start the day while making another person's task list harder to manage.
- Product Hunt praise and the 3.0 App Store score can both be real because early web users and mobile task users are testing different parts of the product.

## Missing evidence

- No hands-on test of task creation, editing, deletion, and sync
- No dependable long-term sample from paid users
- No confirmation that the cancellation complaint or mobile crashes are fixed

## Spoken language bank

- dump everything in one place
- turn messy thoughts into a first list
- find the note without remembering where it lives
- creates a new task instead of fixing the old one
- an assistant for an overwhelmed person cannot create more cleanup
