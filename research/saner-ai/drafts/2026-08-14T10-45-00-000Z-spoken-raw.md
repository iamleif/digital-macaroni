# Saner.AI review: spoken raw draft

Saner.AI is built for a person who has notes and tasks everywhere and does not want to organize all of them by hand. You can dump in a messy thought, ask the assistant to turn it into reminders, and search old notes without remembering where you saved them.

That is a useful idea, especially for someone with ADHD. The problem is that the assistant can still make basic task work harder. When changing a task creates another copy or finished items do not match between phone and web, the user ends up cleaning the system instead of using it.

## The brain-dump idea is genuinely useful

Starting with an empty task list can be harder than doing the work. Saner.AI lets you write the thought as it arrives, then asks the software to pull out the useful notes and tasks.

Some users like that part a lot. One App Store reviewer said the app made sense of messy thoughts and turned them into reminders. Another liked opening the app in the morning and being told what to work on. This is the clearest reason to try Saner.AI.

The assistant can also search notes and information pulled from email, calendar, Google Drive, and Slack. That helps when you remember saving something but not where it lives. It matters less if your notes already sit in one simple app and search works well.

## Basic task mistakes create the wrong kind of work

The detailed task complaints are hard to ignore because they hit the product's main promise.

One Reddit user subscribed after trying Saner.AI for ADHD support. Asking the assistant to change a task often created a new copy instead. The assistant could not delete tasks, move one to a different list, or reliably recreate its subtasks. Completed items on the web did not always show as completed on the phone.

That left the user deleting old versions by hand and spending the daily AI allowance explaining the same change. The tool was supposed to remove steps. It added more.

The App Store has similar complaints in shorter form. One person said editing a task caused the app to crash. Another could not mark a checklist item as finished. The current App Store score is 3.0 from 13 ratings, far below the much more positive Product Hunt page.

## Test the boring actions first

Do not begin by moving every note into Saner.AI. Create a small list and do the ordinary things that have to work every day. Change a due date. Move a task. Add a subtask. Mark it done on the phone and check the web. Delete it through the assistant.

If those steps work, then connect email, calendar, or Google Drive. If they do not, adding more information will give the app more places to make a mess.

This test may sound too basic for an AI assistant. That is the point. A clever answer about old notes does not make up for a task list you cannot trust.

## Pricing

Saner.AI has a free plan with 100 notes and 30 AI messages each month. Starter is listed at $8 a month. Standard is listed at $16, while the page also advertises a $12 early-user price.

The free plan is enough to run the task test. Paid plans raise the limits and add more room for notes and files, but higher limits do not solve a bad task action. Pay only after the small list stays correct across the devices you use.

## Who should use Saner.AI

We recommend trying Saner.AI if messy thoughts are easy to capture but hard to turn into a first plan. Its approach is more useful than asking someone who feels overloaded to build a perfect folder system before getting help.

We do not recommend paying for it until task editing, deletion, and phone sync work on your account. If the assistant creates three versions of the same reminder, it is doing the opposite of what an ADHD-focused product should do.

Saner.AI understands the problem. It still needs to become much better at the boring parts of solving it.
