Morgen is for people whose day is split across too many places. Work meetings are in Google Calendar. Personal appointments are in iCloud. Tasks might be in Todoist, Notion, Linear, or Microsoft To Do. Morgen brings those items into one view so you can work out what happens today.

That sounds simple, but it is the whole point. Morgen is not another place to write a longer task list. It should stop you opening four apps every morning just to see whether there is time to finish anything.

We think it can be useful for that job. We would not pay for it after a tidy demo week, though. The real test is whether changes stay correct when the week gets messy.

## What Morgen actually does

Morgen connects to several calendar and task services. It shows meetings, appointments, deadlines, and tasks on the same screen. You can then drag a task onto the calendar to reserve time for it. This is usually called time blocking. In normal language, it means deciding that the proposal gets 9:00 to 10:30 instead of hoping it gets done sometime today.

The product also has an AI Planner. It can suggest or move blocks based on rules such as working hours and deadlines. Booking pages and scheduling links let someone else choose a time that appears free. Calendar automations can add rules such as a buffer around a meeting.

It runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, mobile, and in a browser. The Linux app matters. Many planning tools treat Linux users as an afterthought or give them only a web page.

All of this is useful only if the combined view is correct. If a meeting moves in Outlook, Morgen needs to show the new time. If a task is completed in Todoist, it should not remain open in the plan. If a booking link says 2:00 is free, every calendar that matters needs to agree.

## The best reason to use it

Morgen makes the most sense when the shape of the day is hard to see.

Imagine a consultant with a company calendar, a personal calendar, and client tasks in another app. None of those tools is broken. The problem is that each one shows only part of the day. The consultant can promise work without noticing a school appointment. A large task can sit on a list for a week because no time was reserved for it.

Putting those items together helps answer a better question than “What is on my list?” It answers “When can I actually do this?”

Combining calendars and tasks is the clearest strength in the public feedback. People also like the flexible layout and the ability to use the same planning system on different desktop platforms. There is praise for helpful support too.

If you already use one calendar and one task list, this is much less interesting. You can block time directly in the calendar you have. Adding Morgen would give you another account, another bill, and another copy of the same day.

## Sync is the part to test

Morgen sits between services made by other companies. That makes the update in both directions more important than the first connection.

Do not test this by linking an empty calendar and admiring the layout. Use the accounts that make real life difficult. Move a meeting in the original calendar and check Morgen. Move another meeting in Morgen and check the original. Complete a task on each side. Change its deadline. Disconnect the internet, make a change if the app allows it, then see what happens after the connection returns.

Repeated tasks need special attention. Complete one occurrence. Move one occurrence without changing the rest. Then change the whole series. Do the same work in the source task app and in Morgen. A repeated task that comes back after completion or changes every occurrence by mistake will make the plan hard to trust.

Also cross a time zone or a daylight-saving change if travel or international calls are normal for you. The meeting needs to appear at the right local hour everywhere. Then share a booking link and make a real test booking from another account. Confirm that the selected time is blocked in every calendar it should be.

The goal is not perfection in a five-minute setup. The goal is to find out whether Morgen removes checking. If you keep opening the original apps to make sure Morgen is right, it has created a second checking job.

## The phone has to handle a bad day

A calendar tool cannot live only on a large monitor. Plans change while someone is walking to a train, sitting in a car park, or leaving a meeting.

Mobile quality appears as a concern in the public sample. That does not tell us how the current app will behave on your phone. It does tell us not to assume the desktop experience carries over.

Take one crowded day and manage it from the phone. Move a task around two meetings. Add an appointment. Mark work complete. Open the original calendar and task app to confirm the changes. Try it on a weak connection. Check whether the screen makes conflicts obvious without pinching and scrolling all over the place.

People who depend on keyboard control, screen readers, zoom, or strong color contrast should test those needs directly. Accessibility controls also appear as a reported limit. A planning view can look polished and still be difficult to use for eight hours a day.

## Automatic planning should reduce decisions

The AI Planner can be useful when a week has more tasks than open time. It can place work around meetings and adjust blocks when the schedule changes.

Do not judge it by whether it fills the calendar. Any automatic planner can make a busy-looking week. Judge it by the choices it makes.

Give it one important task, one short flexible task, a hard deadline, lunch, travel time, and a period when you do not want focused work. Then let a meeting move. See what the planner protects and what it pushes away. Check whether it splits work into sensible pieces or leaves an unrealistic block at the end of the day.

If fixing its plan takes longer than making your own, turn it off. Morgen can still be useful as a combined view and a manual time-blocking tool. The automatic part does not need to win for the rest of the product to work.

## Price changes the decision

The Individual plan costs $30 when paid month to month. It costs $15 per month when billed yearly, or $180 for the year. That is a large discount, but it also asks the buyer to commit before the product has lived through much real work.

The Team plan costs $25 per person month to month. The annual rate is $10 per person per month, with at least two seats. Morgen includes unlimited calendar and task integrations, planning tools, scheduling, and its apps in those paid plans.

There is a 14-day trial and no card is required. The company also states a 30-day money-back guarantee. Academic and nonprofit buyers can ask for a 25% discount. People switching from another tool can ask for 15% off.

Use the trial before choosing the annual rate. Two weeks is enough to include a packed weekday, a quiet day, a weekend boundary, several moved meetings, and repeated tasks. It is not enough to prove every rare sync problem, so keep the original data and know how to leave.

At $30 a month, Morgen needs to save real time or prevent missed work. A nicer calendar is not enough. At the annual rate, the price is easier to justify, but only after the workflow works.

## Who should choose Morgen?

Morgen is a good product to test if you have several calendars, tasks in another service, and no clear place to plan the whole day. It is also one of the more practical options for someone who wants a proper Linux app.

It is harder to recommend to someone who works mainly from a phone, has specific accessibility needs that the trial does not meet, or already controls the day with one calendar and one task list. It is also a poor choice if connected data often needs manual checking.

We did not connect an account or run these tests ourselves. This review is based on current product information and patterns across public feedback. The sample is smaller than the sample for the largest calendar tools, so the trial has to carry more of the decision.

Our rule is straightforward. Connect the real work and personal calendars plus the task systems you actually use. Plan one messy week. Change items from both sides. Test recurring work, a time-zone case, the phone, automatic planning, and a booking link.

Count missing, late, duplicate, and stale items. Also count every time you open a source app because you do not trust the combined view. Keep Morgen if that checking mostly stops. If it does not, the product has not solved the reason for buying it.
