# Morgen writer's notebook

## Research scope

- Research-based review; no hands-on account
- Pricing checked 2026-08-14
- 33 Product Hunt, 25 G2, 12 Trustpilot, a Capterra profile, and 11 community voices

## Reader and job

- Primary reader: a person whose work calendar, personal calendar, and tasks live in several apps
- Plain example: see a work meeting, a family appointment, and a Todoist task in one place, then reserve 90 minutes to finish the task
- Cost of failure: a moved meeting stays stale, a repeated task appears twice, or the person checks every original app anyway

## Product facts

- Combines Google, Outlook, iCloud, Fastmail, Zoho, and other calendars
- Connects task tools such as Todoist, Notion, Linear, ClickUp, Google Tasks, Microsoft To Do, and Obsidian
- Windows, macOS, Linux, mobile, and browser apps
- Time blocking, automatic planning, scheduling links, booking page, and calendar automations
- Individual: $30 monthly or $15 per month billed yearly
- Team: $25 per seat monthly or $10 per seat monthly billed yearly; two-seat minimum
- 14-day trial with no card and stated 30-day money-back guarantee

## Experience records

### Several accounts became one daily plan

- Calendar and task consolidation is the clearest positive pattern
- Consequence: useful when scattered tools force a person to assemble the day by hand

### A repeating task did not behave as expected

- Recurring-task reliability appears in the community sample
- Consequence: test one changed instance, the whole series, completion, and changes made in the source app

### Desktop planning did not travel well to the phone

- Mobile quality appears as a mixed complaint
- Consequence: reschedule a busy day from the phone before deciding the workflow works

## Feature translation

### Time blocking

- Dragging a task onto the calendar to reserve actual time for it

### Automatic planning

- Letting Morgen suggest or move task blocks based on deadlines, working hours, and other rules

### Calendar automation

- A rule that changes calendar behavior, such as adding travel or buffer time

### Scheduling link

- A link that lets another person choose from times that Morgen thinks are free

## Conflict ledger

- One planning view saves time only when every connected item stays current.
- The wide desktop product and Linux support are strengths; the phone may still be a weak point.
- The annual plan is far cheaper than paying monthly; that discount can push a buyer to commit before the workflow is proven.
- Helpful support praise exists beside complaints about speed and product controls.

## Missing evidence

- No calendar or task connection
- No two-way sync, conflict, time-zone, daylight-saving, recurring-task, mobile, offline, accessibility, booking-link, automation, automatic-planning, or support test
- No measured time saving against using the source apps directly

- a second checking job
- plan one messy week
- keep it only if the original apps can stay closed
