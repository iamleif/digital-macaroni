Puzzle makes startup accounting easier for a founder to read. It connects banks, cards, payment services, payroll, and other startup systems. The dashboard then shows cash, spending, burn, runway, revenue, and financial statements.

That view can save time. It can also make a wrong category or revenue date look finished. Puzzle holds the general ledger, so the entries under the chart matter more than the chart itself.

I recommend Puzzle only for a startup whose accountant can close two hard months in both systems. Do not replace the old ledger because Puzzle looks simpler. Complex companies and teams without expert review should skip it.

## The founder view comes last

Puzzle includes transaction categories, account reconciliation, journal entries, period locks, cash and accrual books, statements, founder metrics, revenue recognition, and migration tools. Connections can reduce copying from modern startup services.

Cash, burn, and runway are useful only after the books close. A duplicate transfer can overstate spending. A customer prepayment recorded on the wrong date can distort revenue and tax work.

Use the founder dashboard after every bank, card, payment, payroll, loan, and equity balance has been checked. Recalculate the key totals outside Puzzle. The clean view should explain proven books rather than replace the close.

## Give the accountant two ugly months

The founder should not design the trial alone. Ask the accountant or bookkeeper responsible for the books to choose two real months. Include the hardest normal activity.

Test payroll and tax, card charges, refunds, disputes, annual customer payments, prepaid software, a loan payment, founder expenses, unpaid bills, foreign currency, transfers, duplicates, and reversed transactions where relevant.

For each case, record the right account, date, amount, file, and treatment. Import the same history into Puzzle and the old ledger. Compare entries and final statements.

This review is not accounting, tax, audit, legal, or investment advice. The company's qualified advisers must set its policies.

## Automation needs a visible exception path

Automatic categories can remove routine work. They can also repeat a bad rule across many transactions. A high accuracy rate says little when the rare error changes revenue, payroll, tax, or cash.

Test similar vendors that belong in different accounts. Split a transaction, correct a suggestion, attach evidence, and add a note. Then undo the change and inspect who changed what and when.

The accountant needs a clear queue for exceptions. Corrections should apply to the intended items and stay fixed. Public feedback from experienced users raises concerns about category accuracy and overrides, though some criticism comes from competitors.

Fast wrong books are still wrong.

## Every balance must reconcile

Close both test months. Match each bank and card to its statement. Tie payment sales, fees, refunds, disputes, and payout timing to source records. Match payroll cost, tax, cash, and liabilities.

Check unpaid bills, customer balances, loans, equity, prepaid costs, and deferred revenue. Compare the trial balance, profit and loss, balance sheet, and cash activity between ledgers.

Investigate every difference instead of forcing totals to agree. Lock the first month and try to change it. Test the approved correction route and inspect the history left behind.

## Revenue timing deserves its own file

Puzzle may appeal to software companies with prepaid plans. Use a monthly sale, annual prepayment, midterm upgrade, discount, refund, cancellation, and a service date that differs from payment.

Have the accountant approve an outside revenue schedule. Compare each month's earned revenue and remaining liability with Puzzle. Tie customer cash to the payment service and both statement totals to the ledger.

Software can calculate a schedule. The accountant decides whether the policy and inputs are correct.

## Reports and exports decide whether you can leave

Build the package used by the board, investors, and tax preparer. Include the exact statements, budget comparison, revenue, burn, and runway the company needs. Check dates, comparison periods, rounding, and exports.

Export the general ledger, trial balance, chart of accounts, journals, statements, attachments, vendor details, matches, and change history. Give the package to someone who did not set it up.

The company should be able to move or answer an audit question without screenshots. If key evidence stays trapped in Puzzle, the migration has created a new risk.

## Pricing rises with accounting depth

When our evidence was collected, standard annual rates were $25 monthly for Starter, $60 for Core, $100 for Complete, and $300 for Scale. Monthly list rates were $30, $72, $120, and $360.

The 14-day trial included Complete access. Discounts and a close-time guarantee had detailed conditions. Read those conditions and check current plans before buying.

Price the tier that covers the real close, revenue rules, controls, and reports. Add accountant time, cleanup, migration, and parallel books. A lower software bill has little value if the close takes longer or needs manual repair.

## Who should choose Puzzle?

Puzzle is worth testing for an early-stage, single-entity startup with modern financial connections and direct books. Its founder reporting can make a closed month much easier to understand.

It is a weak fit for complex entities or teams that need mature firm workflows and deep controls. Independent review coverage is thin for a product holding the general ledger. We did not migrate books, close a month, file tax work, or contact support.

Have the accountant close and reconcile two difficult months in both systems. Test exceptions, locks, revenue timing, reports, and complete exports. Trace every important dashboard number to its source.

Keep Puzzle when the accountant can explain the differences and sign off on the books. Until then, runway is only a clean chart built on unproven entries.
