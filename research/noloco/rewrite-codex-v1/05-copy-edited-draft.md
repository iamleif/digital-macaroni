# Noloco is quick when the process already has shape

Noloco turns tables and business records into an app for staff and clients. An agency can show its team the whole project while each client sees only their own files, dates, comments, bills, and approvals.

That is much faster than building a basic portal from scratch. The speed depends on a process that already fits records, roles, forms, and actions. Large databases or unusual product design can push Noloco beyond its best use.

I recommend Noloco for an agency or service firm with a clear process. Avoid it until real data stays in sync and two test clients can reach only the records they should see.

## A known process can become an app quickly

Picture an agency table with one row for each project, holding the client, owner, due date, file, status, and approval. Staff need the full table, while clients need a small private window into their own work.

Noloco can create both views from the same records. A client may download a draft, leave a comment, send a form, or approve a step. A button can update the status or start more work.

Fast setup and flexible data connections receive broad praise. Support also has a strong record in the larger samples. The useful part is concrete: staff keep the working view, and clients stop asking for access to an internal table.

Visual freedom is more limited than a blank website or custom app. That trade is fine for a private project portal. It becomes harder to accept when the interface itself is the product being sold.

## Data links must survive a real load

Noloco can connect to tools such as Airtable, Google Sheets, Xano, Postgres, MySQL, HubSpot, and Supabase. This avoids a full data move, but records must still arrive quickly and stay correct on both sides.

One disputed G2 account describes Xano sync failures, duplicate cleanup, slow editing, record limits, and a refund fight. Noloco said the customer exceeded the supported scale and disputed key parts of the story. The old plan in that complaint does not match current pricing.

The dispute remains a useful sizing warning. Load a large sample of the real records, then create and edit entries from Noloco and the source. Measure delays, look for duplicates, and ask what happens when the account reaches its limit.

Get the expected record count and support route in writing before moving a large operation. Stop if staff must keep checking the source because the portal cannot be trusted.

## Client access needs an unfriendly test

Private portals fail badly when a client can see another client's job. A clean home screen cannot prove that the rules underneath it are safe.

Create client A and client B. Try changing record links, using search, opening old email notices, and following file URLs. Check whether either account can edit dates, staff notes, or fields meant only for viewing.

Remove one user and test the old links again. Check every email for private fields. Repeat the same work from a phone because a narrow screen can hide the sign that someone is in the wrong record.

Noloco has tools for access by record and field. The person building the app still owns the setup and the test. No client data should move into the portal before these checks pass.

## Current limits belong in the budget

When our evidence was collected, Free included unlimited apps and team seats, fifty client seats, 2,000 records per app, and community support. That was enough to test real roles and a complete job.

Build cost $79 monthly with ten team seats, up to 1,000 client seats, 25,000 records, 3,000 workflow runs, and live chat and email support. Extra team seats cost $6. Each extra block of 25,000 records cost $25 monthly, up to 250,000 records before a custom plan.

Confirm current terms before buying. Count records, staff, clients, files, and automatic runs for the next twelve months. Include deleted or archived work if it still counts toward a limit.

The base price can be fair when Noloco replaces custom portal work. Growth blocks and extra seats can change that answer. A spreadsheet with expected monthly volume will tell you more than a small demo.

## Verdict

Noloco is a strong choice for an agency or service firm with a clear process and a moderate data set. It can place useful screens, forms, comments, and actions over records much faster than custom development. Data sync, record limits, and client access deserve hard tests before launch. Use two client accounts and a real load. Choose Noloco when both stay correct. Look elsewhere for huge data or a highly custom public product.
