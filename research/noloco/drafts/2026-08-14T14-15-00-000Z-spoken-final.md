# Noloco review: plain-language final

Noloco turns business records into an app for staff and clients. An agency can keep projects, files, invoices, and approvals in one system. Staff see the full work. Each client signs in and sees only the records that belong to them.

This is useful when the process already exists in Airtable, Google Sheets, a database, or several tools. The people using it get something cleaner than the working table.

Noloco is faster than building a custom portal for this kind of standard work. It becomes less convincing when the business needs an unusual design, a very large database, or behavior far outside the ready-made views and workflows.

## It puts a usable screen over business data

Imagine a video agency tracks projects in a table. Each row has a client, due date, editor, file link, status, and approval. The table works for staff, but the agency should not give every client access to it.

Noloco can turn those rows into a staff board and a client portal. The client can see a project, download a draft, leave a comment, and approve it. Permission rules hide other clients and internal fields. A button can change the status or start another action.

Users often praise this path from data to working app. G2 currently shows 4.8 from 36 reviews. Capterra has 13 more. Ease, quick setup, flexible workflows, and responsive support appear often.

This is also where Noloco differs from a blank website builder. Its screens are built around records and business actions. That makes it quick when the process fits. It can feel limited when a team wants complete freedom over every visual detail.

## Free is big enough to test a real process

The current Free plan includes unlimited apps and team seats, 50 client seats, and 2,000 records in each app. It includes community support and 100 workflow runs. Apps carry a small Noloco label.

That is enough to load real work, not just sample rows. A team can create staff and client accounts, test what each role sees, submit a form, change a status, and check the phone view.

Build is $79 a month. It includes 10 team seats, up to 1,000 client seats, 25,000 records, 3,000 workflow runs, 20GB of files, and live chat and email support. It also connects the full set of listed data sources, including Airtable, Sheets, Xano, Postgres, MySQL, HubSpot, and Supabase.

Extra team seats cost $6 each. Each extra block of 25,000 records costs $25 a month, up to 250,000 total records. Larger needs move to a custom Enterprise plan. Extra file storage and AI building credits can also add cost.

This pricing is simpler than the older Starter, Pro, and Business structure found in past reviews. Buyers should use the current page, not an old review, when calculating the bill.

## Records and sync need a real load test

Noloco can connect to an outside database and show those records in the app. That saves a move, but it creates another question. How quickly and safely do changes move between the two systems?

One detailed G2 reviewer described a very bad outcome with Xano. The account had tens of thousands of rows. The reviewer reported sync failures, duplicate cleanup, a strict row limit, an editor that took minutes to change a setting, and a dispute over refunds and support.

Noloco replied that row limits had always existed, that the team spent hours helping, and that the project exceeded what the platform was meant to support. It also disputed the refund account.

We cannot settle that dispute. It does show what a buyer must test. Load a useful sample of the real data. Change records in both places. Measure the delay. Ask what happens at the limit. Get the expected record volume and support path in writing before moving a large operation.

The current Build plan now lists 25,000 records with paid blocks above that. The old 50,000-row complaint does not describe today's exact price structure. The risk of choosing the wrong scale still applies.

## Permissions are the part that cannot be guessed

A portal is only useful if each person sees the right data. Create at least two client accounts. Confirm that client A cannot open client B's record by changing a link, searching, or following a notification.

Also check the smaller details. Can clients edit a due date they should only view? Can staff see private notes? Does an email include an internal field? Does a removed user still count or keep access?

Noloco provides record and field permission tools. The builder still has to set and test them. A polished client page does not prove the underlying rules are safe.

## Who should use Noloco

We recommend Noloco to agencies and service firms with a known process and a clear set of records. It is especially useful when staff need a full working view and clients need a simpler private view of the same job.

Start with 100 real records and one complete job. Test both roles, the outside data link, a workflow, and the phone screen. Then estimate the rows, team members, clients, files, and workflow runs for the next year.

We do not recommend Noloco as the first choice for a highly custom public product or a system expected to handle huge data sets without careful testing. That is where its ready-made strengths can become boundaries.

Noloco can remove months of custom portal work. It cannot remove the need to size the data and test who can see it.
