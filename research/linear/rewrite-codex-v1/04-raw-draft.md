# Linear is fast because it makes the hard choices

Linear is an issue tracker for software teams. It handles bugs, product work, short cycles, and plans for future releases. The reason to choose it is not a giant feature list. It is the chance to make project tracking feel like less work.

Across user discussions and reviews, the same praise keeps appearing. Linear feels fast and the interface stays clean. Keyboard controls make routine actions quick, while Slack and GitHub links fit the tools many software teams already use.

I recommend Linear for a small product and engineering team that wants strong defaults. I would avoid it when deep reporting, detailed approvals, or one shared system for every department matters more.

## The speed helps a team tell the truth

A project board is useful only while people update it. When creating, finding, and moving issues feels slow, the board starts drifting away from the real work.

Linear attacks that daily friction. Reviewers repeatedly mention quick navigation, a calm interface, and keyboard shortcuts. Teams moving from Jira often describe less setup and less maintenance.

That matters more than it sounds. A fast board gets small updates that a heavier system may miss. The team spends less time managing the tracker and has a better chance of seeing what is truly blocked.

The benefit is strongest for a team that already thinks in issues, cycles, and releases. Linear gives that team a clear home without asking it to design a project system first.

## Clear defaults also create hard limits

Linear's speed comes with a defined way of working. That is helpful until the team's real process does not fit.

Reporting is the clearest concern. Several sources describe reports as limited, especially for leaders who want to compare teams or answer unusual questions. Custom workflow options can also feel narrow. Labels may end up carrying structure that deserves its own field or state.

A small team may never care. A large organization probably will. More departments bring more approvals, ownership rules, planning views, and reporting needs. Linear can then require workarounds or a separate data tool.

Run one active project through a pilot. Include the manager who needs reports and the person with the strangest workflow. If either has to rebuild the work elsewhere, the speed advantage is already shrinking.

## Software teams get the best version

Linear speaks the language of product and engineering work. That focus is a strength for its main buyer. Bugs, issues, cycles, and releases make immediate sense.

The same language can make adoption harder for marketing, sales, operations, or client-service teams. They may need campaign calendars, intake forms, detailed approvals, or views that Linear does not emphasize.

Do not assume a tool that delights engineering will become a calm company-wide workspace. Decide whether you need the best issue tracker for software work or one flexible system for many kinds of work. Those are different purchases.

## Account risk needs a practical check

Most of the product feedback in our evidence is positive. The service feedback is less reassuring.

Trustpilot had a small sample when checked. It included serious complaints about account access, workspace data, and support. These are individual reports, and we could not verify them. The sample is too small to describe the experience of most customers.

The complaints still deserve attention because Linear may hold a team's working record. Before moving important work, check account recovery, user ownership, exports, and how quickly support answers a real question. Keep a recent export until you trust those systems.

The contrast between platforms is also useful. G2 showed a much stronger score from a larger sample, while Trustpilot's smaller service-focused sample was negative. That suggests the product itself satisfies many users, while account problems can feel severe for the people who encounter them.

## Who should choose Linear

Choose Linear for a small software team that values quick updates, clear defaults, and a focused issue workflow. It is especially appealing when the current tool requires too much setup or feels slow during ordinary work.

Look elsewhere if executives need deep built-in reports, teams require many custom approval steps, or several departments must share one workspace. A broader platform will take more setup, but it may match the organization better.

Linear earns an 8.4 because its core experience appears unusually strong. The lower support score reflects the limited but serious account and service complaints. The product score stops short of the top because reporting and customization can become real constraints.

Pilot it with real work before migrating. Confirm the reports, required states, integrations, export path, and account recovery process. If those checks pass, Linear is one of the easiest issue trackers to recommend for a focused software team.

## Verdict

Linear makes routine issue tracking remarkably easy to maintain. That is a meaningful advantage for a small product and engineering team. Its narrow reports, firm workflow choices, and uncertain service feedback make it a harder choice for a complex organization. Use it when focus is the goal, and verify its limits before moving the team's record.
