Encharge is for a software company that wants email to react to what a customer does. A new trial user might sign up but never create a project. Encharge can wait two days, check whether that step is still missing, and send the right help.

That is different from a normal newsletter. A newsletter sends a message to a list. Encharge is most useful when each customer can receive a different message based on signup, product use, payment, or another action.

The visual builder makes those paths easier to understand. The harder part is getting clean customer actions into Encharge and paying for the plan that supports them.

## The flow builder shows what will happen

Encharge calls each automated path a flow. It appears as boxes joined on a page. A trigger starts the path. A filter checks something about the person. An action sends an email, adds a tag, or performs another task.

A basic trial flow could start when someone signs up. It could wait, split people based on whether they created a project, and send help only to the group that got stuck. A marketer can see that logic without reading code.

This is the part users praise most. G2 currently shows 4.7 from 374 reviews. Trustpilot has 267 reviews, and Capterra has 294. Across those sites, people often mention the visual builder, broad automation, and setup that feels easier than heavier tools.

There is still a learning curve once the flow has many branches. Reviews also mention limited templates, missing features, and gaps in some integrations. A map makes the logic visible. The person building it still has to decide which action matters and stop customers from receiving the wrong email twice.

## Drag and drop starts after the data is connected

The phrase behavior-based email means sending a message after a person does or does not do something.

Encharge cannot know that a user created a project unless the software sends that action to it. The company may need to add tracking code, connect an API, or use another integration. Someone also has to name the events clearly and make sure they fire at the right time.

That work can require a developer. It also needs testing. If the event arrives twice, a customer could enter the same flow twice. If it never arrives, the useful email never gets sent.

One Reddit user complained that the main dashboard showed too many events and lacked the filter they wanted. Another found the email and delivery setup hard. Someone later said those parts may have changed. We would treat these as test points, not current facts about every account.

## Premium holds many of the software-company features

Growth starts at $99 a month for 2,000 subscribers. Premium starts at $159. Paying yearly lowers the starting monthly amounts to $79 and $129. Both plans include unlimited flows, unlimited team members, and a 14-day trial.

The plan difference matters more than the headline price. Product events, event-based groups, order emails, and links with HubSpot, Salesforce, Segment, Shopify, and WooCommerce require Premium.

For a software company, product events may be the whole reason to consider Encharge. A buyer should not plan a $99 setup and learn later that the needed action data pushes the bill to $159 or more.

Pricing also rises with the number of subscribers. Encharge counts people with an email address or user ID, plus unnamed people who have entered a flow. Unsubscribed and archived people are not counted. Sending above the monthly email allowance costs $100 for each extra 100,000 messages.

## Treat the high review scores with care

Encharge has far more public reviews than many tools in this category. The same useful features appear across several sites. That gives the main praise some weight.

The collection was not fully neutral. The founder disclosed offering feature extensions for some honest G2 reviews. A Trustpilot complaint made a similar claim about extra subscriber room. An offer does not prove a review is false. It can encourage happy or price-sensitive customers to post when other users stay silent.

We would use the review text to find things to test. We would not use the 4.7 score alone as proof that setup, support, or delivery will work for a specific business.

## Decide with three real messages

Before starting the trial, write down three messages the business needs to send. Name the exact action that should start each one. Then ask who will connect that action to Encharge and which plan supports it.

Build one complete flow during the trial. Use test accounts that take different paths. Confirm that the right email arrives, the wrong email does not, and a repeated action does not create a copy.

We recommend Encharge to software companies with clear onboarding or retention messages tied to product use. The canvas can make a complicated customer path much easier to inspect and change.

We do not recommend it for a small business that mainly sends the same newsletter to everyone. The starting price and setup only make sense when customer behavior changes what the business needs to say.

Encharge is good at showing the plan. The business still has to supply the right customer data for that plan to work.
