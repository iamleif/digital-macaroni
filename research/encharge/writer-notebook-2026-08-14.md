# Encharge writer's notebook

## Research scope

- Research-based review; no product test
- Pricing, flow, and event documents checked on 2026-08-14
- 935 public review voices across G2, Trustpilot, and Capterra, plus community discussion
- Scores are discounted because the founder disclosed giving feature extensions for some honest G2 reviews

## Reader and job

- Primary reader: a software company that wants email to react to what a customer does inside its product
- Plain example: send help when a new user signs up but does not finish setup
- Wrong reader: a small newsletter that mainly sends the same message to a list
- Cost of failure: paying for Premium, developer setup, and a large contact list before the useful events work

## Product facts

- A flow is a set of boxes joined on a canvas
- A trigger starts the flow, a filter chooses who continues, and an action sends an email or changes data
- Growth starts at $99 monthly for 2,000 subscribers; Premium starts at $159
- Annual starting prices are $79 and $129 per month
- Both plans include unlimited flows and team members
- Product events, event-based groups, transactional email, and several major integrations require Premium
- Email sending above the plan allowance costs $100 per 100,000 messages

## Experience records

### Trial user received the right help

- Source: product capability supported by reviews
- Example: a user signs up, creates no project after two days, and gets a setup email
- Consequence: this is more useful than sending every trial user the same fixed sequence

### Visual path made complex email easier to see

- Source: G2, Trustpilot, and Capterra
- Result: users repeatedly praise the canvas and ability to see the full path
- Consequence: marketers can inspect the logic without reading code

### Setup still needed technical work

- Source: official event guide and Reddit
- Result: product events require an API, tracking code, or integration; one user found deliverability setup hard
- Consequence: drag and drop begins after the right data reaches Encharge

## Repeated patterns

- The visual flow builder is the strongest benefit
- Customer behavior creates the most useful email timing
- Templates, some integrations, and advanced reporting are thinner areas
- Complex flows still take time to learn and test

## Feature translation

### Behavior-based email

- What it does: sends a message after a customer performs or skips an action
- Who cares: software businesses with trials, onboarding steps, and paid upgrades
- Main limit: the business must send clean action data into Encharge

### Event-based segment

- What it does: makes a group such as people who signed up but never created a project
- Main limit: requires Premium

### Deliverability setup

- What it does: proves the company owns the sending address and helps mail avoid spam folders
- Main limit: adds setup work before a flow can be judged

## Conflict ledger

- Large positive review totals suggest real satisfaction, but feature incentives can change who chooses to review.
- The canvas is easier than code, but data tracking still may require a developer.
