# Wispr Flow spoken review brief

## What is it?

Wispr Flow lets you hold a key and speak into almost any place where you would normally type. It does more than copy your words. It removes filler, handles a correction such as “Friday, actually Monday,” and adds the punctuation.

## Who will find it useful?

Someone who writes all day and already thinks well out loud. It is especially useful on a Mac because that version has the best user record. It can also help someone who finds long typing sessions slow or painful.

## When will it not matter?

If you cannot talk at your desk, it will not replace much typing. The same is true if you work in a noisy room or already type quickly and rarely need to edit.

## What changed our opinion?

The cleanup is the real product. Basic dictation gives you exactly what you said, including the mistakes. Wispr Flow tries to give you what you meant to write.

The device matters more than the marketing suggests. Mac users give the most convincing accounts. One detailed App Store account said the iPhone kept forcing the user to switch screens and the iPad did not work properly with an external keyboard.

## What should someone know about privacy?

The words are processed in the cloud on every plan. Privacy Mode stops the data from being used for model training. A separate setting called Private Cloud Sync controls whether the transcript is stored on Wispr's servers.

Context Awareness can read the app and text near the cursor. Screen capture is a separate option and is off by default. For private client work, turn Context Awareness off unless reading that material is acceptable.

## What does it cost?

The free plan allows 2,000 words each week on desktop and 1,000 on iPhone. Android dictation is unlimited. Pro costs $12 per user each month and removes the limit.

## Our answer

Mac users who write a lot should try it. Use the free plan in the actual apps and room where you work. Pay if the text needs little repair and the cloud settings are acceptable.

Do not buy it based on the Mac praise if you mainly work from an iPhone, iPad, or Windows computer. Test that device first.
