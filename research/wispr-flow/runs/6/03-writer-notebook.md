# Wispr Flow writer's notebook

## Research scope

- Research-based review; no product test.
- Nine excerpt-backed sources, including official product, pricing, privacy, independent review, community, and App Store material.
- Product Hunt displayed 73 sampled voices in the stored packet; G2 had 13; the earlier App Store sample had three detailed reviews; the refreshed Reddit thread contains many replies but also heavy competitor promotion.

## Reader and job

- Primary reader: a person who writes for hours and wants to speak into the apps already in use.
- Strongest buyer: a Mac user with a quiet or private place to talk.
- Existing tools: a keyboard, built-in dictation, or a separate transcription app that requires copying and pasting.
- Failure cost: more correction time than typing, device friction that breaks the habit, or sensitive text leaving the device under settings the user did not understand.

## Product facts

- A shortcut starts dictation in the current text field.
- The service removes filler words and false starts, applies spoken corrections, and changes formatting based on the app and nearby text.
- Dictation works on Mac, Windows, iPhone, and Android. The current Notetaker is Mac-only.
- Free: 2,000 words each week on desktop; 1,000 on iPhone; unlimited dictation on Android.
- Pro: $15 per user monthly or $12 per user each month with annual billing; unlimited dictation.
- Speech is processed in the cloud at every privacy setting.
- Privacy Mode prevents retention and model training. Private Cloud Sync and local desktop storage are separate controls.
- Context Awareness is on by default on Mac and Windows. It may include nearby text, app details, screenshots, and conversation content in a request.

## Repeated patterns

- Cleanup is the useful difference from ordinary dictation. People value false-start removal and automatic formatting more than raw speech-to-text.
- Mac has the strongest detailed record. Windows and mobile have more specific caveats.
- Accuracy varies with noise, microphones, names, and languages. Broadly positive ratings do not erase the detailed failures.
- Privacy complaints often mix four separate questions: cloud processing, training, server retention, and screen context. The article must separate them.
- The free desktop limit is enough for a trial and too small for all-day use.

## Feature translation

### System-wide dictation

- Plain explanation: hold a key and talk into the text box already open in email, chat, a document, or an AI tool.
- Useful for: a person whose thoughts arrive faster than their typing.
- Does not matter when: the person cannot speak at work or already types comfortably.

### Cleanup

- Plain explanation: say a sentence, correct yourself halfway through, and receive the corrected sentence instead of both attempts.
- Useful for: anyone who wants sendable text rather than a transcript that needs another edit.
- Limit: names, technical terms, noise, and multilingual speech can still require repair.

### Context Awareness

- Plain explanation: the app reads where the cursor is and some of what is on the screen so it can format an email differently from a chat message.
- Useful for: people moving among apps who accept the data tradeoff.
- Limit: nearby client or company material may be included with the dictation request.

## Conflict ledger

- Wispr Flow can be dramatically faster for a slow typist and a poor purchase for someone who cannot speak at work.
- High overall ratings and detailed transcription failures can both be true because language, microphone, room, and device differ.
- Privacy Mode is meaningful even though speech still goes to the cloud. It changes retention and training, not where transcription happens.
- Context Awareness improves formatting and increases the amount of surrounding information the service may receive.
