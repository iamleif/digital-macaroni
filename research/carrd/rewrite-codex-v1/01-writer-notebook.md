# Carrd writer's notebook

Research-based review with no Digital Macaroni hands-on build. Eleven sources were checked on August 13 and 14, 2026. The buyer needs a simple site for a profile, event, waitlist, portfolio, or landing page. Carrd's strength and limit are the same: it is built for one responsive page.

Verified facts: sections can look like separate pages but remain on one page. Pro Lite started at $9 yearly for three sites, Standard at $19 for ten, and Plus at $49 for 25. Standard was the first plan with custom domains, forms, widgets, code embeds, analytics, and meta tags. Plus added advanced forms and site controls.

Repeated strengths are low cost, quick setup, clean templates, and an editor that beginners can learn. Carrd is a poor base for a true blog, many search pages, complex selling, a database, or app behavior. The independent professional sample is small, while the community sample includes experienced Carrd users.

Plain terms: a section hides and shows part of the same page; it does not create a normal page with its own content record. A custom domain is the buyer's own web address. An embed places an outside booking, payment, or signup tool inside the page.

Missing evidence: no custom-domain setup, form delivery, phone layout, support, billing, or search-indexing test was completed.
