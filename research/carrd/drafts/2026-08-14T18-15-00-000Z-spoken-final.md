# Carrd review: plain-language final

Carrd builds simple one-page websites. Pick a template, replace the words and images, add a button or form, and publish. The page adjusts for phones without much work.

It is a good tool for a profile, portfolio, event, waitlist, link page, or landing page. It is not a smaller version of a full website builder. The one-page limit is the reason Carrd stays easy, and the reason some sites should never start there.

We recommend Carrd when the whole site genuinely fits on one page. Its clean templates, small editor, and low annual price make that job unusually easy. We would use another platform when regular articles, many products, separate search pages, accounts, or app behavior are part of the plan.

## Carrd is good when the message is narrow

A useful one-page site has a simple job. It explains who someone is, what they offer, why a visitor should care, and what to do next.

A freelance designer might show a short introduction, three examples, a few client quotes, answers to common questions, and a contact form. An event page might show the date, place, schedule, map, and ticket button. Carrd can handle both without making the owner set up a large site.

Templates give the page a working layout at the start. Text, images, buttons, columns, videos, audio, icons, and forms can be added with the visual editor. Carrd also provides a phone preview and lets elements use different settings on smaller screens.

That does not mean every page will look good without judgment. A beginner can still choose tiny text, weak colors, or too many moving effects. The smaller set of tools makes those mistakes easier to find and fix than in a builder with hundreds of options.

G2's 33-review sample is small, but it agrees with several active community discussions. People return to the same strengths: low cost, quick setup, clean results, and less to learn than a general website platform.

## Sections do not make it a normal multi-page site

Carrd can divide one site into sections. When a visitor clicks Work, About, or Contact, the page can hide one section and show another. Each section can also have a link ending in a name such as #work.

This can make a small portfolio feel like it has several pages. It is still one underlying page. Carrd describes sections as a way to simulate the effect of multiple pages.

That difference matters when the site needs to grow. A blog normally gives every article its own address, publish date, category, author, related posts, and place in an archive. A business site may need separate service pages that can each appear in search results. Carrd does not have a built-in content system for that work.

Do not solve this by creating 30 sections. The editor may still publish them, but updating, linking, and organizing the content becomes awkward. Search engines and visitors also have less clear structure to work with.

Write the planned site outline first. If the headings all support one subject and one next step, Carrd fits. If the outline includes a blog, several detailed services, many locations, or a large help area, use a normal multi-page builder.

## Forms and embeds cover simple business needs

Pro Standard adds contact and signup forms, custom domains, outside widgets, custom code, analytics, and search-page details such as the title and description.

That is enough for many small business pages. A visitor can send a message, join an email list, book through an outside calendar, pay through an embedded service, or open a product sold elsewhere.

Pro Plus adds custom forms that can send entries to email, Airtable, Zapier, Make, n8n, or another web address. It also adds password protection, redirects, site downloads, variables, and deeper settings.

These options make Carrd flexible, but they do not turn it into an app builder. A complex store, member area, customer dashboard, database, or custom booking system depends on outside services or code. At that point, the simplicity starts to disappear.

Test the complete visitor path. Open the page on a phone, submit the form, check the reply or connected system, use every payment or booking link, and read the result page. A nice template is only half the job if the contact message never arrives.

## Standard is the plan most businesses need

Free allows up to three basic sites on Carrd addresses. It is useful for learning the editor and publishing a personal page, but it keeps Carrd branding and tighter limits.

Pro Lite is $9 a year for three sites. It removes branding and adds better images, premium templates, site sharing, and other design features. It does not include custom domains, so most businesses should skip it.

Pro Standard is $19 a year for ten sites. It adds custom domains, forms, widgets, code embeds, analytics, meta tags, and local fonts. This is the sensible starting point for a portfolio, company landing page, or lead form.

Pro Plus is $49 a year for 25 sites. Pay for it when advanced forms, redirects, password access, downloads, or detailed site settings are actually needed. Larger site bundles are also available.

The domain itself is bought separately. Carrd connects it and provides secure access, but the annual domain fee goes to the domain company.

Carrd is unusually cheap even on the useful plans. That makes it good for an agency or freelancer running several narrow pages. It should not change the decision about structure. Rebuilding a growing blog later costs more than starting it on the right platform.

## Who should use Carrd

We recommend Carrd for a person or small business that needs one clear page online. It is also good for temporary campaign pages, product waitlists, event details, simple portfolios, and small experiments.

We would not use it for a site built around regular publishing, many search pages, a product catalog, user accounts, or information pulled from a database. Those are not edge cases to patch with more sections and embeds. They are different kinds of websites.

Before choosing, write the full outline and the next 12 months of expected changes. If the site can still be one focused page, Carrd is hard to beat for the price. If the plan already sounds like a collection of pages, choose a builder made for that from the start.
