# Twenty CRM review

## Short answer

Twenty CRM launched on July 19, 2023. It offers a clean interface, flexible data model, custom objects, APIs, and open-source code. The project has strong open-source momentum with active releases and a large developer community. Self-hosting and upgrades can be tricky, with reports ranging from smooth small deployments to migration failures requiring deep debugging. Compared to mature CRMs, Twenty has gaps in marketing automation, mobile, reporting, integrations, permissions, and workflow depth. Managed-cloud usability and self-hosted operability must be evaluated separately. GitHub popularity shows adoption but not satisfaction or production reliability. Digital Macaroni did not perform a new installation, so current feature and security claims require verification.

## What it is good at

Twenty CRM excels in providing a clean and intuitive user interface. Users often praise its flexible data model and custom objects, which allow for tailored setups. The open-source nature of the software attracts developers who value transparency and customization. APIs are well-documented, making integration with other tools straightforward. The project has strong momentum in the open-source community, with active releases and a large developer base contributing to its growth. Teams that prioritize UX and data-model flexibility find Twenty a compelling choice. The managed cloud option offers a polished interface, which is appreciated by users who prefer not to deal with hosting complexities.

## Where it gets in the way

Self-hosting Twenty CRM can be challenging. Reports indicate that upgrades and migrations often require deep debugging, especially for larger datasets. While small deployments tend to go smoothly, larger setups can encounter significant operational hurdles. The software lacks some features found in mature CRMs, such as robust marketing automation, mobile support, and advanced reporting. Integrations and permissions are also areas where Twenty falls short. The polished interface of the managed cloud option does not guarantee painless upgrades for self-hosted instances. Users have noted slow support responses, which can be frustrating when issues arise. These limitations make Twenty less suitable for teams needing a fully-featured CRM out of the box.

## What using it is likely to feel like

Using Twenty CRM feels like working with a modern, lightweight tool that prioritizes user experience. The clean interface makes navigation straightforward, and the flexible data model allows for customization. However, self-hosting introduces complexity, especially during upgrades and migrations. Users may find themselves spending time debugging issues rather than focusing on their core tasks. The managed cloud option offers a smoother experience but comes with its own set of limitations. Teams that value transparency and customization will appreciate the open-source nature, but those needing comprehensive features might feel constrained. Overall, Twenty provides a solid foundation but requires patience and technical expertise to fully leverage its potential.

## Price and value

Twenty CRM offers both managed cloud and self-hosted options. The managed cloud plan provides a polished interface and ease of use, but it comes at a cost. Self-hosting eliminates subscription fees but introduces operational overhead. The value of Twenty depends on whether its core workflow can replace enough manual work to justify the paid plan. Teams with technical expertise can save money by self-hosting, but they must be prepared for potential upgrade and migration challenges. For small businesses or startups, Twenty can be a cost-effective solution, especially if they prioritize customization over comprehensive features. Larger organizations might find the limitations outweigh the benefits.

## Who it is for

Twenty CRM is ideal for teams that value flexibility and customization. Developers and tech-savvy users will appreciate the open-source code and well-documented APIs. Small businesses and startups can benefit from its cost-effective managed cloud option. Teams prioritizing user experience and data-model flexibility will find Twenty a good fit. However, organizations needing robust marketing automation, mobile support, and advanced reporting might find Twenty lacking. It’s also suitable for those who prefer transparency and community-driven development over proprietary solutions. Larger enterprises with complex needs might want to consider more mature CRM options.

## Verdict

Twenty CRM is a solid choice for teams valuing flexibility and customization. Its clean interface and open-source nature are strong points. However, self-hosting introduces operational challenges, and the software lacks some features found in mature CRMs. Managed cloud users enjoy a polished experience but must deal with limitations. Teams needing comprehensive features should look elsewhere. Twenty is best suited for small businesses, startups, and tech-savvy users who prioritize transparency and customization over out-of-the-box completeness. Larger organizations with complex needs might find Twenty insufficient for their requirements.
