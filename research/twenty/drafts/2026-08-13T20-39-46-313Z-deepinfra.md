Choose it only if your team values flexibility over reliability and can handle technical debt. The core conflict is simple: developer enthusiasm clashes with operational reality. This decides whether Twenty saves money or creates hidden costs.

## What it is good at

The open-source codebase allows modifications impossible in closed systems like Salesforce. This strength appears across Product Hunt reviews and community discussions where teams report successfully molding Twenty to their sales processes.

These limitations surface across Product Hunt critiques and benchmark comparisons. Workflow depth also lags behind paid alternatives according to multiple sources.

## Who it is for

Twenty fits technical teams prioritizing control. Developers modifying CRMs will appreciate the open codebase and API access. Small sales teams needing basic pipeline tracking without marketing tools may find it sufficient. 

Avoid Twenty if you need mobile access, complex automations, or guaranteed uptime. Non-technical teams should skip self-hosting entirely. Enterprises requiring deep permissions or reporting should consider mature alternatives.
