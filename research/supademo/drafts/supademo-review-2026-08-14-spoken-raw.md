# Supademo review: will the demo still work next month?

Supademo records a software workflow and turns it into a guided demo. Instead of watching a long video, the viewer clicks through a series of screens and instructions.

That is useful when a salesperson, support agent, or trainer explains the same task every week. One clear walkthrough can live on a website, in a help article, inside an onboarding email, and in a sales follow-up.

The important question is not how fast the first demo appears. It is whether a new person can finish the task, whether the recording survives a difficult screen, and whether the team can update it after the product changes.

We would test Supademo with one real workflow that includes a scroll, menu, modal window, text entry, loading state, and two quick clicks. Publish it in three real places. Then change a button and time the repair.

## Show one useful job

An interactive demo works best when it answers one question. How do I invite a colleague? How do I build the first report? How does this feature solve the problem I came with?

It works poorly when the creator tries to squeeze the whole product into one tour.

Choose a task that a new user can finish in three to seven minutes. Write the result first. Remove every click that does not help reach it. Use plain instructions that say what to do and why it matters.

Give the demo to five people who did not help make it. Do not explain it over a call. Watch where they hesitate, click the wrong place, go backward, or quit.

If all five need help at the same step, the demo has found a problem. Fix the instruction or the product. Do not add more animation around a confusing screen.

## Difficult screens are the real recording test

Supademo can capture screenshots and video as the creator moves through a workflow. Public feedback repeatedly praises the speed of this process.

It also points to trouble with dynamic interfaces, scrolling, modal windows, and clicks that happen close together.

Use the actual browser, monitor size, and product account used for production. Record a long page, a menu near the edge, a loading spinner, a popup, a drag action, and a screen that changes after a save.

Check every frame. The right button should be visible. Private names and customer data should be removed. A loading state should not look like the final result. The click target should be large enough on a phone.

Now correct a missed click and replace one bad screen. If that requires recording the whole path again, the advertised speed will disappear as demos grow.

## Branching needs a reason

Scale includes branching and variables. Branching lets a viewer choose a route, such as administrator or normal user, instead of following one fixed path.

This can keep a demo relevant. It can also turn a short guide into a tree that nobody checks.

Draw the routes on paper. Every branch needs a different viewer need, a clear label, and an ending. Run every route after an edit. Check that the back button does not leave a person in the wrong version.

Use separate demos when the audiences need different stories. A buyer deciding whether the product fits does not need the same detail as an employee learning a daily task.

## Reuse creates a maintenance job

The same Supademo can be shared as a link, embedded on a page, placed in a collection, or exported in other formats. This is one of the strongest reasons to use it.

Reuse also means one stale screen can appear in several places.

Give each demo an owner, purpose, audience, product area, last review date, and next review date. Keep a list of every place it is embedded. When the product changes, the owner should know which demos need attention.

Change a button label and move a menu in the test product. Time how long it takes to find every affected demo, replace the screen, check each route, and republish it.

Supademo is saving work only if this is faster than replacing the old help article or recording a new short video.

## Analytics should lead to a change

Scale adds tracking links and analytics. These can show whether somebody opened a demo and how far that person moved through it.

Create separate links for a website, sales email, and onboarding message. Open them from a normal browser, a private window, and a phone. Confirm that the source and completion numbers remain separate.

Do not treat a completed demo as a sale or a trained user. It shows that the person reached the end.

Decide what each number will change. A common exit at step three may justify a clearer instruction. Low starts from an email may mean the message is wrong. If the team will not make a decision from the number, the dashboard is decoration.

Review consent, cookies, personal data, and retention before requiring an email or connecting viewer activity to a sales record.

## Voice and video need a correction route

Scale adds recorded or uploaded voiceover, AI voices, captions, and translation. Growth adds voice cloning.

Test the real product names, personal names, abbreviations, numbers, and languages. Captions should match the spoken words. A translation should preserve warnings and button labels instead of inventing local wording that does not exist in the interface.

Make one change in the middle. Check whether the creator can replace a sentence without rerecording the rest. Listen on a phone and laptop. Keep a text version for viewers who cannot or do not want to use audio.

Voice cloning needs explicit permission from the person whose voice is copied. The team also needs a way to stop using it when that person leaves.

## Count the people who need to edit

Free allows one creator and five guided demos. It includes unlimited external views, 50 4K video recordings, basic editing, links, embeds, and exports. Free MP4 export is limited to 1080p with a watermark.

Scale costs $38 per month for each creator or administrator. It adds unlimited guided demos, collections, branching, variables, analytics, voiceover, translation, protection options, and five view-only collaborators.

Growth costs $350 per month for five creators. Extra creators cost $50 monthly. It adds guided HTML demos, sandbox demos, versioning, advanced AI editing, voice cloning, and unlimited view-only collaborators.

Enterprise has custom pricing and begins with ten creators. Paid plans have a 14-day trial without a credit card.

A creator can record and edit. An administrator is also a paid seat. A viewer can view, analyze, and share existing work but cannot create or edit it.

List everyone who will make urgent corrections. A cheap setup with one creator is not useful if that person is away and the main onboarding demo is wrong.

## Check what remains after a downgrade

Supademo says existing demos remain accessible after a downgrade to Free, but premium features are removed. Branches, variables, advanced analytics, collections, zoom animations, custom domains, and branding can be affected.

During the trial, create a demo that uses paid features. Export the text and images. Record its settings. Ask what viewers will see after downgrade and what happens to embedded versions.

Test password protection, expiring links, allowed domains, and removal of a team member. Make sure a private internal demo cannot be found through a public collection or old link.

## Who should choose Supademo?

Supademo makes sense for a team that repeatedly explains short software tasks and will assign somebody to maintain the demos. Fast creation and the ability to reuse one asset across several jobs are supported by a large review sample.

It is unnecessary when a screenshot and four sentences answer the question. It is a poor match when workflows change every week, advanced video editing is normal, or many people need occasional editing access.

We did not record a workflow, test a viewer, embed a demo, verify analytics, change a plan, or contact support. The visible feedback is heavily positive, so uncommon reliability problems may be easy to miss.

Our rule is to build one difficult real demo. Give it to five new people. Publish it in three places. Check every captured screen, branch, phone view, caption, and tracking link.

Then change the product and time the full update. Keep Supademo if viewers complete the task and the owner can repair every copy quickly. If the demo becomes another stale help page, fast creation has only helped the team publish the problem sooner.
