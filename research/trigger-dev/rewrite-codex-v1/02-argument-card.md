# Argument card

- Thesis: Trigger.dev earns its place when it makes recovery easier to prove than the old queue.
- Strength: code-first TypeScript tasks, long waits, retries, logs, history, and managed workers.
- Risk: retries can repeat charges, shipments, messages, or other outside actions.
- Buyer: TypeScript teams with meaningful long-running background jobs.
- Skip: tiny jobs, non-TypeScript ownership, or cheaper safe queue workloads.
- Price anchor: Free included $5 credit, Hobby was $10 with $10 credit, and Pro was $50 with $50 credit.
- Buying test: break one reversible production job at every boundary.
- Verdict: Strong background jobs still require safe retries and replays.
