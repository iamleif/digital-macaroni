## Claims

- Pipedream publicly launched its beta on October 16, 2019. [verified; fact; launch, product-hunt]
- Pipedream runs event-driven workflows that combine prebuilt API actions with editable Node.js, Python, Go, or Bash code. [verified; fact; pricing-docs, product-hunt, g2]
- Workflow credits are charged by execution segments, compute time, and memory. At 256 MB, one segment uses one credit for each started 30 seconds. [verified; fact; pricing-docs]
- Development and test runs do not consume workflow credits, while paid plans include a credit allowance and bill additional use. [verified; fact; pricing-docs]
- Moving from an API idea to a running workflow quickly, without managing servers, is the strongest positive theme. [supported; reported-pattern; product-hunt, g2, capterra]
- Prebuilt components that can be inspected or replaced with code, managed authentication, webhooks, scheduling, and broad integrations are repeated strengths. [supported; reported-pattern; product-hunt, g2, hacker-news]
- The product is harder for nontechnical owners, and scripts can make business workflows difficult to review and maintain. [supported; reported-pattern; g2, reddit]
- Credit cost can be difficult to forecast for long, memory-heavy, branching, or frequently resumed workflows. [supported; reported-pattern; pricing-docs, g2, hacker-news]
