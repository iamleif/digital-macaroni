# folk private argument card

- Buyer: a small relationship-driven team moving beyond spreadsheets or a bulky CRM
- Job: keep contacts, conversations, stages, and next actions together
- Thesis: folk is useful when a small team needs a shared memory and one next action for each relationship; it is less convincing as the reporting and control center for a large sales operation
- Main reason to buy: quick capture, enrichment, email and calendar context, and a low-friction interface
- Main reason to hesitate: per-seat cost, Premium gates, duplicate and privacy risks, and limited proof for complex analytics and sales operations
- When it is unnecessary: one person can manage the list in an existing tool, or the company needs deep forecasting, quoting, territories, approvals, and sales administration
- Decision rule: import a dirty sample of 100 contacts, test merge and enrichment, sync two accounts with sensitive threads, run one small campaign, use mobile after a meeting, and reproduce the manager's weekly report
- Evidence limit: research-based; no hands-on setup or sending test
