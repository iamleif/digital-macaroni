Quo is a cloud-based business phone system that allows small teams to manage calls, texts, and shared lines without physical hardware. It is designed for remote and distributed teams, offering a unified workspace for communication and collaboration. The main feature that stands out is its support for shared phone lines and the ability to handle incoming calls and messages from a single number. However, the tradeoff is that Quo may not be ideal for businesses that rely heavily on real calls, texts, or number porting. Some users reported issues with call quality, message delays, and support during critical times, which can affect the overall experience. Before committing to Quo, it's important to check whether the trial includes real calls and texts, whether number porting is possible, and whether there's a plan for handling potential outages.

Shared Lines and Unified Communication

Quo's ability to support shared phone lines is one of its key strengths. Teams can use a single number to manage incoming calls and messages, which simplifies communication for small businesses. This feature is particularly useful for remote teams, as it allows everyone to access calls and texts from a centralized location. The unified workspace also supports internal collaboration, making it easier for team members to stay connected. However, the effectiveness of this feature depends on the reliability of the service and the quality of the connection. Some users have reported issues with call quality and message delays, which can disrupt communication and reduce the overall user experience.

Ease of Use and Onboarding

## Setting up Quo is generally considered to be straightforward

The onboarding process involves choosing a number, installing the apps, and sharing a line, which are all approachable steps for most users. The interface is clean and easy to navigate, which makes it simple for teams to manage their communication. However, number porting and messaging registration can be more complicated, as they depend on carrier policies and local regulations. These steps require additional effort and may not be completed during the trial period, which could affect the overall experience. Despite these challenges, the ease of use and intuitive design make Quo a viable option for small teams looking for a modern phone system.

Support and Reliability

## Support is a mixed area for Quo

While many customers praise the helpfulness of the support team, there are reports of slow response times and unresolved issues during critical incidents. Some users have described problems with number porting, service outages, and billing issues, which can be frustrating when dealing with live numbers or accounts. The reliability of the service is also influenced by external factors such as local networks, devices, and carriers. While Quo offers a strong foundation for communication, the support experience can vary, and it's important to consider this when evaluating the service for your team.

Pricing and Plans

## Quo offers different pricing plans to suit various business needs

The Starter plan costs $19 per user monthly or $15 annually, the Business plan is $33 monthly or $23 annually, and the Scale plan is $47 monthly or $35 annually. Each plan includes one number per user, with additional numbers costing $5 monthly. Taxes and usage fees may apply, so it's essential to review the full quote before committing to a plan. The pricing structure is clear, but the additional costs for extra numbers and taxes can add up, making it important to carefully consider the total cost of ownership. For small teams, the per-user pricing model is a significant advantage, but it's crucial to factor in all potential expenses before making a decision.

Conclusion

Quo is a strong modern phone system for small teams that prioritize a cloud-based solution and don't require advanced call routing or physical phone equipment. The shared lines and unified communication features make it an attractive option for remote and distributed teams. However, the tradeoff is that the service may not be ideal for businesses that rely heavily on real calls, texts, or number porting. Issues with call quality, message delays, and support during critical times can affect the overall experience. Before committing to Quo, it's important to check whether the trial includes real calls and texts, whether number porting is possible, and whether there's a plan for handling potential outages. While Quo offers a clean interface and easy onboarding, the reliability and support experience can vary, and it's important to consider these factors when evaluating the service for your team.
