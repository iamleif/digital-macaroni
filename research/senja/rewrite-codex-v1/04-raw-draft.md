Senja gives customers one link where they can write or record a testimonial. The responses stay in a library, and selected quotes or videos can appear on a website without building the section from scratch.

That solves a narrow problem well. Praise often gets lost in email, social posts, calls, and support tools. Senja can collect it, though it cannot turn a vague answer into useful proof.

I recommend Senja for a small business that already has happy customers and needs a steady collection process. Start on Free with five people. Do not pay for a polished grid of generic praise.

## The form removes work from both sides

A customer can open the form and choose text or video. They do not need a scheduled interview or a shared-drive upload. The business receives the answer in the same library as imported proof.

Tags and search help find a quote for a page. Website widgets show selected items in a ready-made layout, while a public page can hold a larger collection.

The simple collection route is the strongest public theme. Test it with a customer who is willing to help and has little patience for new software. The form must work without a private tour.

## Better questions create better proof

Asking whether somebody liked the service usually produces a soft answer. Ask what took time before the purchase, what changed, and which result the customer can describe.

A short video with one real result is more useful than a long speech with no detail. Text works just as well when the customer prefers it.

Record permission to publish the name, company, image, quote, and video. Let the customer approve the final version. Senja handles collection and display after the business earns a useful answer.

## Free has a firm 15-item boundary

When our evidence was collected, Free included 15 total text and video testimonials, one form, one project, one seat, Senja branding, and unlimited widgets.

The limit did not reset monthly. Forms kept accepting submissions after item 15, but later answers stayed hidden until an upgrade. A business should not learn that after sending a mass request.

Starter cost $29 monthly and added unlimited testimonials, three forms, two seats, custom branding, HD export, automatic imports, Zapier, API access, and webhooks. Pro cost $59 with unlimited forms, five projects, and five seats. Check current terms before buying.

## Imports and finding proof need a real library

Import praise from the places the business already uses. Keep the original source address, customer identity, date, permission, and full wording. Check video quality and text formatting after import.

Build a small library around several products or customer types. Ask a teammate to find proof for one pricing concern and one service page. The right quote should appear without opening every card.

Pricing, some imports, integrations, and finding the right testimonial appear as public limits. A focused 20-item test reveals whether the library remains useful after the first page.

## The website block needs an escape test

Publish a text quote and a video on a test page. Check the page on a phone, keyboard navigation, load time, layout shifts, video controls, and what happens when Senja is unavailable.

Remove an item from the library and confirm the website changes as expected. Replace it without breaking the page. Test the custom branding and domain only when the paid plan matters.

The testimonial section should support the page rather than slow or crowd it. Keep a plain-text fallback when the proof is central to the sale.

## Keep another copy of every original

A five-review Trustpilot sample contains complaints about login failures, missing testimonials, and weak support. That is too small to describe the normal experience. It is enough to make backup and export part of the trial.

Save the original text, video file, permission, source, and date somewhere the business controls. Export the Senja library and open it without Senja. Check what happens to tags, forms, and website data.

G2 and Product Hunt are much more positive, though some G2 reviews were invited and marked as rewarded. We did not test login, export, recovery, or support.

## Who should choose Senja?

Senja is a strong choice for a small business with real customer results scattered across other tools. Free is large enough to build a useful first page before paying.

It is a weak purchase for a company that has no clear customer outcome to ask about. Design cannot supply the missing substance.

Start with five customers and one focused question. Test text and video, publish the page, save permission, export the originals, and keep another copy. Then decide whether the 15-item boundary justifies Starter.

Keep Senja when it makes specific, honest proof easier to collect and use. If every answer says only that the product is great, the form has worked and the marketing has not.
