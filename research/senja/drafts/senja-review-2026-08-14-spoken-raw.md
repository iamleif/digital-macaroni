Senja gives a business one link where customers can type or record a testimonial. The responses stay in a library, and selected quotes or videos can be placed on a website without designing the whole section by hand.

That solves a real but narrow problem. Customer praise often gets buried in email, social replies, support tickets, and call notes. Senja can pull it into one place and make it easier to use.

It cannot make a weak testimonial convincing. A polished block full of people saying “great product” still tells the next buyer very little.

## The form removes work for the customer

A business can send a Senja form after a project or purchase. The customer opens the page and chooses text or video. They do not need to schedule an interview, upload a large file to a shared drive, or learn new software.

G2 users often praise this simple collection process. One reviewer liked being able to send a link that lets a client record a video. Others mention the clean interface and the ability to keep testimonials organized.

The business can also import praise it already has from social sites and review platforms. Tags and search help find a quote for a specific page. A website widget then displays selected items in a ready-made layout. Senja calls a larger public collection a Wall of Love.

These tools save handling time. They do not decide which proof belongs on a pricing page, home page, or page for a specific service.

## The question matters more than the template

“Did you like our service?” will usually produce a soft answer. A better form asks what was happening before the purchase, what changed, and what result the customer can describe.

For example, “What task took the most time before you used the product, and how long does it take now?” gives the customer something real to answer. That detail helps a future buyer decide whether the story applies to them.

Video does not fix a vague question either. A natural 30-second answer about a clear result can be more useful than a polished three-minute speech with no detail.

Senja makes collecting and displaying the answer easier. The business still has to ask a good question, get permission, and avoid presenting selected praise as if every customer had the same result.

## Free is enough for a real testimonial page

The Free plan holds 15 text and video testimonials in total. It includes one form, one project, one user, imports, tags, website widgets, and public testimonial pages. Senja branding stays on the output.

The limit does not reset each month. After the 15th item, the form continues to accept new submissions. Later testimonials remain hidden until the account upgrades. A business should know this before sending the form to a large customer list.

Starter is $29 a month. It removes the 15-item limit and Senja branding. It also adds three forms, two users, a custom domain, HD video export, automatic imports, Zapier, an API, and webhooks.

Pro is $59 a month. It raises the account to unlimited forms, five projects, and five users. Extra projects and seats cost more. Annual billing is listed at 17% less. There is no timed trial for paid features because Senja uses the Free plan as the test.

For a freelancer with six strong quotes, Free is probably enough. For an agency managing proof for several services or clients, the Pro structure makes more sense than forcing everything into one project.

## Keep another copy of the originals

Trustpilot has only five Senja reviews and a 3.1 score. Complaints mention login failures, testimonials that disappeared, and support that did not solve the problem. That sample is far too small to call data loss a normal result.

It is still a good reason to test export and keep the originals. Save the written permission, text, and video file somewhere the business controls. A marketing tool should organize the proof, not become the only place it exists.

G2 is much more positive at 4.6 from 20 reviews, though several reviews were collected through an incentive. Product Hunt feedback is also mostly positive. The gap means we would test the form, website block, login, and export rather than treating either score as the answer.

## Who should use Senja

We recommend Senja to a small business that already has happy customers but has no steady way to ask for and publish their stories. The Free plan is large enough to build a useful page before paying.

Start with five customers, not a mass email. Ask one focused question. Publish only the answers that explain a real problem and result. Export every original.

We do not recommend paying $29 just to make a nice grid of generic praise. The design is the easy part. The useful work is getting a specific, honest answer from the customer.

Senja handles that answer well once it exists. It cannot create the substance for you.
