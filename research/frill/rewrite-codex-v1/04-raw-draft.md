Frill gives a software company one public place for customer ideas. People can submit requests, vote, see a roadmap, and read an update when work ships.

We recommend Frill to a small team that wants a focused feedback board and will maintain it every week. The clean board is only the start. Someone must merge repeated ideas, protect private details, change roadmap labels carefully, and tell customers what happened.

Frill is unnecessary when the company already handles this loop in its support tool or receives too little feedback to need a board.

## The complete feedback lifecycle defines the value

Feature requests often arrive through email, chat, sales calls, and support tickets. The same need may appear under several names, while the link to each customer gets lost.

Frill collects those requests on an idea board. The team can review new ideas, merge copies, add topics, and change the public status. A roadmap shows selected work, while update posts explain what shipped.

Widgets can put the board, roadmap, or updates inside the company's own product. Customers do not need a separate bookmark.

The clean design, quick setup, roadmap, widgets, connections, and responsive support receive broad praise in a small favorable sample. Paid plans allow unlimited teammates to manage the board.

This focus is useful when product and support need the same view. It does not replace a full helpdesk. Frill can create one more place to maintain if the current support tool already covers requests and updates.

## Repeated ideas reveal whether the board works

Customers rarely use the same words for one need. Frill has tools for finding similar ideas and joining them.

Import a real group of messy requests during the trial. Ask normal teammates to decide which ones belong together. After a merge, check votes, comments, followers, customer identity, private notes, and the old link.

Try an incorrect merge too, then confirm whether the team can undo it. A clean title means little if the wrong customer history moves with it.

Vote totals also need judgment. Requests from different plans or customer sizes may have different weight. Frill can use custom priority factors, though the company still needs its own rule.

Use votes as one input. Add customer value, support cost, effort, and product direction when those facts matter. Explain the choice without promising that the top vote always wins.

## Roadmap labels can become promises

A public roadmap can reduce repeated questions. It can also make customers think an idea has a fixed delivery date.

Labels such as Planned or Coming soon need a shared meaning. Decide who can change each label and what approval is required. Avoid dates the team cannot control.

Test an item that moves backward, gets canceled, or splits into two releases. Check what followers receive and whether the old idea explains the change.

An update should finish the story. Publish a small test announcement, connect it to the request, and confirm that the people who voted or followed receive the right email. The useful result is a customer who understands what changed.

## Private feedback can force a higher plan

A board may be public, invite-only, or shown only to known customers. Frill supports guest and anonymous sign-in, along with developer tools and single sign-on options.

Test one person from several devices and email addresses. Make sure votes do not multiply by mistake and one account cannot see another customer's private idea. Import customers with repeated identities and inspect how their history joins.

Private boards, approved-user access, and private ideas were part of Frill's Privacy add-on. The pricing page placed Privacy in Growth.

Startup cost $25 per month for 50 active ideas and one survey. Business was $49, removed the active-idea limit, and allowed three surveys.

Growth was $149 with unlimited ideas, Privacy, unlimited surveys, and no Frill branding. Enterprise was $349 and added audit logs, IP limits, dedicated hosting, dedicated support, and SOC 2 coverage.

The $100 jump from Business to Growth may come from privacy instead of idea volume. Check that requirement before importing customer requests. A title copied from a private support ticket may contain names, contract details, or account history that should never appear on a public board.

## The limited evidence requires validation

The independent feedback sample is favorable but limited. G2 had 24 reviews and Product Hunt had ten. Detailed discussion outside formal review pages was sparse, and an old AppSumo page showed conflicting counts that were excluded.

The positive pattern supports a trial, not a broad claim about every connection or large import. Missing features and connections are the main minority complaint, with little detail.

Import real ideas and submit close copies. Test known, anonymous, and private users. Move an item through every public status, publish an update, and confirm each follower gets the correct message without seeing private data.

We recommend Frill when that full loop is easy and one person accepts weekly ownership. Skip it when nobody will moderate requests or explain roadmap changes. A clean board earns its price only when the team returns to customers with an answer.
