# Frill writer's notebook

## Research scope

- Research-based review; no hands-on portal
- Pricing checked 2026-08-14
- Clean voice sample: 10 Product Hunt and 24 G2
- AppSumo count excluded due internal contradiction

## Reader and job

- Primary reader: a small software team that needs one public place for feature ideas, voting, plans, and shipped updates
- Plain example: customer submits an idea, team merges a duplicate, adds internal context, changes the public status, ships the work, and notifies everyone who asked
- Cost of failure: votes split across duplicates, a private customer appears publicly, the roadmap becomes a promise, or the shipped update never reaches the requester

## Product facts

- Idea board, voting, roadmap, announcements, widget, surveys, API, webhooks, custom domain
- Startup $25 monthly: 50 active ideas, one survey
- Business $49: unlimited ideas, three surveys
- Growth $149: privacy, unlimited surveys, white labeling
- Enterprise $349: SOC 2, audit logs, IP allowlist, dedicated hosting and support
- Unlimited admins and tracked users on paid plans
- Fourteen-day trial without card

## Experience records

### A team launched a feedback portal quickly

- Ease, clean UI, and setup are repeated praise
- Consequence: Frill suits teams that want a focused tool rather than a broad support suite

### Customers submitted the same request in different words

- Frill supports similar-idea detection and merging
- Consequence: test whether votes, comments, subscribers, and history survive the merge

### A roadmap status looked like a promise

- Product risk, even when not a dominant complaint
- Consequence: define public status language and ownership before launch

## Feature translation

### Idea board

- A page where customers submit and vote on product requests

### Roadmap

- A public view of work being considered, planned, or built

### Announcement

- A post explaining a shipped update, often sent to interested customers

### Tracked user

- A known customer whose activity or feedback can be connected through product identity

## Conflict ledger

- Simple focus helps setup and limits the breadth of the product.
- Positive feedback is consistent but comes from a small favorable group.
- Public transparency helps customers and can create accidental commitments.

## Missing evidence

- No import, duplicate merge, widget, identity, privacy, survey, roadmap, announcement, email, API, export, or support test
- No migration from Canny or another tool
- No accessibility or large-board performance test

## Spoken language bank

- the point is to close the loop
- merge the same request without losing people
- a roadmap status must not become an accidental promise
- unlimited admins keep the team cost simple
- privacy and white labeling require a much higher plan
