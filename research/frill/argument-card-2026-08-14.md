# Frill private argument card

- Buyer: a small software team that wants a clean customer feedback portal
- Job: collect ideas, combine duplicates, decide what matters, show plans carefully, and tell requesters what shipped
- Thesis: Frill is useful when the team needs a focused feedback-to-update loop and will actually maintain it; the board alone is not the value
- Main reason to buy: simple public ideas, voting, roadmap, announcements, and unlimited admins
- Main reason to hesitate: small evidence base, missing integrations or depth for some teams, and a large price jump for privacy and white labeling
- When it is unnecessary: the team receives little feedback, already closes the loop in its support tool, or cannot commit to reviewing and responding
- Decision rule: import a real sample, submit and merge duplicates, test signed-in and anonymous identity, change a public status, publish an update, and confirm every requester receives the right message without seeing private data
- Evidence limit: research-based; no hands-on portal or migration
