## Research scope

- Research-based review; no hands-on workspace or server

## Reader and job

- Primary reader: a software or product team that wants a cleaner project system and may want control over hosting
- Plain example: take a bug from intake, plan it into a two-week cycle, connect it to a larger feature, document the decision, and report whether the release is on track
- Cost of failure: lost issue history, weak access control, a broken sync, a report rebuilt by hand, or a self-hosted upgrade that takes the project system offline

## Product facts

- Work items, projects, cycles, modules, estimates, dependencies, views, timeline, pages, wiki, intake, and customer requests
- Cloud Free up to 12 users
- Pro displayed annual rate: $6 per seat monthly; paid trial two weeks
- Business displayed annual rate: $13 per seat monthly
- Self-hosted community, commercial, and air-gapped choices
- GitHub, GitLab, Slack, Sentry, imports, API, and exports depending on plan

## Experience records

### A team left a heavy issue tracker

- Clean speed and lower setup are the strongest themes
- Consequence: measure ordinary work creation and finding, not only admin configuration

### A manager needed a report Plane could not produce

- Reporting depth is a mixed limit
- Consequence: build the real weekly and quarterly reports during the trial

### A self-hosted upgrade broke login

- Setup and upgrade complaints recur across the historical sample
- Consequence: clone production, upgrade, and restore before migration

## Feature translation

### Cycle

- A fixed period, often one or two weeks, in which the team plans to finish selected work

### Module

- A group of related work inside a project, such as a feature or release area

### Work item

- A task, bug, request, or other piece of work the team tracks

### Air-gapped

- Installed in a network that cannot reach the public internet

## Conflict ledger

- Plane aims for less initial weight while adding a broad set of advanced features.
- Data control from self-hosting comes with operating work and commercial feature boundaries.
- A large repository community does not guarantee a smooth upgrade.
- Low per-seat cloud pricing may not represent self-hosted commercial or enterprise cost.
