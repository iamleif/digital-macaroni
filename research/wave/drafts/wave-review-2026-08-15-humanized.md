## Product overview
Wave provides a suite of accounting, invoicing and payment tools without a subscription fee. The service is funded by advertising, allowing core bookkeeping functions to remain free. Account creation is straightforward: a user signs up, links a bank account, and can begin issuing invoices within minutes. The dashboard displays a clean layout that guides users through basic tasks such as categorising transactions, reconciling bank statements and generating profit-and-loss reports. Double-entry accounting is supported, which helps businesses that need formal records for tax compliance while remaining simple enough for sole proprietors who track cash flow only.

The platform also includes receipt scanning, allowing paper receipts to be photographed and attached to expense entries. This reduces manual data entry and keeps expense records organized. For freelancers and very small businesses, the ability to maintain a formal ledger without paying a monthly fee can simplify tax preparation and provide a clearer view of cash flow.

## Invoicing features
The invoicing module lets users create line items, attach supporting documents and email invoices directly to clients. Automatic reminder emails are sent when a payment becomes overdue, helping to keep accounts receivable current. Recurring invoices can be set up for subscription-based services or regular retainers, which saves time for businesses that bill on a repeat schedule.

The workflow requires only a few clicks: select a client, enter the amount, set a due date and send. The built-in receipt scanner can also capture paper receipts and link them to expense entries, further reducing manual effort. These capabilities are useful for users with limited accounting knowledge, while still offering enough flexibility for more complex billing scenarios such as multi-item invoices or tax-inclusive pricing.

## Payment processing risk
Wave includes a built-in payment gateway that accepts credit cards and bank transfers. In practice, the gateway can place holds on funds or disable accounts when transactions are flagged as high risk. Documented cases show that a payment of several thousand dollars was returned and a portion of the amount was held for sixty days, while another instance involved a fifteen-thousand-dollar freeze during a compliance review with no defined release timeline. The platform’s response in such situations is limited to a generic “high-risk” label, offering little actionable detail.

For businesses that rely on timely cash flow, especially those processing larger or more frequent payments, this uncertainty can create operational risk. The risk is most apparent when the volume or value of transactions exceeds the typical low-volume usage that many freelancers experience. In those cases, the potential for delayed access to funds may outweigh the benefit of a free service.

## Support experience and final recommendation
When a payment hold occurs, the resolution process typically begins with an AI-driven chat interface that requests documentation before escalating to a human representative. Escalation can take several days, during which the affected funds remain inaccessible. This delay adds operational risk for users who need immediate access to cash.

Despite the payment-hold risk, the free accounting tools for bookkeeping, invoicing and receipt scanning remain valuable for freelancers and micro-businesses that can tolerate occasional payment hiccups. The platform’s simplicity allows users to maintain basic financial records without a monthly fee, and the automated invoice reminders help reduce the likelihood of unpaid invoices.

We recommend using Wave as a primary bookkeeping and invoicing solution for freelancers and micro-businesses that process low-volume payments or handle cash transactions outside the platform. Conduct a small pilot by processing a modest payment, monitor the clearance time, and assess the support response. If the payment clears quickly and support provides a timely answer, Wave can serve as a cost-effective core financial system.

We would skip adopting Wave as the main payment processor for businesses that depend on high-volume or high-value transactions. In those cases, the risk of fund holds and delayed support outweighs the benefit of a free service, and a dedicated payment gateway with stronger guarantees should be chosen instead.
