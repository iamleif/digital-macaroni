Wave offers a free, ad‑supported suite that combines bookkeeping, invoicing, and a payment gateway. It is aimed at freelancers, contractors, and solo‑owner businesses that need to track income, record expenses, and send invoices without paying a subscription fee. The core accounting tools are free because the company earns revenue from advertising, a model that has been in place since the product’s early days. While the bookkeeping and invoicing components work well for many small operations, the payment side introduces a risk that can be problematic for businesses that rely on timely cash flow.

## Invoicing is reliable and easy to adopt
Creating a bill in Wave takes only a few clicks. Users can add line items, attach files, and send the invoice directly to a client’s email address. The system also generates automatic reminders, so a follow‑up email is sent if a payment is overdue. One long‑time user, Speaker Labs, has been invoicing clients with Wave for eight years and reports never having an unpaid invoice because the reminder feature functions as expected. The interface is straightforward enough that a new user can start sending invoices after a brief walkthrough, matching the product’s promise of a unified system for accounting and invoicing.

## Payment processing can freeze funds without warning
Where invoicing shines, the payment gateway shows a different picture. Several reviewers on Trustpilot describe situations in which payments were held or accounts were disabled without a clear explanation. In one case, a food‑consulting business saw a $4,500 payment returned and the account permanently disabled, leaving $2,000 stuck for 60 days. Another small business experienced a $15,000 freeze during a compliance review, with no timeline provided for release. The vendor responses were limited to a generic “high‑risk” label, offering no actionable detail. For a business that depends on receiving client payments promptly, this uncertainty is a serious drawback.

## Support response is often slow and automated
When a payment hold occurs, quick assistance becomes essential. Reviewers consistently describe Wave’s support as slow and heavily reliant on AI‑driven chatbots. The bots typically request additional documentation before escalating an issue, and the escalation process can take days. In the example of the frozen $15,000, the business reported that support kept asking for more proof, leaving the company without a reliable way to contact its customers during the investigation. This lack of responsive human assistance compounds the frustration caused by payment holds.

## Free core features suit solo operators but carry payment risk
The free core tools—accounting, invoicing, and receipt scanning—are attractive for freelancers who need to track expenses, generate basic financial reports, and send invoices without a monthly fee. Onboarding is smooth: a new user can sign up, link a bank account, and begin creating invoices within minutes. The simplicity of the interface means that even users with limited accounting knowledge can keep their books in order. Some small businesses also find Wave simpler than alternatives such as QuickBooks, appreciating the minimal learning curve.

However, the payment risk remains a factor to weigh. If a business model does not depend on receiving payments through Wave—perhaps because cash is collected in person, a separate processor is used, or transaction volume is very low—the risk of frozen funds becomes less relevant. In those scenarios, Wave can serve as a free bookkeeping hub while payments are handled elsewhere. Conversely, businesses that process large or frequent client payments should treat Wave as a secondary tool rather than the primary payment gateway.

**Recommendation**
Wave delivers a solid, free solution for basic bookkeeping and invoicing, especially for freelancers and very small businesses that can tolerate occasional payment hiccups. Before committing, run a small test: process a low‑value payment, observe how quickly the funds appear, and ask a simple question of support. If the payment clears promptly and support replies within a reasonable timeframe, Wave may be comfortable for day‑to‑day operations. If the test reveals delays or unhelpful support, keep the existing payment method and consider a more reliable accounting solution for critical cash flow.
