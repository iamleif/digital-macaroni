Wave is a free, ad‑supported accounting suite that bundles bookkeeping, invoicing, and a payment gateway. It is built for freelancers, contractors, and solo‑owner businesses that need to track income, expenses, and send invoices without paying a subscription. Our judgment is that Wave does the bookkeeping part well, but the payment side can freeze funds without warning, and the support experience is often frustrating. If you can live with occasional payment hiccups, the zero‑cost core features are attractive; if you need reliable cash flow, you should look elsewhere.

## Invoicing works reliably and is easy to adopt
Wave’s invoicing tool lets you create professional‑looking bills in a few clicks, attach line items, and send them directly to clients. Users report that the system sends reminders automatically and that the follow‑up process is straightforward. One long‑time user, Speaker Labs, has been invoicing clients with Wave for eight years and says they have never had an unpaid invoice because the reminder feature works as expected. The interface is simple enough that a new user can start sending invoices after a short walkthrough, which aligns with the product’s promise of a unified system for accounting and invoicing.

## Payment processing can freeze funds unexpectedly
Where Wave’s invoicing shines, its payment processing stumbles. Several Trustpilot reviewers describe situations where payments were held or accounts were disabled without a clear reason. One food‑consulting business saw a $4,500 payment returned and the account permanently disabled, leaving $2,000 stuck for 60 days. Another small business had $15,000 frozen during a compliance review with no timeline for release. The vendor’s response was limited to a generic “high‑risk” label, offering no actionable explanation. For a business that relies on timely receipt of client payments, this risk is a serious drawback.

## Support feels like a black hole
When payment issues arise, getting help quickly is essential. Reviewers consistently describe Wave’s support as slow, often limited to AI‑driven chatbots that ask for additional proof before escalating. The same user who experienced frozen funds reported that support took days to respond and kept requesting more documentation, leaving the business without a reliable way to contact customers during the investigation. This lack of responsive human assistance compounds the frustration of payment holds.

## Free core features make it attractive for solo operators
Wave’s core accounting, invoicing, and receipt‑scanning tools are free, funded by advertising. For freelancers who need to track expenses, generate basic financial reports, and send invoices without paying a monthly fee, the value proposition is strong. The onboarding experience is smooth: a new user can sign up, connect a bank account, and start creating invoices within minutes. The simplicity of the interface means that even users with limited accounting knowledge can keep their books in order.

## When the payment risk does not matter
If your business model does not depend on receiving payments through Wave—perhaps you invoice and collect cash, use a separate payment processor, or have a very low transaction volume—the risk of frozen funds becomes less relevant. In such cases, Wave can serve as a free bookkeeping hub while you handle payments elsewhere. Conversely, businesses that need to process large or frequent client payments should treat Wave as a secondary tool rather than the primary payment gateway.

## Bottom line and practical decision rule
Wave delivers a solid, free solution for basic bookkeeping and invoicing, especially for freelancers and very small businesses that can tolerate occasional payment hiccups. Before committing, run a small test: process a low‑value payment, check how quickly the funds appear, and contact support with a simple question. If the payment clears and support replies promptly, you may be comfortable using Wave for day‑to‑day operations. If the test reveals delays or unhelpful support, keep your existing payment method and consider a more reliable accounting platform for critical cash flow.
