# Dorik private argument card

- Buyer: a non-technical owner or small agency building simple marketing sites
- Job: publish and maintain a good-looking site without a developer
- Thesis: Dorik is a fast and fairly easy way to build a normal site, but it is a poor choice if the design or selling process already needs unusual behavior
- Main reason to buy: a simple editor, ready-made sections, hosting, blog tools, and useful prices for several sites
- Main reason to hesitate: the final design polish, mobile layout, integrations, and live reliability deserve more testing than the fast first draft suggests
- When it is unnecessary: an existing site works, a one-page profile is enough, or the business needs a full store or custom web app
- Decision rule: during the trial, build the hardest real page first, connect the domain, submit every form, check phone and desktop layouts, invite the real editor, and republish after a change
- Evidence limit: research-based; no hands-on build or publish test
