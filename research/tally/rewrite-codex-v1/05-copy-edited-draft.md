## Ordinary forms are quick to make

A client intake form may ask for contact details, service, budget, and project notes. Tally lets the business write that page in order and place each answer where it belongs.

The next question can depend on an earlier answer. A website client can see questions about pages and launch dates, while a logo client follows a shorter route. The final answer can send a qualified lead to booking or another person to a different message.

Results can move to common tools such as Google Sheets, Notion, or Airtable. This combination of simple editing and useful connections is the strongest public theme.

## Free is enough for real work

Tally Free allows unlimited forms and responses within its fair-use policy. It also includes payments, signatures, file uploads, calculations, branching, redirects, duplicate prevention, and webhooks.

The normal Tally address and branding remain. That is usually fine for an internal process or a small business proving the route.

Build the real form before considering an upgrade. Collect safe test responses, trigger every connection, download files, process a test payment, and export the answers.

## Fair use matters at unusual volume

Unlimited has a boundary. Tally cited 50,000 monthly submissions, 100GB of monthly uploads or 500GB total storage, and 50,000 monthly notices as examples of heavy use that can need a custom plan.

Most small teams will never approach those levels. A public campaign, large event, or file-heavy application should describe the expected load to Tally before launch.

Test the process when an upload fails, an email bounces, or the receiving tool is unavailable. Decide which system holds the official response and how a missed record is recovered.

## Paid plans solve brand and team problems

During European research, the live page showed Pro at €20 monthly and Business at €65. Tally separately listed a U.S. Pro price of $29 in its help material, so buyers need to check their local page.

Pro removed branding and added a custom domain, custom CSS, shared workspaces, company-domain email, larger uploads, analytics, unfinished responses, and 30-day version history. Business added automatic removal of old responses, email checks, and 90-day version history.

Upgrade when one of those jobs matters. A public form alone is not a reason to pay.

## Complex rules end the simplicity

Tally can calculate a price or score and route people to different results. A large assessment with ranked outcomes, score gaps, and ties may require helper fields and careful comparisons.

Write a table with sample answers, expected totals, and the correct final page. Test empty fields, ties, boundary values, and routes that should stop early. Repeat the table after every change.

The rule may be the difficult part rather than Tally. Still, a form nobody can explain or maintain is the wrong place for an important decision. Move the logic into a real app or the system receiving the answers.

## Privacy and retention belong in the form design

Ask only for information the process needs. Limit who can open responses, download files, export data, or change the form. Test password protection and email checks when they matter.

Business can remove older responses automatically. Other plans need a manual retention process. Delete a safe test response and file, then confirm what remains in exports and connected tools.

Tally did not receive a new hands-on security or privacy test from us. Review the agreement, storage, deletion, consent, and connected services for the actual form.

## Who should choose Tally?

Tally is a strong choice for almost any small team starting a normal form. Free has enough room to build the real process and learn whether people finish it.

It also handles a few useful branches and simple calculations well. Teams whose forms make high-stakes decisions through dozens of hidden rules should skip it.

Build the hardest branch first and run an answer table through every route. Test the connection, failure path, export, deletion, and real device. Check current fair-use and regional pricing.

Keep Tally when the form remains easy to explain and maintain. If the page has become a hidden software application, the clean editor is hiding too much work.
