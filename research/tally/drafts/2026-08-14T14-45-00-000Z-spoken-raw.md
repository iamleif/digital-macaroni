# Tally review: spoken raw draft

Tally is a form builder that feels like typing a document. Add a question, write the words, and move to the next line. It is much less work than arranging cards and opening settings for every small change.

The bigger reason to use it is the Free plan. Tally includes unlimited forms and responses within fair use, plus files, payments, signatures, logic, calculations, redirects, and links to common tools.

For most contact forms, client intake, event signup, surveys, applications, and simple orders, we would start with Tally before paying for another form tool.

## Normal forms are quick to build

A simple client form might ask for a name, email, service, budget, and project details. Tally lets the business type that page in order and add each answer field where it belongs.

The form can also change as the customer answers. If someone chooses website design, it can show questions about pages and launch dates. If they choose a logo, those questions stay hidden. Tally calls this conditional logic.

The answer can also decide what happens at the end. A qualified lead can go to a booking page. Someone outside the budget can see another message. The result can be added to Google Sheets, Notion, Airtable, or another connected system.

This range is why the public reviews are so strong. G2 shows 4.9 from 124 reviews, and Capterra shows 4.9 from 39. People often praise the simple editor, generous Free plan, integrations, and helpful support.

## Free is a complete tool for most people

Tally does not place a normal response limit on Free. It also includes calculated totals, file uploads up to 10MB per file, payment collection, signatures, password protection, duplicate prevention, custom thank-you pages, and webhooks.

The word unlimited has a fair-use boundary. Tally says it may contact accounts that regularly collect about 50,000 submissions a month, upload 100GB a month or store 500GB total, or send 50,000 email notices a month. Those are examples of heavy use that may need a custom plan.

Most small teams will never reach those numbers. A high-volume event, file-heavy application, or public campaign should still ask Tally about the expected load before launch.

The Free plan keeps Tally branding and the normal Tally address. That is often fine for an internal form or a small business testing the process.

## Pro is mostly about brand and team work

During our European research session, the live page showed Pro at €20 a month and Business at €65. Tally's U.S. help material lists Pro at $29, so the local price and currency should be checked before paying.

Pro removes Tally branding and adds a custom domain, custom CSS, shared workspaces, email from a company domain, larger file uploads, and form analytics. It can also save unfinished responses and restore form versions from the past 30 days.

Business adds automatic removal of old responses, email verification, and 90 days of version history. Automatic removal matters when a company should not keep personal answers forever.

We would not upgrade just because a form is public. Pay when the company branding, domain, team access, unfinished responses, or drop-off report solves a real problem.

## Complex logic is where the simplicity ends

Tally can calculate a price, score a quiz, and send people to different results. It can also build a much more complicated assessment with ranked outcomes, score gaps, and tie-breakers.

That second form takes real work. A detailed G2 reviewer said advanced scoring required helper fields and step-by-step comparisons. Community posts show people debugging totals, fields that stayed at zero, and routes that did not behave as expected.

This does not make Tally bad at logic. It means the rule itself is complicated. A clean editor cannot make 40 conditions easy to reason about.

Build the hardest branch first. Write a small test table with the answers, expected score, and expected final page. Run every row through the form. Test ties, blanks, the lowest value, the highest value, and answers that should stop the form early.

If that becomes impossible to maintain, the process may need a real app or some of the logic may belong in the system receiving the answers.

## Who should use Tally

We recommend Tally to almost any small team starting a normal form. The Free plan has enough room to build the real process, connect it, and learn whether people complete it.

We also recommend it for forms with a few useful branches or a simple price. Ask only the questions that apply, show the result clearly, and keep the path short.

We would be cautious with a form that decides something important through dozens of hidden rules. Tally can hold those rules, but the business must be able to explain and test them.

Tally makes form building unusually easy. It does not pretend a complicated decision is simple just because the questions fit on a nice page.
