## Reader and job

- Primary reader: a software team that needs to ask the right customer a short question at the right point in a website or app, or a regulated team that needs control over deployment
- Plain example: show an onboarding question after a user completes setup, ask why a customer cancels, then link the answer to the correct user and product event
- Cost of failure: the survey targets the wrong person, repeats too often, stores identity incorrectly, lowers product use, or exposes sensitive answers

## Product facts

- Link, web, email, and in-product surveys
- Logic, hidden fields, partial responses, single-use links, API and SDKs
- Cloud Hobby: free, 250 responses monthly
- Cloud Pro: $74 monthly annually, 2,000 responses, unlimited seats, branding removal, respondent identity, segments, integrations, follow-up email, mobile SDKs
- Scale: $325 monthly annually, 5,000 responses, roles, quotas, feedback management, charts, 2FA, spam protection
- Fourteen-day Pro trial
- Community Edition: AGPLv3 core, unlimited surveys, responses, and users
- Enterprise Edition gates branding removal, roles, segments, audit logs, SSO, spam protection, advanced feedback, and support controls

## Experience records

### A product team showed a survey after a real event

- Targeting and API access are repeated strengths
- Consequence: test identity, frequency, eligibility, and the path when an event arrives twice

### A technical team self-hosted for data control

- Some deployments are smooth; others require significant technical work
- Consequence: test upgrades, backups, email, monitoring, and security ownership

### A self-hoster expected an advanced feature in the free edition

- Open-core gates create recurring frustration
- Consequence: make a required-feature list against the license table before deployment

## Feature translation

### In-product survey

- A question shown inside the website or app while the person is using it

### Targeting

- Rules deciding which person sees a survey and when

### Partial response

- Answers saved before the person completes the survey

### Open core

- The core product is open source, while some advanced features use a paid license

## Conflict ledger

- Self-hosting gives control and transfers operations to the team.
- A clean no-code survey sits on top of technical identity and event work.
- Free community use is broad, but company controls and branding are paid.
