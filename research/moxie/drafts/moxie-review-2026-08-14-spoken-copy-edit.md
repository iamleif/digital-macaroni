Moxie is made for freelancers who run the business from too many small tools. A lead arrives through a form. The contact goes into a spreadsheet. The proposal is made in a document app. The contract lives somewhere else. Time is tracked in another tab, and the invoice starts with the same client details copied all over again.

Moxie puts that work together. It keeps the client, proposal, contract, schedule, project, time, messages, invoice, and payment connected.

That is a useful idea for a solo service business. It does not mean Moxie is the best tool in every one of those areas. The buying question is whether its full workflow is good enough to replace several separate apps.

We recommend Moxie for a solo freelancer who wants proposals, contracts, invoices, and client work in one place.

## What Moxie actually does

Moxie starts with the client record. People often call this a CRM. In plain language, it is the place where a business keeps the client's contact details and the work connected to that person. It can show conversations, proposals, contracts, projects, appointments, invoices, and payments without making the freelancer search through separate accounts.

The sales pipeline shows where a possible job stands. A lead might be new, waiting for a call, waiting for a proposal, won, or lost. Once the client agrees, Moxie can send a contract for signature, collect a deposit, schedule a meeting, create the project, track time, and send the invoice.

It also includes forms, a combined calendar and inbox, basic bookkeeping, an AI assistant, and mobile apps. The Pro plan adds a client portal with the freelancer's own branding. A portal gives the client one page for messages, files, forms, appointments, tasks, and bills.

The appeal is not one amazing feature. It is the handoff between ordinary features. A signed proposal can become a project without typing the scope and price again. Tracked time can become an invoice. The invoice stays attached to the client who approved the work.

## Follow one client through the whole job

The best way to test Moxie is not to click through the menu. Create a client that behaves like a real one.

Start with an inquiry form. Use it to create the lead, book a call, and move the lead through the sales columns. Make a proposal with two choices. Turn the chosen offer into a contract and deposit invoice. Sign it from a different email address and pay a small test amount if the payment setup allows a safe test.

Then create the project. Add a deadline, several tasks, and one person helping with delivery. Track time from a desktop and phone. Ask for a file. Send a message. Change the scope after work starts and make sure the new price and approval are recorded.

Finally, send the balance invoice. Mark part of it paid, let another part become late, and send a reminder. Download the invoice and the signed contract. Look at the client record and ask whether the entire history is clear.

This one trip tells you more than a feature list. It shows where Moxie saves typing and where the freelancer still leaves to use a document, spreadsheet, project board, payment page, or accounting tool.

The public feedback is most convincing on this basic client-to-invoice flow. Freelancers repeatedly like having the pieces together. They describe the everyday path as easy and time-saving. Support and frequent product changes get a lot of praise too.

## The client portal has to work without a tour

The client does not care that the freelancer bought an all-in-one system. The client wants to sign, pay, find a file, and know what happens next.

Open the portal from a private browser on a laptop and a phone. Better yet, ask someone who has never seen Moxie to use it. Give that person one sentence: “This is where we will manage the job.” Do not guide each click.

Can the person find the contract, next appointment, open request, shared file, and invoice? Are email alerts understandable? Can a client reply from an email without learning a new inbox? Check what happens when the person forgets a password or uses the wrong email address.

Pro lets the freelancer use a custom domain and remove most Moxie branding. That can make the experience feel like part of the freelancer's own business. Branding is not the important test, though. The portal has to reduce questions. If clients still ask where to sign and how to pay, the portal is decoration.

## Project management is deliberately lighter

Moxie can hold projects, deliverables, tasks, dates, time, and collaborators. That is enough for a writer producing five articles, a designer making a small brand package, or a consultant working through a clear list of sessions.

It may not be enough for an agency coordinating many people across dependent work. A dependency means task B cannot start until task A is complete. Capacity planning means seeing who has ten free hours next week and who is already overloaded. Dedicated project products spend much more of the screen on those problems.

Project management is the main mixed point in the research. Some people find the built-in area clunky or too light for complex planning. Navigation, packages, advanced client tracking, automation coverage, and mobile polish also appear as narrower gaps.

Build the hardest normal project during the trial. Add repeated tasks, internal notes, client approvals, a delayed handoff, and a change that moves later dates. Invite a project collaborator and make sure that person sees the work but not private business information. Try the weekly planning view you would really use.

If the team must keep a separate project tool, that is not an automatic failure. Moxie could still own sales, contracts, and invoices. But count the cost and duplicate updates honestly. An all-in-one product loses much of its point when every project has to be rebuilt elsewhere.

## Automation should remove a repeated step

Moxie Pro includes workflow automation and connections through Zapier, Make, webhooks, and a public API. Those tools let an event in Moxie trigger work in another system.

Start with one boring step that happens for every client. A signed contract could create a project, send a welcome message, request a file, and schedule a reminder. A paid invoice could close the job and send the final materials.

Test each route twice. Then make the trigger happen late or with missing information. No client should receive the welcome message twice. A failed step should be visible and easy to fix. The freelancer should know which system holds the final answer.

Do not build a maze of rules just because automation is included. If a process changes often, a simple checklist may be safer. Moxie is useful when it removes a repeated action, not when the owner spends Friday repairing invisible rules.

## The plans are inexpensive for the breadth

Starter costs $12 when paid monthly or $120 for a year, which works out to $10 per month. It includes client and project management, proposals, contracts, invoices, time tracking, the sales pipeline, basic accounting, forms, scheduling, the combined calendar and inbox, and the AI assistant.

Pro costs $25 monthly or $240 yearly, which works out to $20 per month. It adds the branded client portal, client requests and support tickets, workflow automation, a business phone line in the United States, Canada, or the United Kingdom, outside connections, QuickBooks, and unlimited project collaborators.

Teams costs $40 monthly or $385 yearly. It includes up to five full team members. A team member can work across the business and may send proposals or invoices. A project collaborator is different. That person is invited to selected project work and does not need full access.

There is a 14-day trial. Priority support is listed for everyone. Moxie also describes location-based and need-based scholarships.

The subscription is only part of the cost. Check current payment-processing charges, currencies, payout timing, refunds, disputes, and any phone or message use that could cost more. Ask how taxes appear and what the QuickBooks connection sends. We did not test payments or accounting, so neither should be trusted from a clean demo invoice.

The price looks good if Starter replaces two or three paid tools. Pro will be the more realistic choice for a freelancer who wants the portal and automation that make the suite feel connected. If project work, bookkeeping, and messages still stay elsewhere, add those subscriptions before calling Moxie cheap.

## The high ratings need context

Moxie has a large amount of positive public feedback. Trustpilot showed 536 reviews, and AppSumo showed 83. The all-in-one workflow, value, support, and product improvements repeat across those samples. Mobile reviewers and a small G2 sample point in the same general direction.

The numbers are not neutral. Moxie asks customers for Trustpilot reviews. AppSumo reviewers bought a lifetime deal under terms that current monthly customers do not receive. A six-month external review gives useful detail but contains affiliate links.

Those facts do not erase the praise. They mean the high scores should support a trial, not replace it. We did not create a client, take a payment, run a project, or ask support for help.

## Who should choose Moxie?

Moxie is a strong product to test for a solo freelancer who wants one place for leads, agreements, routine projects, time, and invoices. It may also suit a very small team with simple delivery work.

It is a less natural choice for an agency that needs detailed dependencies, workload planning, complex permissions, deep sales reporting, or a large set of automatic rules. It is unnecessary when a small existing stack already runs smoothly and no one copies information between it.

Our decision rule is to follow one client from hello to paid. Include two proposal choices, a signed contract, a deposit, scheduled work, tracked time, a scope change, a late balance, a client portal visit, and a project collaborator. Do part of it from a phone.

Then export the client, documents, project, and financial records. Cancel every outside tool that Moxie is meant to replace, at least on paper. Count every manual copy and every app that must remain.

Choose Moxie if the client understands the experience and the freelancer can remove enough admin and subscriptions. If the work still lives in several other systems, buying one more “all-in-one” will not make the business simpler.
