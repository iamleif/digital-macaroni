Fathom Analytics tells you how people find and use a website. It shows visits, popular pages, traffic sources, countries, devices, campaigns, and actions such as a signup or purchase.

The difference is what it leaves out. Fathom does not try to copy every screen and setting in Google Analytics. It puts the common traffic questions on one main dashboard that a business owner can read without training.

That makes Fathom easy to like. It also gives us a clear way to judge it. If that short dashboard covers the decisions your team makes, the monthly fee can save time. If your work depends on deep segments, customer paths, or custom reports, Fathom may feel too small.

We recommend Fathom when you want useful traffic numbers without turning analytics into a second job.

## What Fathom makes easier

A normal visit to the Fathom dashboard can answer a useful set of questions. How many people came to the site? Which pages did they read? Did they arrive from Google, a newsletter, or another site? What country and device were they using? Did they complete the action you care about?

Fathom calls that chosen action an event. An event might be a demo request, a purchase, a file download, or a click on a key button. You decide which actions matter and add tracking for them.

Campaign tracking is also included. You add short labels, known as UTM tags, to links in an email or ad. Fathom then groups the visits and results under that campaign. This is enough for a small team to compare two newsletters or see whether a paid promotion led to sales.

The product is designed around privacy. Its tracking script does not build the type of personal visitor profile used for targeted advertising. Fathom says its setup can work under GDPR without an analytics cookie banner. That is useful, but it is not a legal approval for the whole website. Other tools, forms, ads, and embedded media may still set cookies or collect personal data. Check the full site and ask a qualified privacy adviser when the risk matters.

## Simple is the point

The strongest reason to choose Fathom is not a missing feature in Google Analytics. It is that someone may actually look at Fathom.

A dense analytics system can collect enormous amounts of data and still be useless to a small team. If nobody knows where the report lives or trusts how it was built, the extra detail does not improve a decision. Fathom reduces that problem by keeping the main view short and familiar.

That theme appears throughout the customer feedback we reviewed. People like seeing the important traffic numbers quickly. Setup and common website connections are also described as straightforward. Support receives positive mentions in the verified sample, though we did not contact it ourselves.

The tradeoff is just as clear. Fathom gives you fewer ways to break the audience into narrow groups or build a report around an unusual question. It is not a full product analytics tool. It will not replace a detailed study of how logged-in customers move through an app.

This is why a feature list is the wrong way to compare Fathom with Google Analytics. Start with the reports your team used in the last three months. If nobody can name them, the simpler product may be enough. If one report changes ad spending or pricing every week, make sure Fathom can reproduce it before you switch.

## Fathom pricing

The hosted service starts at $15 a month for up to 100,000 pageviews and events. The next levels are $25 for 200,000 and $45 for 500,000. One million costs $60 a month. Annual billing costs ten months, so it gives you two months free.

Every normal plan includes at least 50 websites. This can make the price attractive for an agency, a publisher with several sites, or a business with separate product and campaign domains. Shared dashboards, saved views, ecommerce and event tracking, campaign tracking, data exports, email reports, and API access are included.

There is a seven-day trial but no permanent free plan for the hosted service. Fathom Lite, the older open-source project, can be run on your own server. That is a different choice. The software may be free, but you take responsibility for setup, updates, security, backups, and keeping the collector online.

Two billing details matter. First, custom events use the same allowance as pageviews. A busy site that records several events can move through a tier faster than the page count alone suggests. Second, Fathom looks at ongoing traffic rather than shutting off collection after one spike. Its current policy says it contacts you about an upgrade after two months over the limit.

Compared with a free analytics tool, $15 a month can feel expensive for a small site. Compared with the time spent training staff, fixing reports, and managing consent around a more complex setup, it may be cheap. The answer depends on whether simplicity removes real work.

## How to test Fathom

Do not remove the current analytics code on the first day. Run Fathom beside it for two weeks.

Set up three real events. Use a signup, a sale or lead, and one smaller action that matters. Create links for two active campaigns. Then compare the tools. The visit totals do not need to match exactly because bot filters and privacy rules differ, but you should understand large gaps.

Next, answer the hardest analytics question your team asked recently. Perhaps you need to compare one landing page across campaigns, check sales from a newsletter, or give a client a private dashboard. Build that answer in Fathom without inventing an easier question.

Also export the data, share a dashboard, and check the site speed before and after the script is added. Ask the person who normally reads the report to use Fathom without help. A clean design only matters if it is clear to that person.

At the end of the trial, write down anything you could not answer. Then ask whether each missing detail would change a decision. It is easy to miss a report because it looks impressive. It is harder to name the action you would take from it.

## Who should choose Fathom?

Fathom suits a small business, publisher, agency, or content team that wants a quick and private view of website traffic. It is especially useful when several sites need the same simple reporting or when Google Analytics has become a tool everyone collects and nobody reads.

It is a poor choice for a product team that needs detailed funnels, individual customer journeys, session replay, or many custom groups. It may also be unnecessary if the current tool is already set up well and the team uses it with confidence.

Our decision rule is simple. Run both tools for two weeks. Rebuild the three events and two reports that matter most. Keep Fathom if it answers those questions and makes the numbers easier to use. Keep the deeper tool if a missing report would change money, product, or campaign decisions.

Fathom is not better because it shows less data. It is better when the data it shows is the data you need. For a small team with ordinary website questions, that can be a very good trade.
