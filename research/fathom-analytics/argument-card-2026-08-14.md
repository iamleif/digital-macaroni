# Fathom Analytics private argument card

- Buyer: a small team that wants useful website numbers without maintaining a complex analytics setup
- Job: understand traffic sources, popular pages, campaigns, and key actions
- Thesis: Fathom is worth paying for when a short, readable dashboard covers the decisions the team actually makes; it is not for deep behavioral analysis
- Main reason to buy: privacy-focused tracking and a dashboard that a normal owner can read in minutes
- Main reason to hesitate: limited drill-down and a recurring fee based on pageviews plus events
- When it is unnecessary: the current analytics setup is already understood, or the business needs detailed segments, funnels, user paths, attribution, or product analytics
- Decision rule: run Fathom beside the current tool for two weeks, create the three events and two campaign reports the team uses, compare totals and bot traffic, then ask whether anyone misses a report that affects a decision
- Evidence limit: research-based; no hands-on tracking comparison
