Fillout is an online form builder. It can handle a contact form, but that is only the start. It also supports bookings, payments, signatures, generated PDFs, approval steps, and forms that write directly to Airtable, Notion, Google Sheets, and other tools.

The builder is easy to approach, and the free plan is strong enough for a proper trial. That makes Fillout one of the easier form products to recommend testing.

A form still needs more than a good-looking submit button. The right person must see the right questions. The payment and saved record must agree. Emails need to arrive. An old link must not update the wrong customer. Our verdict depends on those less attractive parts of the workflow.

## Why Airtable and Notion users choose Fillout

A basic form saves each answer as a new row. Fillout can do more with the system behind the form.

Suppose a business keeps clients and projects in Airtable. A new client can fill out an intake form that creates the client record and links it to a project. An existing client can open a private link with known details already filled in. They can change an address and update the right record instead of making a copy.

The same idea works with Notion and other supported tools. This turns Fillout into a front door for a database that customers or occasional staff should not edit directly.

Pre-fill means a form opens with known answers already present. A hidden field passes information, such as a customer ID or campaign name, without showing it on the page. Both are useful. Both can also cause a serious mistake. That can happen if the link reaches the wrong person or a hidden value points to the wrong record.

Test against a copy of real data. Create a new record, update an old one, use linked records, submit the same form twice, and open a private link in a different browser. Check exactly which row changed. Do not assume a successful submission means the database is correct.

The depth of the Airtable and Notion connections is one of the most common reasons people praise Fillout. It is a real advantage when the database is already the center of the work. If all you need is an email from a contact page, that depth will not matter.

## Logic makes the form useful and risky

Conditional logic changes the form based on earlier answers. A person choosing Design can see design questions, while someone choosing Accounting sees a different section. The form can skip pages, calculate a score, show a different ending, or send the person to another page.

This can make a long form feel much shorter. It also creates paths the builder may forget to test.

Draw the branches before launch. Include valid answers, blank required fields, invalid dates, large uploads, a person going backward, and someone returning to a saved form. Test every ending and every email. If a score decides whether someone qualifies, test values just below, at, and just above the cutoff.

A resumed form deserves special attention. The person may return after the underlying database changed or after the team edited the questions. Decide what should happen instead of learning from a customer.

Fillout can collect payments through a form. A payment flow has two records that must agree: the charge at the payment company and the order or application in your system. Make one payment, decline another, close the browser during checkout, and submit twice. Confirm how the team finds and fixes a charge without a matching record.

These are normal tests for any capable form builder. Fillout's easy setup does not remove them. It makes it more likely that a non-technical team can build a complex form, which makes a written test list even more important.

## What the free plan includes

The Free plan allows unlimited forms and unlimited teammates. It includes 1,000 responses a month, multi-page forms, payments, scheduling, and file uploads. You also get PDF creation, workflows, saved progress, calculations, pre-filled answers, hidden fields, and most integrations.

That is unusually useful for a free plan. A business can build the real form and run it with a small group without paying or limiting the trial to a toy example.

Free forms show a Fillout badge and do not include every field type, CAPTCHA, custom endings, signatures, or a redirect after submission. The lack of CAPTCHA matters for a public form that may attract spam.

Starter costs $15 a month when billed as $180 for a year. It raises the allowance to 2,000 responses and adds all field types, login forms, custom endings, redirects, signatures, CAPTCHA, and more design control.

Pro costs $40 a month when billed as $480 for a year. It allows 5,000 responses and adds custom emails, removes Fillout branding, and includes custom share previews, fonts, favicons, and CSS.

Business costs $75 a month when billed as $900 for a year. It includes unlimited responses, form analytics, a custom domain, custom code, partial submissions, data pre-fetching, larger uploads, and priority support. A partial submission saves answers from someone who starts but never finishes.

Monthly billing costs more than those annual rates. Fillout does not charge per seat on these plans, which is helpful when many people need to build or review forms.

The pricing is generous until one specific feature forces a jump. A team may need Business not because of response volume, but because it wants drop-off data, partial answers, a custom domain, or pre-fetched data. Choose the plan from the required controls, not only the monthly submission count.

## The customer feedback is mostly positive

Across G2, Product Hunt, and the visible Trustpilot feedback, people repeatedly praise the quick setup, form logic, integrations, free plan, and responsive support. That matches the practical appeal of the product.

The smaller negative group mentions price, support disputes, payment trouble, formatting, and missing features. Those complaints cover different problems and do not show one broad failure. They do point toward the cases a trial should include.

We did not build or submit a Fillout form. One separate editorial reviewer did claim hands-on use, but that article contains affiliate links, so we treated it as supporting context rather than proof. We cannot claim a payment, database update, PDF, or support response worked in our own test.

## Who should choose Fillout?

Fillout suits a small business or operations team that needs more than a survey. It is especially useful when Airtable or Notion already stores the real records and the form needs to create, update, or link them.

It may be unnecessary for a basic newsletter signup or contact form. It is also not an automatic choice for highly regulated or very sensitive intake. Security features and compliance claims need to be matched to the data, access rules, retention needs, and laws that apply.

Our decision rule is simple. Copy the real database and build the real form. Complete every branch on a phone and desktop. Create and update records, submit a duplicate, resume halfway, fail a payment, check each email, and inspect the PDF. Then reconcile every result with the source system.

If those tests pass, Fillout offers a lot of form power without seat charges and with a genuinely useful free tier. If the team cannot explain what happens on every failure path, the form is not ready, no matter how quickly it was built.
