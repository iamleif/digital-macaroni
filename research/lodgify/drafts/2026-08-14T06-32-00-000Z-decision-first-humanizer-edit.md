# Lodgify review — decision-first, edited

## The short answer

I would use Lodgify for a small vacation-rental business that wants to take bookings through its own website. I would not use it as the only calendar for a business that depends on several booking channels.

The website and booking engine solve a clear problem. The channel manager creates a condition: test every connection before relying on it.

Lodgify also includes reservations, payments, guest messages, and property operations. For a solo host or small team, one system can replace several services. For a larger operation, that benefit does not outweigh the limits in reporting, pricing controls, mobile work, and channel reliability.

## What I would buy it for

I would buy Lodgify for the direct-booking website and booking engine.

A host can publish a site, accept a reservation, collect payment, and manage the booking in the same system. The alternative often involves a website builder, a booking form, a payment service, and a separate calendar. Each handoff creates another account to manage and another place for information to fall out of date.

Onboarding is one of the most consistent positives in the customer feedback. That matters when a host is moving live listings and future reservations into a new system. Setup is part of the product, not a bonus.

The reason to buy Lodgify is not its feature count. It is the chance to reduce the number of systems involved in a direct booking.

## The condition I would put on the purchase

Before moving the business, I would test a new booking, a cancellation, a blocked date, a reopened date, and a guest message. I would make each change on every connected channel and check where it appears. I would repeat the test after the first live bookings.

If a calendar update failed, I would stop the move. A channel manager is supposed to remove the need to compare calendars by hand. If I still have to check Airbnb, Vrbo, and the direct-booking calendar after each change, Lodgify has not removed that work.

Calendar failures are not the usual result in the material we reviewed. They still determine my purchase test because one missed update can create two bookings for the same property and date. The result of a failure matters more here than its frequency.

## How I would treat support

I would use Lodgify support for setup. I would not base the purchase on support fixing a booking problem before it reaches a guest.

Customers often credit the onboarding help. The record is less consistent when a channel or reservation is already affected: some describe delays or being passed between contacts. This is not a claim that support always fails. It is a reason to plan for a booking problem without assuming that support will resolve it in time.

I would document each channel connection, keep access to the original booking channels, and decide in advance how to handle a duplicate reservation. A business that cannot manage that fallback needs a different support record.

## How I would use it day to day

The web product would be my main place to run Lodgify. I would use the mobile app to check information and handle limited tasks, not as the only way to manage the business.

The app does not reproduce all of the web product, and complaints about messages, reports, and connections recur. A host who works mainly from a phone should choose a product built around that workflow.

On the web, the routine is to check reservations, answer guests, review payments, and manage availability. These tasks share the same booking record. The benefit ends when staff have to maintain separate notes or verify changes in other systems.

## Price and value

The Starter plan charges a 1.9 percent booking fee. I would apply that rate to the business's expected direct-booking revenue before comparing Lodgify with another product. The fee grows with sales, so the advertised subscription price is not the full cost.

I would also compare plans by the features the business needs. Some features require a higher plan.

For one host replacing a website, booking form, calendar, and payment workflow, Lodgify can cut software costs and admin time. For a team that already has those systems or needs more reporting and pricing control, the package offers less.

## Who I would recommend it to

I would recommend Lodgify to a solo host or small team with three conditions:

1. Direct bookings are part of the plan.
2. The business can test every connected calendar before relying on it.
3. Most management work can happen in a web browser.

I would rule it out for an operator who needs to trust several booking channels without routine checks. I would also rule it out for a team that works mainly from phones or needs reporting and pricing controls beyond what Lodgify provides.

## Verdict

I score Lodgify 7.7. It can replace several tools for a small host, but the channel-management risk limits the recommendation.

My decision would be to use Lodgify for the website, booking engine, and reservation workflow, then require the calendar tests before depending on its channel manager. If the tests pass, I would continue. If one fails, I would choose another system.
