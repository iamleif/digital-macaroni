# Lodgify review: raw review-skill draft

## Our verdict

Lodgify makes the most sense when the direct-booking website is the reason you are shopping. It gives a small rental business a site, booking engine, payments, guest messages, and channel calendar without asking the owner to join those pieces together. That is enough to recommend it to the right host.

The reservation calendar keeps the recommendation from being broad. Lodgify is supposed to carry a booking or blocked date from one channel to the others, yet the most useful failure accounts involve existing Vrbo reservations, listing mappings, and blocked dates going missing during a move. These are not complaints about a button being hard to find. They are the dates the rest of the business is built around.

We recommend Lodgify to a small operator who wants direct bookings and can audit the migration before turning on instant booking. If the business needs to move many live listings and trust them on day one, we would choose another system.

## The website is the reason to buy

Lodgify's current plans make its priorities easy to read. Basic is for one property and centers on the channel calendar and inbox. Starter adds the website builder, booking widget, payments, reports, co-host access, and a guided onboarding call. In other words, Lodgify starts to become distinctive when the host wants guests to book outside Airbnb and Vrbo.

That bundle solves a real problem for an owner who does not want to maintain a website builder, booking form, payment service, and separate reservation tool. A direct booking can enter the same record used for guest messages and open dates. Fewer handoffs mean fewer places to copy a rate, payment, or guest detail.

There is a limit to the website argument. Several operators describe the included designs as basic, and one Reddit user who had used Lodgify for a few months wanted to keep only a booking page without letting Lodgify take over the rest of the listings. Lodgify is a better fit when the booking site and the property system are both part of the purchase. If you already have a site you like and only need calendar sync, much of the reason to choose it disappears.

## Setup help is useful, but migration still belongs to the host

The onboarding praise is unusually consistent. The recent Trustpilot page is full of new customers describing one-to-one calls where someone walked through the account, answered questions, and sent notes, recordings, emails, or videos afterward. One customer said the call surfaced features they would have missed. Another valued being able to return to the recorded session while finishing setup.

That is good evidence for one claim: Lodgify helps new customers configure the product. It tells us much less about what happens after a channel is live because so many of those reviews were written at the end of onboarding. A helpful setup call and a fast response to a broken reservation are different jobs.

The distinction matters when moving existing inventory. One operator brought six furnished rentals into Lodgify because Appfolio did not suit monthly turnover and the business wanted more channels. Existing Vrbo reservations and blocked dates failed to appear in Lodgify. When a new Airbnb listing opened, an already occupied date was booked again.

Another small operator said Vrbo never connected, leaving bookings to be updated by hand across Airbnb and Booking.com. That account also ended with a double booking. Lodgify replied in the public thread and asked for the account details, but the thread does not show whether the problem was fixed.

Those cases do not prove that Lodgify's channel manager usually fails. They do show where a buyer should refuse to take the setup on faith. Before instant booking goes live, compare every existing reservation and blocked date with the original channel. Match properties by address, not only by listing name. Then create and cancel a booking on each connection. If the old dates do not arrive or a test change goes missing, stop there.

## The product gets harder to trust as the operation grows

Small operators can get a lot out of Lodgify. One two-site glamping business valued the real-time channel links, bookings from smaller platforms through the API, and a property module used to coordinate cleaning and repairs. That is the full Lodgify idea working as intended: the booking enters once and the related work stays with it.

The same operator later began looking at alternatives after Lodgify changed the calendar controls. They could still set default rates, but could no longer see how OTA markups changed the final prices. Their workaround was to create mock bookings on each OTA. The software still held the bookings, yet one of the checks it was meant to centralize had moved back to the channel websites.

The mobile record is just as dependent on scale and version. Older App Store reviews say the app grew to cover most tasks that once required a laptop. A 2026 review from an operator with 20 properties describes a different product: minute-long loading, owner statements that grouped fees incorrectly, and support that did not resolve the issue after receiving a screen recording. Lodgify answered that a new release addressed loading and stability, but we have not verified the result.

That conflict is more useful than a blanket claim that the app is good or bad. A small owner who checks bookings on a phone has less at stake than a manager who needs the app and owner reports to be correct across 20 properties. The more staff, reports, and channel mappings Lodgify has to carry, the more proof we would want before moving the business.

## Price and value

Lodgify's pricing changed after our first research pass. The current page lists four plans and advertises a 0 percent booking fee, so the previous 1.9 percent Starter fee should no longer be used. Lodgify says the actual subscription depends on property count and billing term.

Plan boundaries matter more than a single advertised monthly figure. Basic covers one property but does not include the direct-booking website. Starter is available for up to two properties and adds the site, booking widget, secure payments, guided onboarding, reports, and co-host access. Automated messages and phone support begin with Professional, while owner statements, task management, and advanced reports sit in Ultimate.

For one or two properties, Starter contains the part of Lodgify we find most persuasive. The calculation becomes less clear when a larger operation needs Ultimate for staff and owner reporting, because that is also where the detailed complaints about reporting and app performance carry more weight. Get the quote for the real property count and judge it against the tools Lodgify would replace, not against the cheapest number on the page.

## Who should buy it

Lodgify fits an owner-run rental business that wants direct bookings, prefers one system to a custom stack, and has time to verify the channel move before taking new reservations. The website and booking engine are doing enough work in that case to justify accepting some limits elsewhere.

We would skip it for a host who only needs a channel calendar. We would also skip it for a larger team whose decision depends on phone access, owner statements, or detailed comparisons across properties until those workflows have been tested with the team's own data.

The final decision comes down to what Lodgify is replacing. If it replaces four disconnected tools and opens a useful direct-booking channel, it can simplify a small rental business. If it replaces a channel manager that already handles live inventory without manual checks, the move adds more risk than the website removes.
