# Lodgify review — decision-first draft

## The short answer

I would consider Lodgify for a small vacation-rental business that wants to take more bookings through its own website. I would not use it as the only calendar for a business that depends on several booking channels.

That is the decision. The website and booking engine solve a clear problem. The channel manager introduces a risk that has to be tested before the business relies on it.

Lodgify also includes reservations, payments, guest messages, and property operations. For a solo host or a small team, keeping those jobs in one system can replace several services. For a larger operation, that benefit does not outweigh the limits in reporting, pricing controls, mobile work, and channel reliability.

## What I would buy it for

I would buy Lodgify for the direct-booking website and booking engine.

A host can publish a site, accept a reservation, collect payment, and manage the booking in the same system. This matters because the alternative often involves a website builder, a booking form, a payment service, and a separate calendar. Each handoff creates another account to manage and another place for information to fall out of date.

Lodgify also appears to handle setup well. That matters for a host who is moving live listings and future reservations into a new system. I would count onboarding as part of the product, not as a bonus.

I would not buy Lodgify because it has the longest feature list. I would buy it to reduce the number of systems involved in a direct booking.

## The condition I would put on the purchase

I would test every channel connection before moving the business to Lodgify.

The test would include a new booking, a cancellation, a blocked date, a reopened date, and a guest message. I would make each change on every connected channel and check where it appears. I would repeat the test after the first live bookings.

If a calendar update failed, I would stop the move. A channel manager is supposed to remove the need to compare calendars by hand. If I still have to check Airbnb, Vrbo, and the direct-booking calendar after each change, Lodgify has not removed that work.

Calendar failures do not appear to be the usual result. I would still use them as the purchase test because one missed update can create two bookings for the same property and date. That consequence matters more than the average rating.

## How I would treat support

I would treat Lodgify support as help with setup, not as protection from a booking problem.

The record on onboarding is better than the record on urgent cases. Some customers describe delays or being passed between support contacts when a channel or reservation is already affected. That does not mean support always fails. It means I would not make the purchase on the assumption that support will fix a live problem before it reaches a guest.

This changes my operating plan. I would document each channel connection, keep access to the original booking channels, and decide in advance how to handle a duplicate reservation. A business that cannot manage that fallback needs a system with a better record in urgent support cases.

## How I would use it day to day

I would use the web product as the main place to run Lodgify. I would use the mobile app to check information and handle limited tasks, but not as the only way to manage the business.

The app does not reproduce all of the web product, and there are recurring complaints about messages, reports, and connections. A host who works mainly from a phone should choose a product built around that workflow.

On the web, the daily work is straightforward: check reservations, answer guests, review payments, and manage availability. The value comes from having those tasks share the same booking record. That value disappears when staff have to maintain separate notes or verify changes in other systems.

## Price and value

The Starter plan charges a 1.9 percent booking fee. I would calculate that fee from the business's expected direct-booking revenue before comparing Lodgify with another product. A monthly subscription can look inexpensive while the booking fee grows with sales.

I would also compare plans by the features the business needs, not by the advertised entry price. Some features require a higher plan.

For one host replacing a website, booking form, calendar, and payment workflow, Lodgify can reduce both software costs and admin time. For a team that already has those systems or needs more reporting and pricing control, the same package offers less value.

## Who I would recommend it to

I would recommend Lodgify to a solo host or small team with three conditions:

1. Direct bookings are part of the plan.
2. The business can test every connected calendar before relying on it.
3. Most management work can happen in a web browser.

I would not recommend it to an operator who needs to trust several booking channels without routine checks. I would also rule it out for a team that works mainly from phones or needs reporting and pricing controls beyond what Lodgify provides.

## Verdict

Lodgify gets a 7.7 from us. The score reflects a product that can replace several tools for a small host, with a channel-management risk that limits the recommendation.

My decision would be to use Lodgify for the website, booking engine, and reservation workflow, then require the calendar tests before depending on its channel manager. If those tests pass, it is a reasonable choice for a small rental business. If they do not, I would choose another system.
