# Lodgify review: spoken raw draft

Lodgify is most useful for a host who wants a direct-booking website and does not already have one. It gives you a site where guests can see your rentals, check the dates, and book without going through Airbnb or Vrbo. The website builder is limited, but it gets the job done.

If you already have a website you like, that feature will not matter much. You can add Lodgify's booking form to your site, but then the bigger question is whether Lodgify can keep all your bookings and open dates correct across every place you advertise. This is where we have concerns.

## The website builder is useful if you need one

You do not need to know how to code or connect several different tools. Lodgify gives you the website, booking form, payment tools, and booking calendar in one account. A guest can book on your website, and the reservation appears beside your Airbnb and Vrbo bookings.

The designs are fairly basic. That may not bother a host who simply needs a working site and wants to start taking bookings. It will matter more if the website is a big part of the brand or needs a custom design.

Lodgify's Starter plan includes the website builder and works with up to two properties. This is the plan that makes the most sense for a small host starting from scratch. If you already paid for a website, Lodgify is replacing fewer tools, so there is less reason to move everything over.

## Keeping every calendar correct is the main concern

When someone books through Airbnb, Lodgify should block those dates on Vrbo and your own site. The same thing should happen whichever site the booking comes from. If one calendar misses the change, two guests can book the same property.

Some hosts say those connections work well. The more serious problems appeared when hosts connected listings that already had future bookings and blocked dates.

One host moved six furnished rentals into Lodgify. Existing Vrbo reservations and dates the host had blocked did not show up. A new Airbnb listing then accepted a booking for a date that was already occupied.

Another small host said Vrbo never connected properly. The host still had to change calendars by hand across Airbnb and Booking.com, and the business ended up with a double booking. Lodgify asked for the account details after the host discussed the problem in public, but the discussion does not say whether it was fixed.

This does not mean Lodgify loses bookings for everyone. It does mean you should not connect your accounts and assume all your old dates moved over. Check every existing reservation and blocked date before you accept new bookings. Make sure each property is connected to the right listing, then make and cancel a test booking on every site.

## Setup support is better than help with a live problem

Lodgify gets good feedback for helping new customers set up their accounts. New hosts describe one-to-one calls where a staff member answered questions, walked through settings, and sent notes or a recording afterward. That is useful when you have never built a booking site before.

The picture is less reassuring after a booking or connection has already gone wrong. Some hosts describe slow replies, tickets being closed, or different staff members picking up the same problem. We would not assume support will fix a broken calendar connection before it affects a guest.

This is why the setup call is worth using. Ask the person helping you to check the old bookings, blocked dates, and property connections with you. A friendly tour of the dashboard is not enough.

## Lodgify is easier to recommend for a small business

A host with two glamping sites used Lodgify to take bookings and plan jobs for cleaners and repair workers. That is a sensible use of the product. The booking comes in, and the work connected to the stay can be kept in the same account.

The same host ran into trouble after Lodgify changed the way rates appeared in the calendar. The final prices shown on Airbnb and other booking sites were no longer visible inside Lodgify, so the host made fake bookings on each site to check the price. A tool that is supposed to save time should not send you back to every booking site to check its work.

The phone app may also be enough for a small host who wants to check a booking away from a computer. Older App Store reviews say it handles more of the day-to-day work than it once did. A 2026 review from a host with 20 properties described a different experience: the app took about a minute to load, and owner reports put fees in the wrong place. Lodgify said a newer version improved loading and stability, but we do not know if the reporting problem was fixed.

Someone with one or two rentals may never hit those problems. A manager with many properties, staff members, and owners waiting for monthly reports has more to lose when the app or reports are wrong.

## Pricing

Lodgify currently lists Basic, Starter, Professional, and Ultimate plans, with a 0 percent booking fee. The actual subscription price changes with the number of properties and the billing term, so the website does not give us one useful monthly price for everyone.

Basic is for one property and leaves out the website builder. Starter works with up to two properties and adds the website, booking form, payments, setup call, reports, and co-host access. Professional adds automated messages and phone support. Ultimate includes property tasks, owner reports, and more detailed reporting.

Ask for the price for the number of properties you actually manage. Then look at what Lodgify would replace. Paying for Starter can make sense if it replaces a website builder, booking form, and separate booking calendar. It makes less sense when those tools are already working well.

## Who should use Lodgify

We recommend Lodgify for a small host who needs a direct-booking website. The builder is not fancy, but it can put your rentals online quickly and let guests book them. The guided setup is also useful if this is your first time connecting several booking sites.

We would skip it if you already have a website and only want one calendar for a group of live listings. We would also be careful with a larger rental business that depends on the phone app, owner reports, or several property connections working without regular checks.

If you do choose Lodgify, check every old booking and blocked date before turning on instant booking. That one job matters more than how polished the website looks.
