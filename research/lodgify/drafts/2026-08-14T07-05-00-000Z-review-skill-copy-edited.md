# Lodgify review: copy-edited draft

## Our verdict

Lodgify is easier to understand once you stop treating it as a general property management system. Its real pitch is direct booking: a host can build a site, take payment, and put the reservation in the same calendar used for Airbnb and Vrbo. For a one- or two-property business that wants to rely less on those booking sites, the combination has a point.

The trouble starts when Lodgify takes control of inventory that is already live elsewhere. The most useful failure accounts involve existing Vrbo reservations, blocked dates, and property mappings that went missing during a move. One missed date can leave two guests holding bookings for the same rental.

We recommend Lodgify to a small operator who wants direct bookings and can check the migration before turning on instant booking. A business that needs to move many live listings and trust them on day one should choose another system.

## The website is the reason to buy

Lodgify's current plans make its priorities clear. Basic centers on the channel calendar and inbox. Starter adds the website builder, booking widget, payments, reports, co-host access, and a guided onboarding call. Lodgify becomes more useful when a host wants guests to book outside Airbnb and Vrbo.

That setup can replace a website builder, booking form, payment service, and separate reservation tool. The savings are not only financial. A direct booking enters the same record used for guest messages and open dates, so the host has fewer rates, payments, and guest details to copy between accounts.

The website does not settle the decision for everyone. Some operators describe the included designs as basic. One host who had used Lodgify for a few months wanted to keep a simple booking page without letting Lodgify run the listings on other sites. If you already have a website you like and only need calendar sync, much of Lodgify's value disappears.

## Setup help does not remove the migration risk

The onboarding record is one of the clearest positives in the research. Recent Trustpilot entries describe one-to-one calls where Lodgify staff walked through the account, answered questions, and sent notes, recordings, emails, or videos afterward. Customers valued the details: finding settings they had missed and having a recording to consult while they finished the work.

Most of those reviews were written at the start of the relationship. They tell us that Lodgify can guide a new customer through setup. They say less about the response after a booking or channel has already failed.

That gap matters during a move. One operator brought six furnished rentals into Lodgify because Appfolio did not suit the business and a client wanted listings on more sites. Existing Vrbo reservations and blocked dates failed to appear in Lodgify. A new Airbnb listing then accepted a booking for an occupied date.

Another small operator said Vrbo never connected, so bookings still had to be updated by hand across Airbnb and Booking.com. That account also ended in a double booking. Lodgify replied in the public discussion and asked for the account details, but the thread does not show whether the problem was fixed.

These accounts do not establish that Lodgify usually loses bookings. They identify the part of setup a buyer should never take on faith. Before instant booking goes live, compare every existing reservation and blocked date with the original channel. Match properties by address, not only by listing name. Then create and cancel a test booking on each connection. Stop the move if an old date or test change fails to appear.

## Growth exposes a different set of limits

A two-site glamping operator shows why the product can be worth the trouble. The business used Lodgify for live channel links, bookings from smaller platforms, and a property module that helped coordinate cleaning and repairs. Bookings entered once, and the related work stayed with them.

The same operator later began looking at alternatives after Lodgify changed its calendar controls. Default rates could still be set, but the final OTA prices were no longer visible inside Lodgify. The host began creating mock bookings on each OTA to check the markups. One of the jobs Lodgify had centralized moved back to the channel websites.

Mobile use follows a similar split. Older App Store reviews say the app grew to cover tasks that once required a laptop. A 2026 review from an operator with 20 properties describes minute-long loading, owner statements that grouped fees incorrectly, and support that did not resolve the issue after receiving a screen recording. Lodgify replied that a new release addressed loading and stability; we have not verified whether it fixed that account's problems.

The app may be enough for a small owner checking bookings away from a desk. We would demand more proof from a manager who needs the app and owner statements to work across 20 properties. Staff, reports, and additional channel mappings create more places for a small fault to become daily work.

## Price and value

Lodgify changed its pricing after our first research pass. The current page lists four plans and advertises a 0 percent booking fee, so the previous 1.9 percent Starter fee is out of date. The company says the subscription price depends on property count and billing term.

The plan boundaries are more useful than one advertised monthly figure. Basic covers one property but leaves out the direct-booking website. Starter is available for up to two properties and adds the site, booking widget, payments, guided onboarding, reports, and co-host access. Automated messages and phone support begin with Professional. Owner statements, task management, and advanced reports sit in Ultimate.

Starter contains the part of Lodgify we find most persuasive for one or two properties. The calculation gets harder for an operation that needs Ultimate for staff and owner reporting, since the more detailed complaints about reporting and app performance come from that end of the market. Ask for a quote based on the real property count, then compare it with the tools Lodgify would replace.

## Who should buy it

Lodgify fits an owner-run rental business that wants direct bookings, prefers one system to a custom stack, and has time to check the channel move before accepting new reservations. In that case, the website and booking engine do enough work to justify the limits elsewhere.

Skip it if you only need a channel calendar. Larger teams should also wait until they have tested phone access, owner statements, and property comparisons with their own data.

The final decision depends on what Lodgify would replace. When it replaces four disconnected tools and opens a useful direct-booking channel, it can simplify a small rental business. When it replaces a channel manager that already handles live inventory without manual checks, the website is not enough reason to move.
