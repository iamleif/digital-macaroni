## Research scope

- Review type: research-based
- Product access: none
- Research refreshed: 2026-08-14
- Sources reopened: Lodgify pricing, Capterra, Trustpilot, Apple App Store, and two Reddit discussions
- Platform totals are not sample counts. Capterra displayed 1,311 reviews and Trustpilot displayed 4,159 during this refresh.
- Individual material inspected during the refresh: two detailed Capterra records, at least 18 visible Trustpilot entries, five App Store records, and about ten relevant accounts or replies across two Reddit discussions.
- Limits: no Lodgify account was used; the sources cover different plans, property counts, channels, and product versions; several detailed failures concern Vrbo migration or mapping rather than ordinary daily use.

## Buyer and job

- Primary buyer: an owner-run vacation-rental business with a small portfolio
- Main job: add direct bookings without maintaining a separate website, booking form, payment flow, and reservation calendar
- Current alternative: rely on Airbnb or Vrbo, or assemble a website and booking stack from separate tools
- Cost of failure: lost bookings, manual calendar checks, or two guests holding the same dates

## Product facts

- Lodgify combines a direct-booking website and widget with reservations, channel connections, messages, payments, reporting, and property tasks.
- The current pricing page lists Basic, Starter, Professional, and Ultimate. It advertises a 0% booking fee and says the final price varies with property count and billing term.
- Basic is limited to one property on the current page. Starter is limited to two and adds the website builder, booking widget, payments, onboarding, reports, and co-host access.
- Professional adds automated messages, Google Vacation Rentals, manual payments, and phone support. Ultimate adds task management, owner reports, analytics, a guest app, and priority support.
- The previous evidence packet's 1.9% Starter booking fee is stale and must not appear in the new draft.

## Experience records

### Six furnished rentals moving beyond Vrbo

- Source: Reddit, “Advice for optimizing use of Lodgify,” refreshed 2026-08-14
- Context: six furnished rentals; moving from Appfolio and expanding beyond Vrbo
- Event: existing Vrbo reservations and blocked dates did not appear after the Lodgify integration
- Result: a new Airbnb listing accepted a booking for an occupied date
- Consequence: migration checks matter more than a general statement about sync quality
- Confidence: medium; detailed first-person account, but one configuration

### Small operator using two sites

- Source: Reddit, “Thinking of switching to Lodgify,” refreshed 2026-08-14
- Context: two glamping sites
- Positive: valued channel connections, instant booking through other connected services, and the property-manager module for cleaners and repairs
- Negative: after calendar controls changed, the operator could no longer see the OTA effect of rate changes inside Lodgify and resorted to mock bookings on each OTA to verify prices
- Consequence: Lodgify can suit a small operation and still create verification work when controls change
- Confidence: medium-high; detailed mixed account with named workflows

### Vrbo connection never completed

- Source: Reddit, same switching discussion
- Context: small operator using Airbnb, Booking.com, and Vrbo
- Event: Vrbo would not sync; manual calendar work continued
- Result: double booking; Lodgify later replied publicly and asked for account details
- Consequence: support response after public escalation does not establish that the original case was resolved
- Confidence: medium; detailed outcome but unclear configuration and final resolution

### Twenty-property mobile and reporting failure

- Source: Apple App Store, January 2026 review
- Context: 20 properties and a paid reporting feature
- Event: app load times reached about 60 seconds; owner statements grouped fees incorrectly; support had received a screen recording
- Result: the reviewer could not use the report and felt trapped by the cost of switching
- Vendor response: Lodgify said a new app version addressed loading and stability
- Consequence: the mobile and reporting concerns matter more for an established portfolio than for a new one-property account
- Confidence: medium-high; specific account and vendor response, but no independent verification after the update

### Positive mobile records

- Source: Apple App Store, 2023 and 2024 reviews
- Context: small rental businesses using channel and pricing integrations
- Event: reviewers said the app covered most tasks once limited to the web product and praised faster access to calendar information
- Consequence: the app record is mixed and version-sensitive; do not label it universally unusable
- Confidence: medium; detailed but older than the negative 2026 record

### Onboarding records

- Source: current Trustpilot page
- Context: mostly new customers and first onboarding calls
- Repeated details: step-by-step account setup, questions answered live, follow-up notes, email, video, or recorded material, and product features the customer had missed
- Consequence: the praise supports a narrow claim about guided setup, not a broad claim that all support is dependable
- Confidence: high for the onboarding stage; low for long-term support because the visible page is heavily weighted toward new accounts

### Capterra reporting record

- Source: Capterra, April 2026
- Context: self-employed real-estate owner, fewer than six months of use, four properties mentioned in the review
- Positive: used Lodgify to replace manual reporting across three booking platforms; valued one set of booking, payout, and fee records
- Limit: wanted side-by-side property comparisons over custom date ranges
- Source caveat: vendor-referred review with a nominal incentive disclosed by Capterra
- Consequence: reporting can remove spreadsheet work for a small portfolio, but depth becomes a concern as the owner compares properties
- Confidence: medium because the workflow is specific but the review was incentivized

## Repeated patterns

### Direct booking is the clearest reason to buy

- Support: official plan design, G2 summary, Capterra records, and Reddit accounts
- Conflict: some users call the templates basic or want a separate custom site
- Scope: strongest for hosts who need the booking engine as well as the site
- Editorial consequence: a buyer without a direct-booking plan should not pay for Lodgify merely to gain another calendar

### Onboarding and incident support are different products

- Support: recent Trustpilot entries repeatedly describe useful one-to-one setup calls
- Conflict: Reddit and App Store accounts describe delays, ticket closure, or weak follow-through after a live fault
- Scope: stage-specific, not a universal good-support/bad-support verdict
- Editorial consequence: give onboarding credit without treating support as insurance against a live booking failure

### Channel trouble clusters around migration and configuration

- Support: pre-existing Vrbo reservations, blocked dates, listing mapping, and manual OTA verification recur in detailed failure accounts
- Conflict: other small operators describe real-time channel connections working as intended
- Scope: the evidence does not establish a universal sync defect
- Editorial consequence: the decision rule should focus on migration and mapping tests before instant booking is opened

### Portfolio size changes which flaws matter

- Support: a two-site operator valued the core package; a four-property owner wanted deeper comparisons; a 20-property operator described mobile and reporting failures as costly to escape
- Editorial consequence: avoid an arbitrary property cutoff, but become less confident as the business depends on reports, staff access, and many channel mappings

## Conflict ledger

- Setup support can be useful while incident support is slow because those requests reach Lodgify at different stages and with different urgency.
- Channel sync can work for most small operators while migration failures remain purchase-critical because the failures are uncommon but expensive.
- The mobile app can cover most daily tasks for one host and still fail a 20-property operator because app version, workload, and reporting needs differ.
- Lodgify can reduce software sprawl while still create verification work when an OTA connection or rate control changes.

## Missing evidence

- No test account confirms the current website builder, booking flow, or channel setup.

## Feature translation

### Direct-booking website

- What it does: gives a host a website where guests can see the properties, check dates, book, and pay without using Airbnb or Vrbo
- Who cares: hosts who do not already have a useful website and want more direct bookings
- When it does not matter: the host already has a website and only needs a booking form or calendar connection
- Spoken version: the builder is limited, but it gets a small rental business online without hiring someone to build the booking system

### Channel management and calendar sync

- What it does: when a booking or blocked date is added on Airbnb, Vrbo, or the host's own site, the same dates should become unavailable everywhere else
- Who cares: anyone listing the same property on more than one booking site
- When it does not matter: the host uses only one booking source
- Spoken version: every site needs to agree about which nights are still open

### Migration and listing mapping

- What it does: moves existing bookings and blocked dates into Lodgify and connects each rental to the correct listing on every booking site
- Who cares: hosts switching with live listings and future bookings already on the calendar
- When it does not matter: a new host starting with no existing bookings
- Spoken version: check that every old booking moved over and that each property is connected to the right listing before accepting new bookings

### Owner statements and reporting

- What it does: shows bookings, payouts, fees, and property results for the host or property owner
- Who cares: managers handling several properties or reporting to other owners
- When it does not matter: a host with one property and simple bookkeeping elsewhere
- Spoken version: reports can replace a spreadsheet for a small business, but larger managers may need more detail than Lodgify provides
