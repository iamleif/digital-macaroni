# Salesflare writer's notebook

## Research scope

- Research-based review; no hands-on account
- Current pricing checked 2026-08-14
- Large review-platform sample plus a small community sample
- No import, mailbox, pipeline, reporting, export, billing, or support test

## Reader and job

- Primary reader: a small B2B company selling through email, calls, and meetings
- Plain example: a lead replies in Gmail, books a call, receives a proposal, goes quiet, and later talks to another team member
- Cost of failure: missing the reply, duplicating the contact, forgetting the follow-up, exposing a private message, or losing history during a handoff

## Product facts

- Customer relationship management system for contacts, companies, deals, emails, meetings, notes, tasks, and sales stages
- Pulls data from email, calendar, phone, and connected services
- Growth: $29 per user monthly billed yearly or $39 billed monthly
- Pro: $49 yearly or $64 monthly; adds multi-step email workflows, permissions, and custom dashboards
- Enterprise: $99 yearly or $124 monthly; adds migration, training, and an account manager
- Lead credits: 5 Growth, 100 Pro, 250 Enterprise each month

## Experience records

### Less manual entry is the main attraction

- Automatic capture is a common positive theme
- Consequence: test accuracy, duplicates, omissions, and corrections, not just the number of fields filled

### Small teams find it easier than larger CRMs

- Ease of use and support appear repeatedly
- Consequence: run a real handoff between two people and see whether the next action is obvious

### Reporting and customization have a ceiling

- Limits appear in independent and community feedback
- Consequence: build one current management report and one unusual sales route before committing

## Feature translation

### CRM

- The shared record of who the company is talking to, what happened, what may be sold, and what needs to happen next

### Pipeline

- The board that moves possible deals through stages such as new lead, meeting, proposal, and won or lost

### Enrichment

- Filling in company and contact details from connected accounts and outside data instead of typing every field

### Email sequence

- A planned series of emails and follow-up steps that stops or changes when the lead responds

## Conflict ledger

- Automatic capture saves typing and can create wrong matches.
- Email tracking helps time follow-up and may be blocked or inappropriate.
- A focused setup is easier to learn and offers less room for unusual processes.
- A full activity timeline is useful only when the team agrees what the next action means.

## Missing evidence

- No import, deduplication, Gmail, Outlook, calendar, LinkedIn, enrichment, sequence, permissions, dashboard, mobile, export, billing, privacy, deliverability, or support test

## Spoken language bank

- follow one real lead from first email to handoff
- automatic does not mean correct
- the next person should know what happened and what to do
- test the odd deal, not only the normal one
- export before moving the whole customer list
