Salesflare is a CRM for small companies that sell to other businesses. It connects email, calendars, mobile work, and related tools to build contact records with less typing. That is a useful answer to the problem of salespeople forgetting to update a CRM.

Automatic records can still be wrong. A meeting may attach to the wrong person, two contacts may merge, or an important promise may stay in a private message. A full-looking timeline is useful only when the team trusts it.

I recommend Salesflare for a small B2B team with a direct sales process. Follow one real lead from first email through handoff and silence. Teams needing deep reports, unusual workflows, or strict access controls should look elsewhere.

## Automatic capture has to beat manual cleanup

Salesflare can pull contact details, emails, meetings, and activity into one timeline. Public reviews often praise the reduced entry work, email integration, ease, and support.

Import 100 contacts that reflect the real database. Include matching names, several people at one company, aliases, forwarded mail, personal addresses, and someone who changed jobs.

Compare the result with the inbox and calendar. Check wrong companies, missed meetings, merged people, and duplicates. A normal user should be able to fix an error without waiting for an administrator.

Automation earns value when cleanup takes less time than the old entry work.

## A handoff should need no private briefing

Give an active lead to a second salesperson. Ask what the buyer requested, what the company promised, and what should happen next. The answers should be clear from the timeline.

Personal replies must look different from automated messages. An old promise should not disappear behind newer activity. Notes need enough context for someone who was not on the call.

If the second person searches chat and asks the first owner for a tour, the CRM has moved the mess rather than fixed it.

## Pipeline stages need hard rules

Define what allows a deal to enter and leave each stage. A proposal stage may begin when the document is sent, while another team may wait for the buyer to open it. Shared rules make the board useful.

Test a deal that pauses, reopens, splits, or involves two companies. Check whether Salesflare can show the real situation without false stages and awkward workarounds.

Its focused design suits a short B2B process. Several products, territories, approval layers, or custom reporting may expose the ceiling.

## Replies must stop the wrong sequence

Pro includes planned email sequences. They can keep a small team consistent when messages stop after a reply, opt-out, job change, or ownership transfer.

Send a sequence only to internal test addresses. Check timing, sender identity, replies, unsubscribe handling, duplicate sends, and ownership changes. Let a lead reply midway and confirm no inappropriate follow-up continues.

Email opens and link clicks are weak signals. Security scanners can open a link, while blocked images can hide a human read. Review privacy, consent, customer expectations, and delivery effects before tracking people.

## Reports must answer the Monday questions

Write down the weekly decisions before choosing a plan. The team may need new opportunities, idle deals, stage losses, expected closes, and workload by salesperson.

Build those reports from trial data. Open the deals under each total, change a stage, and check the update. Export one result and calculate it separately.

Growth has standard dashboards, while Pro adds custom dashboards. Public feedback suggests reporting and extensive changes are weaker than the core CRM. Buy Pro only when its report reproduces the view management already uses.

## Permissions and mobile can change the plan

Pro adds user permissions. Create a normal salesperson, manager, and temporary user. Check what each person can search, export, change, delete, and see in private timelines.

Remove the temporary user and confirm the company history remains. Teams with contractors, regions, or sensitive conversations should test these rules before importing the full database.

Follow the same real lead on a phone. Add a note, move the deal, attach a file, set the next task, and find the older promise. Mobile work must keep the record honest away from a desk.

## Per-user pricing is clear but plan-sensitive

When our evidence was collected, Growth cost $29 per user monthly with annual billing or $39 month to month. Pro cost $49 annually or $64 monthly. Enterprise cost $99 annually or $124 monthly.

Growth included five lead credits each month, Pro included 100, and Enterprise included 250. Added users were prorated, while disabled-user credit applied to later invoices.

Price every person and the plan required for sequences, permissions, and custom reports. Check current prices and credit rules before buying. Include migration, cleanup, and training time.

## Who should choose Salesflare?

Salesflare is worth testing for a small B2B team that works mainly from email and wants a focused CRM. Its automatic record can remove routine entry and improve handoffs.

It is a weaker fit for deep reporting, extensive changes, or a complex sales organization. We did not import data, connect a mailbox, run a sequence, test mobile, export, or contact support.

Follow one real lead from email to meeting, proposal, handoff, silence, and export. Test awkward contacts, a reply during a sequence, permissions, mobile work, and the Monday report.

Keep Salesflare when the shared record stays correct and another person always knows the next action. If automation creates quiet errors, less typing has produced a less honest CRM.
