# Recall private argument card

- Buyer: a person who saves articles, videos, podcasts, PDFs, and notes but rarely finds or uses them later
- Job: reduce a source to a useful map, connect it with prior material, and recover the exact evidence later
- Thesis: Recall is useful only when it turns saving into verified reuse; a beautiful graph of partial AI summaries is another unread archive
- Main reason to buy: fast summaries, automatic organization, whole-library chat, and resurfaced connections across formats
- Main reason to hesitate: partial capture, AI errors, browser and language rough edges, fair-use limits, and unclear large-library reliability
- When it is unnecessary: a read-later or notes system already makes saved material easy to retrieve and cite
- Decision rule: save 50 varied real sources, compare capture and summary with the source, ask known and conflicting questions, test two languages and both browsers, return after two weeks without browsing manually, then export notes, links, tags, and source references
- Evidence limit: research-based; no Digital Macaroni library
