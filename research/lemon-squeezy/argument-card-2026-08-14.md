# Lemon Squeezy private argument card

- Buyer: a small global software or digital-product seller considering a merchant of record
- Job: take payment, handle tax, provision the product, manage renewals and refunds, and receive net revenue predictably
- Thesis: Lemon Squeezy can remove major tax and commerce work, but it should not become the seller's only revenue route until live approval and several payouts prove the operational relationship
- Main reason to buy: one service handles global tax, checkout, subscriptions, licenses, and customer billing
- Main reason to hesitate: store and risk decisions, payout holds, support complaints, high effective fees, and future Stripe uncertainty
- When it is unnecessary: the seller already has direct payment and tax operations that work at lower cost, or cannot tolerate merchant payout control
- Decision rule: secure approval first, run low-value live sales across domestic, international, PayPal, subscription, refund, and failed-payment cases; reconcile webhooks, access, tax, fees, and invoices; wait for several payouts and test one urgent support case before migrating
- Evidence limit: research-based; no hands-on sale, payout, or financial advice
