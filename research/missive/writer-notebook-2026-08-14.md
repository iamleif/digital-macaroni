# Missive writer's notebook

## Research scope

- Research-based review; no product test
- Pricing, billing limits, assignment, sharing, and search documentation checked on 2026-08-14
- G2: 4.7 from 812; Product Hunt: 4.6 from 11
- Main limit: search can behave differently across email providers

## Reader and job

- Primary reader: a support, sales, or agency team where several people answer the same address
- Plain example: a customer writes support@company.com; one person claims the email, asks a coworker a private question inside it, and sends one agreed reply
- Cost of failure: two people reply, nobody replies, or the team cannot find the earlier promise made to the customer

## Product facts

- Shared email stays in one place instead of being forwarded between teammates
- A message can be assigned to one person or a team
- Internal comments and shared drafts sit beside the customer email
- Personal email stays private unless the owner shares it
- Gmail and Outlook search operators are supported; search scope can be changed
- Free allows three users, two shared accounts, and 15 days of visible history
- Annual pricing: Starter $14, Productive $24, Business $36 per user each month
- Productive adds rules, outside integrations, basic reports, and API access

## Experience records

### Two people could otherwise answer the same customer

- Source: repeated G2 and Product Hunt pattern
- Action: assign the email and discuss it through a private comment
- Result: the team can see who owns the answer and what has already been decided
- Consequence: Missive is most useful when inbox responsibility overlaps

### An old email needed to be found

- Source: repeated G2 and Product Hunt complaint
- Result: some customers describe slow or incomplete search, especially with older mail or non-major providers
- Consequence: a team with years of important email should test known old messages before moving daily work

### Mobile state or notifications did not match expectations

- Source: mixed review pattern
- Result: read markers, icons, or notifications caused confusion
- Consequence: every team member should test the phone app, not just the person setting up the account

## Feature translation

### Shared inbox

- What it does: lets several people work from an address such as support@ without forwarding email or sharing a password
- Main limit: the team must agree who claims, closes, and watches each message

### Assignment

- What it does: puts one person's name on an email so everyone knows who should answer

### Internal comment

- What it does: lets coworkers discuss an email privately beside the email itself; the customer cannot see the comment

### Rules

- What it does: sorts or assigns messages automatically based on details such as sender, address, or subject
- Main limit: rules save work only after someone builds and checks them

## Conflict ledger

- Missive is praised for speed and clean design, but search is a repeated weak point.
- The product can make responsibility clear, but poor sharing and notification settings can create new confusion.
- The free plan is useful for a short test, but 15 days of visible history is not enough to judge old-message search.

## Missing evidence
