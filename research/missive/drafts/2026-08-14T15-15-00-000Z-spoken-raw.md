# Missive review: raw draft

Missive is an email app for teams that share the same inbox. If three people answer support@company.com, they can all see the messages without sharing a password or forwarding email to each other.

That alone is not the useful part. Missive lets the team put one person's name on an email, discuss it privately beside the customer conversation, and prepare the reply together. Everyone can see who should answer and what has already been decided.

We think that makes Missive a good choice for support, sales, and agency teams where email work overlaps. It makes less sense when one person handles the inbox alone.

## The main job is stopping email confusion

A shared address often creates two basic problems. Two people answer the same customer, or everyone assumes someone else will answer. Missive deals with both by letting a message be assigned.

The private comments are useful too. A support person can ask an engineer about a problem without forwarding the email or starting a separate chat. The answer remains beside the customer conversation, where the next person can understand it later. The customer does not see those comments.

The public feedback is strong. G2 shows a 4.7 rating from 812 reviews. Assignment, comments, and having several accounts in one app are common reasons people like it.

The important question is whether your team actually shares email work. If everyone has a separate job and rarely touches the same conversation, Missive adds another system without solving much.

## Search needs a real test

Search is the biggest reason we would not tell a team to move immediately. Some customers say finding older messages can be slow or incomplete. The problem appears more often with providers outside Gmail and Outlook, so it should not be treated as the same for every account.

Missive supports normal search filters and the search commands used by Gmail and Outlook. That does not settle whether it will find the messages your team needs.

Before moving daily work, choose three old emails that you know exist. Pick one by sender, one by a phrase in the subject, and one with an attachment. Search for each inside Missive. Do the same with a recent email. If any known message is missing or takes too much work to find, stop there.

This matters more for a business that uses email as its customer record. A team should be able to find the price it quoted, the promise it made, or the file a customer sent months ago.

## The settings take some care

Missive is clean, but it is not just a normal inbox. A team has to decide which accounts are shared, which messages enter a team inbox, who gets notifications, and what closing a message means.

Rules can sort or assign messages automatically. That can save time when the inbox is busy. It also adds setup work. Start with a small number of rules, then check where each message goes before adding more.

Mobile use deserves its own test. Complaints about read markers, icons, and notifications are not the main view, but they come up enough to check. Ask every team member to use the phone app during the trial. One person's clean setup does not prove that everyone receives the right notice or sees the same message state.

## Free is mainly a test plan

The free plan allows three users and two shared accounts, but only 15 days of history stay visible inside Missive. The older email is not deleted and remains with the email provider. Still, this limit makes Free poor for judging long-term search.

With annual billing, Starter is $14 per user each month, Productive is $24, and Business is $36. Monthly billing costs more.

Starter covers the basic shared inbox, conversations, and tasks for teams of up to five. Productive supports up to 50 people and adds outside integrations, automatic rules, basic reports, and API access. Business is aimed at larger teams that need sign-on controls, IP restrictions, advanced reports, and guided onboarding.

For a small team, the real choice is often Starter or Productive. Pay for Productive when automatic sorting or a connection to another tool will be used every week. Do not pay for it because a feature list looks useful.

## Who should use Missive

We recommend Missive when several people regularly answer the same customers and the team is losing track of ownership or context. Assignment and private comments are simple answers to that problem.

We would not recommend it to a solo user just looking for a nicer email app. Much of the price pays for team work. We would also hold off if old email must be found quickly and the search test fails with the team's provider.

Run the trial with one real shared address and 20 real messages. Assign every message, use private comments on a few, prepare one reply together, and check the inbox on every phone. Then search for old email you already know exists.

If the team stops asking who is replying and can still find its history, Missive is doing the job. If not, adding more rules will not fix the basic problem.
