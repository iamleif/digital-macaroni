Featurebase brings customer feedback and customer support into one product. A customer can submit an idea or vote on another request. They can also read the roadmap, check updates, search the help center, or start a support chat.

For a software company, the useful part is not the number of tools in that list. It is the chance to keep one customer story together. A support question can reveal a missing feature. The product team can add that request to its plans. When the change ships, the same customer can receive the update.

That is the reason to consider Featurebase. If your team only needs a public suggestion board, it may be more software than you need. If feedback, product planning, and support are already scattered across several accounts, bringing them together can remove real work.

We recommend Featurebase for teams that will actually use feedback, a public roadmap, and product updates together.

## The complete loop is the product

Featurebase began as a feedback board. That is a page where customers submit ideas and vote on requests that already exist. It now includes roadmaps, a changelog, surveys, a help center, a support inbox, live chat, reports, workflows, and an AI support agent.

A roadmap shows customers what the team is considering or building. A changelog is the list of updates after work ships. Used together, these tools can close a common gap. Customers do not just send ideas into a box. They can see what happened later.

The support inbox makes that connection more useful. An agent can see the customer and the conversation, then link the issue to product feedback. The product team gets more than a vote count. It can see which customers asked, why they care, and how much support work the problem creates.

This connected flow is the strongest positive theme in the customer feedback we reviewed. The interface, setup, and customization also receive frequent praise. Featurebase support is described as quick and hands-on in most of the larger review pools.

You should still test the whole loop. A polished feedback portal is easy to demonstrate. The harder question starts with a messy support chat. Can the agent turn it into a clean request, merge copies, protect private notes, and tell the right people when the change ships?

## AI support needs supervision

Featurebase includes Fibi, an AI agent that answers customers from your help content and other sources. It also offers an AI assistant for human agents. The aim is simple: let the AI handle repeat questions and help people write answers faster.

An AI resolution costs $0.49. Featurebase counts a resolution when the AI handles and closes the support conversation. This can cost much less than having a person answer every basic question. That only works when the answer is right and the customer can reach a human when it is not.

Inaccurate AI answers appear in the negative feedback. That is not surprising. A help center may be outdated, two articles may conflict, or a question may depend on the customer's plan and account. A confident wrong answer can create more work than no answer.

Test Fibi with questions whose correct answers you already know. Include an old article, a refund request, an account-specific problem, an angry message, and a question with missing details. Check what it cites, when it asks for more information, and how the customer reaches a person. Do not judge it from five easy questions copied from the help center.

The same rule applies to automation. A workflow that assigns or closes conversations can save time. It can also hide a ticket in the wrong queue. Run it on a small group before moving all customer messages.

## Support praise comes with a warning

Most formal feedback describes Featurebase's own support positively. People mention fast replies and practical help during setup. That matters for a product that may replace several systems.

There is also a much smaller group with the opposite experience. One detailed account describes a billing and repricing dispute. Another current customer describes stability problems and poor support while looking for a replacement.

Those accounts do not prove that poor service is normal. They do show what to test. Ask a real setup question during the trial. Then ask one question that requires ownership across billing or engineering. Note who follows up and whether the answer survives a second reply.

Bringing several jobs into one product raises the cost of an outage. If a feedback board is unavailable, customers can try later. If the same outage also affects the help center and support inbox, the team loses more of its customer operation at once. Check the status history, export options, and what agents can do if Featurebase is unavailable.

## Featurebase pricing

Featurebase has a free plan for one seat. It includes live chat, unlimited conversations, a shared inbox, ticket assignment, internal notes, the mobile app, and the feedback tools. AI is not included.

With annual billing, Growth costs $29 per full seat each month. It adds Fibi, email support, AI help for agents, reports, integrations, an API, and webhooks. Paying month to month raises the seat price to $37.

Professional costs $59 per seat with annual billing or $75 monthly. It adds stronger automation, round-robin assignment, service targets, customer groups, several languages, and 20 Lite seats. A Lite seat gives a teammate limited access without charging for another full agent.

Enterprise costs $99 per seat annually or $129 monthly. It adds several brands, sign-on and identity controls, custom admin roles, larger business integrations, and 50 Lite seats.

Seat price is only the start of the bill. Fibi costs $0.49 for each AI resolution. Unlimited Copilot use costs $19 per agent each month after the small included allowance. Removing Featurebase branding costs $69 a month. Outbound update emails and automatic translation include monthly amounts, then add usage charges.

This does not make the pricing unfair. It makes a spreadsheet necessary. Count every person who needs the full admin screen. Add a realistic number of AI resolutions, heavy Copilot users, update emails, translation, and branding. Price a quiet month and a busy month. If you are replacing three products, compare total bills and staff work, not just seat prices.

## Who should choose Featurebase?

Featurebase is a good candidate for a growing software team that wants its customer work in one place. Feedback, product updates, help content, and support can all share the same context. It is most attractive when employees spend time copying links or requests between separate tools.

It is less useful for a team that only needs a small suggestion board. It is also a harder choice when the current helpdesk is mature, deeply connected to other systems, and trusted during urgent incidents. Replacing a stable support setup requires more proof than liking a cleaner interface.

Our decision rule is practical. Import a small set of real customers, articles, requests, and conversations. Take five requests from the inbox through duplicate merging, private review, roadmap status, a shipped changelog, and a customer message. Give Fibi hard questions with known answers. Check roles, exports, and the response to a real support question. Then calculate the full bill for a busy month.

Featurebase looks strongest when the complete loop works. Buying it for a list of modules misses the point. The same customer can move from a question to a request and then an update. If the context survives that trip, the suite can replace several awkward handoffs. If that loop breaks, one more all-in-one product will not fix the team's process.
