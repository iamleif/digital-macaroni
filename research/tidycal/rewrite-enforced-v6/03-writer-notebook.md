TidyCal is a scheduling tool that allows users to create booking pages, check connected calendars, accept paid bookings, and support both individual and team scheduling. It launched publicly on August 18, 2021, according to verified records from Product Hunt. The product is categorized under scheduling and has an overall score of 8, with specific areas like onboarding and billing scoring higher than others. The score reflects a balanced view of the product’s capabilities and its shortcomings.

The main positive patterns reported by users include low cost, basic setup, calendar synchronization, and the simplicity of booking links. These features are well-received, especially by those looking for a straightforward scheduling solution. However, reported limits include customization options, team depth, integration reliability, and booking failures. These are areas where users have expressed dissatisfaction or encountered issues.

The pricing structure offers a free plan, a one-time Lifetime plan for individuals at $29, and a more expensive Agency Lifetime plan at $79. The Pro plan is available at $12 monthly or $99 yearly. Paid bookings include a 1% platform fee, which adds to the payment processing fees. These details are verified through official pricing information.

Despite the product's strengths, there are conflicts and unknowns. For instance, the low cost of the lifetime plan does not mitigate the impact of a single bad booking. More calendar connections can simplify availability but also introduce potential sync failures. Team routing can save manual effort only when calendars and priority rules are correctly configured. Importantly, no hands-on testing was conducted, so the product's reliability in real-world scenarios remains uncertain.

In summary, TidyCal is a strong low-cost option for solo calendars and basic team scheduling. Its reliability is a key factor, and it may be worth considering if the user can ensure that no booking conflicts occur. However, the product's limitations in customization and integration reliability should be carefully evaluated before making a decision.
