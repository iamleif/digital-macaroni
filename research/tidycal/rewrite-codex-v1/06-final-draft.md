TidyCal lets customers book open time from a public page while it checks connected calendars, creates the event, and sends the details. The free and lifetime plans make it much cheaper than many schedulers.

That price is useful only when closed time stays closed. I recommend TidyCal for a simple solo calendar after twenty clean test bookings.

## Test the calendar first

Add private, all-day, repeating, tentative, and last-minute events, then decide which ones block time. Create a 30-minute meeting with buffers, one-day notice, and a daily limit.

Open the public page while signed out. Book beside every hold. Add a second calendar, move an event, delete it, and check how fast the booking page changes.

Try the edges of the working day. Book the first open minute and the final one. Add an urgent hold from the calendar app while the public page remains open. Refresh and try that slot from another browser. Then create two bookings at almost the same time. The page should respect the hold, buffers, daily cap, and existing booking without relying on the host to spot a clash.

Place the same event on both calendars. It should create one blocked period. Move it, change its time zone, then remove it. A clean first booking is not enough evidence that sync will remain correct.

## Cross time zones

Book between two real regions while testing daylight-saving weeks, a device with the wrong zone, and working hours around midnight. The page, email, event, reminder, video link, cancellation, and reschedule must show the same moment.

Ask somebody in the other region to book without help. A person should never need to calculate the meeting time by hand. Repeat the test after changing the host's zone.

## Treat each booking type separately

A free call, paid consultation, class, and recurring session need different rules. Check duration, place, notice, buffer, questions, price, seats, and cancellation.

Keep deep qualification outside the scheduler. Test the last seat, a failed payment, and one change inside a recurring series. Required questions should stay required after a reschedule.

Create the confirmation and reminder with the normal sender details. Check Gmail, Outlook, and the company inbox. Old information should not survive after the time or location changes.

## Check links and team routes

Book through each video service the business uses. Reconnect access, reschedule, and cancel. The host and guest should receive one matching link.

For round robin, create hosts with different calendars and priorities. Book ten times, remove a host, and disconnect one calendar. Ownership must stay clear.

Collective meetings need all required hosts free. Block one person and confirm the slot disappears. A team feature saves work only when its rule is easy to explain and repeats correctly.

Change the priority and book again. Add a new employee after the link has already been shared. Remove a former employee without breaking old meetings. Check who receives the notice and who owns each event. Team scheduling becomes expensive when staff must repair assignments by hand.

## Paid bookings add a fee

TidyCal adds a 1% platform fee to the booker's total, alongside processing fees. Pay, fail, reschedule, cancel, and refund a safe test. The calendar, payment account, messages, and final total must agree.

The customer should see the full price before paying. A failed payment must not look like a confirmed meeting. Price the platform and processing fees at the team's real booking volume.

## Plans are cheap for different reasons

Free includes one calendar connection and unlimited booking types. Individual Lifetime was $29 once. It added more calendars, video links, groups, questions, limits, and analytics.

Agency Lifetime was $79 and added team scheduling and more calendars. Pro was $12 monthly or $99 yearly. It reduced branding and added a custom domain and priority support. Confirm today's plan table before buying.

Lifetime access follows the deal terms. It does not promise that every future feature will enter the same tier. Keep the receipt and a copy of the plan table shown at purchase.

## Who should choose TidyCal?

Choose it for straightforward solo scheduling. Consider Agency for a small team whose routing stays simple. Skip it when qualification, routing, or service promises are complex.

Digital Macaroni did not connect a calendar, book a meeting, take payment, test a team route, change a plan, or contact support. Public feedback covers several product versions.

Make twenty real-route bookings. Use every calendar, time zone, meeting type, link, payment state, host, cancellation, and reschedule. Keep TidyCal when blocked time remains blocked and every person receives the correct event.
