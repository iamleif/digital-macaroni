# Akiflow spoken brief — enforced run 5

Most people do not need Akiflow. It costs $228 a year on the cheaper plan, and that is a lot to spend on remembering tasks.

The buyer who might need it is doing more than that. Work keeps turning up in Slack, email, Asana, Todoist, and a couple of calendars. They already spend part of the morning figuring out what is real and what can fit. Akiflow may be worth the fee if it takes over that bit of clerical work.

The Inbox is the useful part. Captured work waits there until somebody decides it belongs in Today or gives it an hour on the calendar. A long task list can pretend everything is urgent. A calendar runs out of room.

All those integrations also make the product vulnerable. If a completed task stays open or a moved date moves back, the one daily plan is no longer the truth. The current changelog shows active work on exactly this sort of plumbing.

The mobile app does not need every desktop control. It needs small changes to stick. Move a task on the phone and it should still be moved when the laptop opens.

Recommend Akiflow to the person who already has this planning habit and can identify the work it will replace. Everybody else can save the money.
