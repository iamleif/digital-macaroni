# Akiflow research brief — enforced run 5

This replacement run uses the excerpt-backed research reverified on 2026-08-18. Digital Macaroni did not use Akiflow, connect accounts, or perform a sync test. The article keeps its original publication date of 2026-08-14.

Akiflow is being judged as a planning layer for somebody whose actionable work arrives through several systems. The buyer already plans by time and wants to stop rebuilding the day from Slack, email, project tools, task managers, and calendars.

The official evidence confirms a seven-day trial, a $34 monthly price, a $19 monthly equivalent billed annually, the Inbox/Today distinction, broad integrations, and a capable mobile companion. The July 2026 changelog also records a large amount of recent repair work involving calendars, Gmail, Outlook, Todoist, Asana, ClickUp, Linear, mobile background sync, recurring tasks, and time zones.

Trustpilot's current summary supports the consolidation and daily-planning value while retaining complaints about billing, onboarding, and calendar sync. Two Reddit accounts sharpen the tradeoff: one praises time blocking but struggles to justify the price; another describes severe failures involving time slots, task movement, AI behavior, and support. The severe account is an anecdote, not a prevalence claim.

The recommendation remains narrow. Akiflow can be worth paying for when it removes a real daily reconciliation chore. People with one task source, a checklist habit, or little interest in time blocking should skip it.
