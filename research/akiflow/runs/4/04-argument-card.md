# Akiflow argument card — enforced run 4

- Buyer: a professional whose actionable work arrives through several apps and who already plans most days on a calendar.
- Job: replace the daily act of collecting scattered obligations and fitting them around meetings.
- Thesis: Akiflow is worth considering only when it becomes the trusted planning layer above multiple work systems; otherwise it is an expensive calendar wrapper.
- Main reason to buy: the Inbox-to-calendar workflow turns scattered requests into explicit time commitments.
- Main reason to hesitate: Akiflow borrows reliability from every connected system, and its changelog shows how much sync behavior needs continual repair.
- Price judgment: $228 annually is defensible as payment for removing a daily reconciliation chore, not as payment for a prettier task list.
- Buyer boundary: skip it when tasks already live in one system, when a checklist is enough, or when daily time blocking feels like maintenance rather than relief.
- Evidence limit: research-based; no Digital Macaroni hands-on test.
- Decision rule: before paying annually, the two highest-volume connections must preserve capture, completion, deletion, and date changes in both directions for several ordinary workdays.
