# Akiflow writer notebook — enforced run 4

## The useful opinion

Akiflow is not a better place to store tasks. It is a commitment device for people whose tasks already escape from several places. That makes it unusually useful to one narrow buyer and needlessly expensive to everyone else.

## The opening

Lead with the exclusion. Most people should not pay $228 a year for Akiflow. The people who should consider it can name the daily reconciliation chore it replaces: opening Slack, email, a project tool, and a task manager before rebuilding the day on a calendar.

## The central tension

Every integration increases potential value and potential fragility. If Slack capture, Todoist completion, or a calendar change goes stale, Akiflow stops being the trusted layer and becomes one more place to check.

## Reader-facing consequence

The buyer does not need a feature tour. They need a threshold:

- Work arrives from at least two important sources.
- They plan by time most workdays.
- They will use the Inbox/Today distinction instead of treating both as another backlog.
- The annual price replaces a recurring planning chore, not merely an app they like looking at.

If those conditions are absent, recommend a simpler task manager or the calendar they already use.

## Evidence language

Say "Akiflow's documentation shows," "the changelog records," or "reviewers repeatedly describe" where attribution matters. Do not narrate the research process. Do not say "we found in testing," "we connected," or "we moved tasks." Proposed reliability checks must be labeled as the buyer's acceptance test.

## Concrete scene

A customer request is saved from Slack. A follow-up lives in Gmail. A project deadline sits in Asana. Akiflow can bring all three into one Inbox, but the useful moment is not capture. It is deciding that only two fit beside today's meetings and putting them into actual hours.

## Language to keep

- expensive calendar wrapper
- the daily reconciliation chore
- a trusted planning layer
- borrowed reliability
- the annual price must replace work
- a calendar that says no

## Language to avoid

- powerful, robust, seamless, game-changing
- "users report" repeated as a paragraph opening
- rigid feature-by-feature sections
- repeated commands to run a trial after every criticism
- calling an isolated failure a normal experience
