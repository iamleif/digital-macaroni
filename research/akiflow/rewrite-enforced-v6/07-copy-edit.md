Akiflow is a productivity tool designed to help professionals manage multiple task and calendar apps by bringing tasks from various sources into a single inbox and scheduling them on a calendar. Its main appeal lies in its ability to import tasks from email, messaging, project, and task apps, then place them directly on a calendar. This feature helps users with time blocking and daily planning, making it a good fit for those who rely heavily on calendar-based workflows and need to manage tasks from several apps. However, the app faces challenges with reliability and usability, which can affect its practicality in daily use. Some users report recurring sync issues and a clunky mobile experience, which can disrupt the workflow for those who depend on the app to keep their tasks and calendar in sync. Before paying, a user should consider whether they need calendar-based planning and are willing to deal with potential technical hurdles. They should also evaluate whether the cost is justified for their specific needs.

Akiflow's strongest asset is its ability to combine tasks and calendars for daily time blocking. This feature is particularly useful for professionals who need to manage multiple projects and deadlines without switching between apps. By importing tasks from various sources into one inbox, the app helps users create a clear view of their schedule and allocate time effectively. The reported pattern of users finding this feature beneficial suggests that it addresses a real need for those who plan work around a calendar. However, the effectiveness of this feature depends on how well the app manages the import process and how smoothly it integrates with different platforms. Recent official updates have included fixes for calendar sync, Gmail settings, and connections to Asana, ClickUp, Microsoft To Do, and Todoist, which shows the development team is actively addressing integration issues. While these improvements are welcome, they do not fully resolve the ongoing sync problems that some users still report.

## The reliability of Akiflow is a major concern

Despite active updates and fixes for calendar sync, Gmail settings, and app integrations, some users continue to report recurring sync issues. These problems can disrupt the workflow, especially for those who rely on the app to keep their tasks and calendar in sync. The mobile experience is another area where the app falls short. Although recent updates have addressed some mobile gaps, older complaints about a clunky mobile app and poor integration setup remain. This suggests that while the app has improved over time, the mobile version may still lag behind the desktop experience, making it less practical for users who need to access their tasks on the go.

## Akiflow's pricing model is straightforward but potentially expensive for a personal planner

The Pro plan costs $34 monthly or $19 per month billed yearly after a seven-day trial. While this pricing is clear, it may not be cost-effective for users who don't need the full range of features. The single plan structure makes it easy to understand, but the price point could be a barrier for some users, especially those looking for a more affordable alternative. The value proposition depends on how much users rely on the app's calendar-based planning and task consolidation features. For those who need these functions regularly, the cost may be justified. However, for users who only use the app occasionally, the price might not be worth it.

## Support for Akiflow is generally praised, though it was not tested directly

Users on platforms like G2 and Trustpilot have reported that support is responsive and helpful, which is an important factor for any productivity tool. However, the lack of hands-on testing means that the quality of support remains uncertain. While this is a positive sign, it also means that users should be prepared for the possibility that support may not always meet their expectations. The app's official changelog and recent updates suggest that the development team is actively addressing issues, which is a good indication that support is a priority for the company.

For users who need calendar-based planning and have tasks coming from multiple sources, Akiflow can be a valuable tool. Its ability to consolidate tasks and schedule them on a calendar helps with time blocking and daily planning. However, the app's reliability and usability issues, particularly with the mobile experience, are significant drawbacks. The cost may also be a factor for some users, especially those looking for a more affordable option. Before committing to Akiflow, it's important to consider whether the app's strengths align with your specific needs and whether you're willing to deal with the potential technical hurdles. If you're someone who heavily relies on calendar-based planning and needs tasks from various apps consolidated into one place, Akiflow may be worth the investment. Otherwise, there may be better alternatives available that offer a more seamless experience.
