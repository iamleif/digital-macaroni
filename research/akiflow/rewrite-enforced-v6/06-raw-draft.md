Akiflow is a productivity tool that helps professionals manage multiple task and calendar apps by bringing tasks from various sources into a single inbox and scheduling them on a calendar. Its main appeal is its ability to import tasks and place them directly on a calendar, which helps with time blocking and daily planning. For users who rely heavily on calendar-based planning and need to manage tasks from several apps, Akiflow can be a good fit. However, it faces challenges with reliability and usability. Some users report recurring sync issues and a clunky mobile experience, which can affect its practicality in daily use. Akiflow's strongest asset is its ability to combine tasks and calendars for daily time blocking. This feature is particularly useful for professionals who need to manage multiple projects and deadlines without switching between apps. The app imports tasks from email, messaging, project, and task apps into one inbox, then lets the user place them on a calendar. This consolidation helps users create a clear view of their schedule and allocate time effectively. The reported pattern of users finding this feature beneficial suggests that it addresses a real need for those who plan work around a calendar. However, the effectiveness of this feature depends on how well the app manages the import process and how smoothly it integrates with different platforms.

## The reliability of Akiflow is a major concern

While the app has been actively updated with fixes for calendar sync, Gmail settings, and connections to Asana, ClickUp, Microsoft To Do, and Todoist, some users still report recurring sync issues. These problems can disrupt the workflow, especially for those who rely on the app to keep their tasks and calendar in sync. The mobile experience is another area where the app falls short. Although recent updates have addressed some mobile gaps, older complaints about a clunky mobile app and poor integration setup remain. This suggests that while the app has improved over time, the mobile version may still lag behind the desktop experience, making it less practical for users who need to access their tasks on the go.

## Akiflow's pricing model is straightforward but potentially expensive for a personal planner

The Pro plan costs $34 monthly or $19 per month billed yearly after a seven-day trial. While this pricing is clear, it may not be cost-effective for users who don't need the full range of features. The single plan structure makes it easy to understand, but the price point could be a barrier for some users, especially those who are looking for a more affordable alternative. The value proposition depends on how much users rely on the app's calendar-based planning and task consolidation features. For those who need these functions regularly, the cost may be justified. However, for users who only use the app occasionally, the price might not be worth it.

## Support for Akiflow is generally praised, though it was not tested directly

Users on platforms like G2 and Trustpilot have reported that support is responsive and helpful, which is an important factor for any productivity tool. However, the lack of hands-on testing means that the quality of support remains uncertain. While this is a positive sign, it also means that users should be prepared for the possibility that support may not always meet their expectations. The app's official changelog and recent updates suggest that the development team is actively addressing issues, which is a good indication that support is a priority for the company.
