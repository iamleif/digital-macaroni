Akiflow is worth trying if your work arrives in several apps and you already plan most days on a calendar. It pulls tasks into one inbox and lets you place the important ones beside meetings. That can replace a daily round of checking email, chat, and project tools.

The product is much harder to justify as a normal task list. Akiflow cost $34 a month or $19 per month with annual billing when we checked its pricing. A person who makes a weekly list can get what they need for much less.

The trial should answer two questions. Does Akiflow make the daily plan faster, and do its connections keep every task correct? The first question is why people like the product. The second is where the risk sits.

## A calendar makes the workload honest

A task list does not care how many hours are available. It can hold 40 tasks beside a day filled with meetings. Akiflow makes you choose which work deserves a real block on the calendar.

Suppose a proposal needs an hour. You can place it from 10:00 to 11:00 and see what it does to the rest of the day. If new meetings appear, unfinished tasks need to move. The work was always competing for the same time. Akiflow simply makes that conflict harder to ignore.

This works best for someone who already likes daily planning. The inbox collects work first, then the calendar becomes the place where the plan is made. Keyboard controls can make capture and rescheduling quick on a desktop. These small savings matter because the same actions happen throughout the day.

Akiflow will not decide which request matters. It can also become another full inbox when every saved message and assigned task comes through. The user still needs to clear the noise and make choices.

## Connections save copying when they stay correct

Akiflow can pull work from email, messaging apps, and task tools. The original item remains in its source while Akiflow gives it a place in the daily plan. This can save a lot of copying for someone who works from several systems.

It also means the product depends on connections it does not fully control. Sync bugs and missing integrations recur as complaints. Akiflow's changelog included fixes for calendar updates, Gmail settings, Asana, ClickUp, Microsoft To Do, and Todoist. The fixes show active maintenance, while also giving buyers a good reason to test their own setup.

Connect the two sources that create the most work. Complete some tasks in Akiflow and check the source app. Complete other tasks in the source and check Akiflow. Change dates and delete test items. A stale task or a duplicate is easy to catch in a short trial and difficult to live with for months.

Import filters matter too. If Akiflow collects every message and issue, planning becomes cleanup. The useful setup brings in work that needs time on the calendar and leaves background material where it started.

## The phone must keep the plan current

Akiflow's mobile app can capture tasks, show calendar views, move tasks into time slots, create events, and run timers. Widgets and the phone's share menu can also help with quick capture.

Older complaints describe a weaker mobile experience. The app has changed since some of those accounts were written, so they cannot describe every current use. Mobile gaps still deserve attention because a daily plan changes away from a desk.

The phone does not need every desktop control. It needs to save a new task, move unfinished work, mark a task complete, and show the correct next meeting. Make those changes on the phone, then check the desktop and the source app. If the three views disagree, the promise of one current plan falls apart.

## The price rules out casual use

Akiflow offered a seven-day trial before its Pro subscription. The plan was $34 month to month or $19 per month with annual billing. That is a high price for personal planning.

The price can work for someone who repeats the same planning routine every morning. Saving time on capture, copying, and rescheduling has value when it happens five days a week. A person with one task list and a few meetings will not get the same return.

Use the trial for normal work. Connect two important sources and plan three real days. Let a meeting move and leave some work unfinished. Try the same small changes from the phone. A perfect demo day will reveal less than an ordinary Tuesday.

We recommend Akiflow for people who live by their calendars and need to collect tasks from several places. We would skip it when one task app already holds everything, or when daily time blocking feels like extra work.

The decision comes down to trust and repetition. If the connections stay correct and Akiflow saves time every day, the subscription may be reasonable. If either part fails, it becomes an expensive second calendar.
