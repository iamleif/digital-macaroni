# Freshsales review: strong value until sales work gets complicated

Freshsales is a CRM that keeps sales contacts, companies, deals, email, calls, tasks, and reports in one place. A seller can open a lead, see the conversation, make a call, move the deal, and set the next task without copying the update into another system.

For a small team with a normal sales process, that is a useful package at a low starting price. The interface and standard pipeline work receive strong praise across a very large review sample.

The problem begins when the company judges the product from the easy pipeline board. Custom reports, advanced automation, permissions, mobile work, and higher-plan features create most of the complaints. We would build the Monday sales report before spending time making the demo pipeline look perfect.

## Freshsales is good at normal sales work

A CRM should answer a few basic questions. Who is the customer? Who owns the next step? What happened last? Where is the deal? What might close this month?

Freshsales puts contact and company records beside the deal pipeline. The pipeline can be shown as a Kanban board, which means each deal appears as a card under a stage. Sellers move the card from New to Qualified, Proposal, Won, or whatever stages the team uses.

Email templates and account sync help keep messages with the record. The built-in phone and chat tools can reduce the gap between a call and the follow-up task. Basic workflows can assign work or update a field after something happens.

This combination is the main reason people like Freshsales. The CRM can replace a separate contact list, manual pipeline sheet, and some of the copying between phone and inbox. The normal flow is easier to approach than several larger CRM suites.

That does not mean setup is automatic. The stages still need clear definitions. The team needs to decide when a lead becomes a contact, how duplicates are handled, and which activity counts toward the forecast. A friendly screen will not settle those rules.

## Start with a dirty import

Do not trial Freshsales with ten perfect sample contacts. Import a copy of real data.

Include duplicate people, two contacts at one company, old owners, blank email addresses, deals without a value, and customers with several currencies or regions. Run the duplicate tools and inspect every merge. Confirm that notes, emails, tasks, and deal links remain on the right record.

Next, connect two test inboxes. Send from the CRM and reply from outside it. Forward a message. Add another recipient. Check which record receives the thread and what teammates can see.

Place and receive a test call if the phone feature matters. Miss a call, leave a voicemail, and call from mobile. Make sure the activity appears where the seller expects and creates the right next task.

An import can finish without being usable. The real question is whether a seller can open one customer and trust the full history.

## Reports are the most important trial

Freshsales can be easy for a seller and frustrating for the manager who needs a number.

Custom reporting is the clearest repeated weakness in the customer feedback. Some people find the setup confusing. A recent long-term account describes broken custom reports and poor support follow-through. That is one account, not the normal result across more than a thousand reviews. It still describes a high-impact failure.

Take the report used in the real Monday meeting. Build it with the same date rules, owners, stages, currencies, and closed-deal logic. Change an old deal after the period closes. Reopen a lost deal. Move a deal between owners. Check whether the totals behave as expected.

If the team forecasts, define what the forecast means. Freshsales calls some features deal insights, contact scoring, and forecasting insights. Contact scoring is a number used to rank which lead looks most ready to buy. A forecast estimates future sales. Neither is useful until the inputs match the team's process.

Ask a manager to rebuild the report without the trial owner beside them. If that person needs a spreadsheet export every week, include that manual work in the product decision.

## Automation should fail safely

A sales sequence is a timed set of emails and tasks used to follow up with a lead. A workflow changes something after a trigger, such as assigning a lead or creating a task.

Both can save time. Both can send the wrong message or hide a deal if the conditions are wrong.

Build one sequence with addresses you control. Reply after the first message and confirm the later steps stop. Bounce another address. Change the contact owner halfway through. Unsubscribe and confirm that future messages do not send.

For a workflow, test missing fields and unexpected values. Make two rules fire from the same change. Turn one rule off and back on. Check what appears in the history so an admin can explain why the record moved.

Do this before adding AI. Freddy tools may help rank, summarize, or answer, but AI cannot repair an unclear sales process. The current Freddy AI Agent add-on costs $49 per 100 customer sessions. Paid accounts receive 500 one-time sessions to try it. Estimate use after testing answer quality and human handoff, not from the free allowance.

## Freshsales pricing

Growth costs $9 per user each month when billed annually. It includes the pipeline board, contact stages, chat, email, phone, templates, custom fields, and basic workflows.

Pro costs $39 per user each month with annual billing. It adds contact scoring, deal insights, custom sales activities, territory management, and sales sequences. A territory is a rule that assigns customers or deals to a sales group, often by location, industry, or account type.

Enterprise costs $59 per user each month with annual billing. It adds forecast insights, permissions for individual fields, custom record modules, audit logs, and a sandbox. A sandbox is a safe copy where an admin can test changes before they affect live sales data.

The jump from Growth to Pro is large. A five-person team moves from $540 a year to $2,340. Enterprise would cost $3,540. Growth is cheap only if the team can stay on Growth.

The current official page does not list the old permanent free plan found in some older material. It offers a 21-day trial. The account is suspended if the team does not choose a paid plan afterward.

Freshworks also lists paid document and AI add-ons. Confirm every required feature and charge in a quote before buying, especially when phone usage, AI sessions, documents, or another Freshworks product is involved.

## Mobile and support need real tests

Freshsales has mobile apps, but feedback on the mobile experience is mixed. Some sellers find it useful. Others say it is slower or does not match desktop depth.

Complete a real after-meeting routine from the phone. Find the contact, write a note, change the stage, add a task, scan or attach what matters, and check the manager's report. Do not accept a mobile app as proof that a field seller can finish the job.

Support also receives mixed feedback when a problem involves a custom report or advanced workflow. Ask one normal setup question during the trial, then one question that requires the support person to reproduce a broken result. Note whether one person owns it through the answer.

## Who should choose Freshsales?

Freshsales suits a small or growing team with a familiar lead and deal pipeline. It is strongest when built-in email, phone, and automation replace manual updates across several tools.

It is a harder choice for a company with specialized quoting, complex revenue rules, many layers of permissions, or reports that already stretch a general CRM. Those needs may fit Enterprise, another Freshworks package, or a different product.

Our decision rule is simple. Build the Monday sales report first. Import dirty records and merge duplicates. Sync two inboxes, make and miss a call, run a sequence, break a workflow, and complete the full update on mobile. Then map every required feature to Growth, Pro, or Enterprise.

Freshsales can be good value. The low headline price is not the verdict. The verdict is whether the real report stays correct and the team can recover when custom work goes wrong.
