# Jitter writer's notebook

## Research scope

- Research-based review; no Digital Macaroni product test
- Current product and pricing pages checked on 2026-08-14
- Product Hunt shows 4.9 from 48 reviews; other scored samples are thin
- Numeric paid prices were not exposed in the current pricing-page response, so the review does not guess them

## Reader and job

- Primary reader: a designer or marketer with a static design that needs simple movement
- Plain explanation: make text, logos, charts, or app screens move, then save the result as a video, GIF, or web animation
- Cost of failure: paying for an advanced plan when a template or the free plan would have handled the job

## Product facts

- Runs in a browser and imports layered Figma designs
- Includes more than 300 templates, presets, custom timing, audio, video, and collaboration
- Free includes unlimited drafts, three workspace files, and 720p 30fps export with a watermark
- Pro removes the watermark and raises export to 1080p 60fps
- Max adds 4K 120fps, transparent video, and frame-by-frame export
- Ultra mainly adds more AI use
- Annual billing advertises a 15% saving

## Experience records

### Figma design moved without rebuilding it

- Source: Product Hunt and Reddit
- Result: designers brought layers from Figma into Jitter and animated them with familiar controls
- Consequence: this is the clearest reason to use Jitter instead of a full video editor

### Presets sped up simple animation

- Source: Product Hunt and Reddit
- Result: text and interface movement could be created without learning a deep professional timeline
- Consequence: useful for social posts, product demos, and portfolio pieces

### Finished work still needed judgment

- Source: Motion Design community critique
- Result: output made in Jitter was judged on pacing, sound, and editing rather than the software used
- Consequence: an easy tool does not decide when movement should start, stop, or support the message

## Repeated patterns

- Fast Figma handoff is the strongest practical benefit
- Templates and presets make basic motion approachable
- The free plan is useful for learning but the watermark blocks most client work
- Jitter sits between static design tools and deep motion software

## Feature translation

### Figma import

- What it does: carries separate design layers into Jitter so they can move on their own
- Who cares: people whose logos, app screens, or social designs already live in Figma
- Main limit: poor layer organization can still require cleanup

### Lottie export

- What it does: saves a small animation file that a website or app can play
- Who cares: product and web teams
- Main limit: it is not the same thing as a normal video and needs developer support

### Transparent export

- What it does: saves motion without a solid background so an editor can place it over other footage
- Who cares: professional video workflows
- Main limit: requires Max or above

## Conflict ledger

- Jitter is easier than After Effects for basic movement, but ease does not make it equally capable.
- Current Figma motion tools may remove a handoff for tiny jobs, while Jitter still offers more presets and text control.
