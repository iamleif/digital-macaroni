# Voicenotes writer's notebook

## Research scope

- Research-based review; no product test
- Sources refreshed on 2026-08-14 from the Supabase packet, current pricing guide, App Store, and community discussions
- The App Store displayed 6.6K ratings at 4.8, but detailed recent reviews include serious recording failures
- Main limit: Voicenotes says newer versions save recordings locally before processing, but we did not test whether this fixed the reported losses

## Reader and job

- Primary reader: someone who wants to capture ideas or conversations by speaking, then find and reuse them later
- What they use now: phone recorder, scattered voice memos, or written notes
- Cost of failure: a forgotten idea for casual capture; an unrecoverable conversation for an important meeting

## Product facts

- Records memos, meetings, and dictation, then creates transcripts and summaries
- Lets the user search notes, ask questions about them, and turn them into other writing
- Free plan: 100 minutes of transcripts and summaries each week, unlimited raw recordings, and 30 days of visible history
- Pro: $9 per user each month with unlimited transcripts, summaries, dictation, history, imports, storage, and added connections to other tools

## Experience records

### Long meeting recordings disappeared

- Source: App Store
- Context: iPhone, Mac, and Apple Watch user testing the app for two weeks
- Event: a paused 54-minute note vanished; a separate two-hour Mac meeting disappeared after recording stopped; Watch recordings also stopped
- Vendor response: said later versions improved saving and handling
- Consequence: do not use Voicenotes as the only recorder for an important conversation until it proves reliable on the exact device

### Field recording stopped without warning

- Source: App Store
- Context: landscape architect recording client conversations while walking a property
- Result: office and Zoom use worked better, but mobile field recordings stopped and left only short audio files
- Consequence: a controlled room and a moving field job are different tests

### Organization and exports became work

- Source: App Store
- Context: frequent recorder using meetings, summaries, and exports
- Result: notes piled up, summaries stalled, files exported one at a time in mixed formats, and transcript timestamps were inconsistent
- Consequence: capture is easy, but a large note library still needs dependable organization and export

### Early enthusiastic user

- Source: App Store
- Context: used iPhone, browser, and other devices
- Result: praised dictation, related-note suggestions, stored audio, and cross-device access; also wanted better organization and clearer separation between answers from personal notes and outside AI knowledge
- Consequence: the core idea has been useful since early releases, while trust and organization remain the harder jobs

## Repeated patterns

- Quick spoken capture is the strongest reason to use Voicenotes
- Search, summaries, and reuse are useful after the recording survives processing
- Lost or cut-off recordings are not the usual rating, but they are the failure that matters most
- The product is easier to recommend for thoughts and reminders than for interviews, legal records, or important meetings

## Feature translation

### AI voice notes

- What it does: record a thought and receive text that can be searched later
- Who cares: people who think faster than they type or capture ideas while walking
- When it does not matter: the user already keeps short written notes without friction

### Ask your notes

- What it does: search old recordings by asking a question instead of remembering the file name
- Main limit: the answer is only useful when the source recording and transcript are complete

### Meeting summaries

- What it does: turns a long conversation into a shorter account and list of actions
- Main limit: it cannot repair a recording that stopped or disappeared

## Conflict ledger

- A 4.8 App Store rating can coexist with serious recording-loss accounts because most captures may work while a small number of failures carry a high cost.
- Voicenotes can be excellent for everyday ideas and still be unsuitable as the only record of an important meeting.
- Recent saving changes may have improved reliability, but the refreshed research does not prove the older problem is gone.

## Missing evidence

- No hands-on recording test across phone, Mac, Watch, and field conditions
- No proof that the latest local-save change prevents every reported failure
- No broad sample showing how often summaries stall or transcripts invent content

## Spoken language bank

- tap record before the thought disappears
- find an old idea without remembering its title
- a note you can search and reuse
- keep a second recorder for anything you cannot repeat
- easy capture does not matter if the recording disappears
