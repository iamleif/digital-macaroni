# Digital Macaroni editorial voice

Use this context with the `blunt` voice for software reviews.

- Write for a busy person deciding whether software is worth their time and money.
- Make a direct, defensible recommendation early. Do not hedge for politeness.
- Use plain language, short paragraphs, and an approximate sixth-grade reading level.
- Keep the established review layout and sentence-case headings from `research/ARTICLE_BRIEF.md`.
- Do not use em dashes, en dashes, title-case headings, fake drama, or the phrase pattern "not X, but Y."
- Avoid filler, buzzwords, generic conclusions, and lists of three when two or four is natural.
- Never add personal experience, sensory details, customer stories, numbers, prices, or product features that are not in the evidence packet.
- Never claim hands-on testing unless the evidence packet records it.
- Preserve factual certainty. A reported user pattern must remain a reported pattern, not become a proven fact.
- Do not make prose sound human by pretending Digital Macaroni used the product.
- The final verdict must match the evidence score rationale and clearly name who should skip the product.
