I build voice AI products. I am impressed by the technology almost every day. I also tense up when a synthetic voice answers a customer service line and begins with an overlong greeting.

That is not a contradiction. Building with a technology makes its possibilities clearer, but it also makes the avoidable mistakes harder to ignore.

## Latency is not a technical detail

Human conversation is full of tiny timing decisions. We overlap, pause, acknowledge and adjust before a sentence is complete. A voice agent that waits too long feels broken. One that responds too quickly feels like it was not listening. One that interrupts at the wrong moment can turn a simple task into a fight for the floor.

Teams talk about latency in milliseconds because that is how systems are measured. Customers experience it as social behavior. The pause changes whether the agent feels attentive, confused or impatient.

This means performance work is conversation design. Reducing a delay does more than improve a metric; it changes the perceived competence of the service.

## Stop auditioning for personhood

Many voice agents spend too much effort pretending to be human. They add filler words, invented warmth and little verbal hesitations. The result is often more unsettling than direct synthetic speech because the customer begins evaluating the performance instead of completing the task.

An agent does not need a fictional childhood or a suspiciously cheerful personality. It needs to be clear about what it can do, listen well and move the conversation forward.

<Callout>
The goal is not to pass as human. The goal is to be a good participant in a human interaction.
</Callout>

## Give the agent something useful to do

A natural voice cannot rescue an agent with no authority. If it can only repeat website copy and offer to take a message, the experience will feel thin regardless of the model behind it.

Useful agents can inspect relevant context, complete bounded actions and recognize when the request requires judgment they do not have. That last ability matters. Confidence without authority is one of the fastest ways to lose trust.

The strongest voice AI experiences are narrow enough to be dependable. They know the job, the available actions and the edge of their competence. They can explain what happens next without turning the handoff into a surprise.

## The bar is the service, not the demo

Voice AI demos are persuasive because a few good turns feel like magic. Real service is less cooperative. People call from noisy streets. They change their minds halfway through. They use names the transcription system has never heard. They arrive angry about something that happened before the call began.

The work is not finished when the agent can have a fluent conversation. It is finished when the entire system can handle the ordinary mess of a customer's day.

That is why I still dislike talking to most AI agents. The voice is ready for the future. Too often, the service behind it is not.
