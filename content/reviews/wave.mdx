Wave puts accounting, invoices, and payments in one service. It is made for freelancers and very small businesses. Simple books and invoice follow-up are the main reasons to consider it.

The payment route carries a much larger risk. Detailed public accounts describe money being held or returned, accounts being closed, and slow explanations from support.

I recommend Wave for basic books and invoices after a careful trial. Do not make it the only way customers can pay when delayed funds would harm the business.

## Move one month of real records

Import a safe month of bank transactions. Sort income, costs, fees, refunds, and owner transfers. Reconcile the final bank balance and compare the profit report with trusted records.

Create a client, product, tax rate, and expense. Correct one mistake. Check whether the change is clear in the audit trail, then give the books to the accountant or tax adviser.

Basic accounting is useful only when the records can survive review. Stop the trial if the owner cannot explain why the bank, invoice list, and profit report differ.

## Invoice follow-up is the strongest everyday job

Create a real invoice with line items, tax, due date, and a supporting file. Send it to a safe address. Check the sender, subject, link, phone page, and printed copy.

Set a reminder and make a recurring version. Mark one payment outside Wave and confirm that the balance changes once. Cancel and correct an invoice without hiding the original record.

One official customer account described years of recurring invoices and easy follow-up. That is a credible use case, but it does not prove the payment service is safe for every business.

## Start payments with small amounts

Process a low-value card payment and bank transfer if both routes matter. Check the amount shown to the customer and the fee. Match the arrival date, bank deposit, invoice state, receipt, and book entry.

Fail a safe payment, issue a refund, and handle a dispute in a test or low-risk setting. Keep another processor ready while the business learns how Wave treats its type of customer and transaction.

Do not move a large client payment merely to finish the trial. Increase value and frequency slowly, and keep enough cash outside the service to cover a delay.

## Payment holds change the recommendation

One detailed account described a $4,500 payment being returned. It said $2,000 was held for 60 days after the account was called high risk. Another account described $15,000 held during a check with no clear release date.

Those accounts cannot show how often holds occur. Their impact is still serious. A small business may miss payroll, tax, or supplier payments while waiting for its own money.

Ask which documents Wave may request, how risk reviews work, and what happens to pending payments after an account closes. Save customer and payment records outside Wave so the team can explain each transaction quickly.

## Support needs a payment question

Send support a real question during the trial. Ask about a failed payment, hold, refund, or account review. Judge the time to a useful human answer, not the speed of the first automated reply.

Write down the escalation route and the information support needs. A payment tool should tell the owner which transaction is affected and what can be done next.

If no one can give a clear route before the account holds important funds, use Wave for accounting and invoice creation while another provider handles payment.

## Export before depending on it

Export transactions, contacts, invoices, payments, receipts, and reports. Open the files outside Wave. Check whether dates, taxes, fees, invoice links, and attached files remain useful.

Give the export to another bookkeeping tool or accountant. Rebuild one invoice and reconcile the sample month. Record how to leave before several years of records collect in the service.

A low monthly cost does not make locked data harmless. The business needs a copy of the books and a second route to its customers.

## Who should choose Wave?

Choose Wave for a freelancer or micro-business that needs basic books and invoices and can test payments slowly. It is safer when another payment option exists.

Skip Wave as the main processor for frequent or high-value payments when access to cash cannot wait. A dedicated payment service with a proven support route is the better choice.

Digital Macaroni did not import records, send invoices, process payments, inspect billing, export data, or contact support. Some large review sources were inaccessible, and the complaint-heavy sample may overstate failure frequency.

Keep Wave when the books reconcile, invoice follow-up works, and small payments clear with useful support. Keep payment risk away from the part of the business that must stay liquid.
