Ghost is publishing software for a writer, newsroom, or member business that wants its own site and audience. It combines articles, emails, member records, paid plans, reader groups, themes, and built-in stats.

The product has a clear view: the publisher should control the brand and member link. Ghost is open source and run by a nonprofit that puts revenue back into the project. Outside evidence was too thin to judge theme work, support, or email delivery broadly.

We recommend Ghost for a publication that wants a direct reader business and can own its site setup. Ghost(Pro) is the safer start for teams that do not want to run servers.

## Publishing and newsletters share one audience

A writer can post an article on the site and send it as an email from the same editor. Members stay in one reader record rather than being copied between the site and an email service.

That can reduce broken tags and duplicate lists. It matters most when the publication sends regularly and wants free and paid readers on the same site.

<Callout>
Ghost is strongest when the website, newsletter, and member record form one reader relationship.
</Callout>

Create a free member, paid member, and unsubscribed reader during the trial. Publish one article to each intended group. Check the site, inbox, member page, and opt-out.

Reader groups can send different work to different people. Keep the rules simple enough to explain. A group built from weak data can hide a useful article from people who paid for it.

## Paid memberships need a full money test

Ghost supports monthly and yearly paid plans. A publication can offer member-only posts without giving a large outside network control of its readers.

Complete a low-price test payment. Check receipt, access, renewal, failed payment, cancellation, refund, and export. Learn which payment fees and tax duties remain with the publisher.

The value is ownership, not sure income. Ghost can provide the paywall and member record. It cannot create demand or decide the right offer.

Export member data and keep consent records. A direct audience is only truly owned when the publication can take a safe copy elsewhere.

## Theme control creates design and update work

Ghost themes give a publication broad control over brand and layout. A ready-made theme can launch fast. A custom theme may need a web developer.

Test article pages, tags, author pages, search, member forms, payment, phone screens, and accessibility. Check what happens to custom changes after a Ghost update.

Keep theme code in source control and use a test site. A design change should not break sign-up or lock paid readers out of content.

The packet did not contain enough real user evidence to rate theme difficulty for different teams. Price design and maintenance work alongside the subscription.

## Hosted and self-hosted Ghost serve different buyers

Ghost(Pro) handles hosting, updates, email systems, and much of the daily tech work. Self-hosting offers more control and can fit a tech publisher with a clear reason to own the servers.

Self-hosting means owning security fixes, backups, email delivery, storage, checks, and recovery. Run a restore before launch. A publishing business cannot treat its member site as a side server.

The foundation publishes money and team details, which supports the product's open story. It does not remove the need to check current plans, support, and terms.

The evidence packet lacked enough current pricing detail for an exact example. Price members, staff, email volume, theme work, payment fees, support, and hosting operations.

## Verdict

Ghost offers a clear home for independent publishing. Articles, emails, members, paid access, stats, and brand control serve the same reader business.

The main choice is operational. Hosted Ghost costs money but removes server work, while self-hosting gives control and responsibility.

Publish one issue, send it to free and paid test readers, complete a payment, and export the audience. Keep Ghost when the whole reader path works and the hosting choice has a clear owner. Skip self-hosting when the team cannot maintain delivery and recovery.
