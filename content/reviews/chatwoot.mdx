Chatwoot is an open-source support inbox. It brings web chat, email, social messages, and other channels together. It also includes a branded chat box, help center, AI replies, and a self-hosted option.

The product has a strong shape for teams that want more control over customer data. The evidence comes too much from the vendor to support firm claims about setup, help, AI quality, or use at scale.

We recommend Chatwoot for a technical support team willing to pilot the hosted and self-hosted tradeoffs. We would not self-host only to avoid a fee; the company must want and staff the added control.

## A shared inbox can reduce lost context

Chatwoot can place messages from several channels in one queue. Agents can assign a chat, add private notes, and see the customer history. This helps when support moves between shifts or teams.

A vendor customer story says the inbox became essential to sales. Another founder said the channel view changed customer work across several companies. These are selected stories, not independent proof.

<Callout>
Chatwoot is valuable when the next agent can continue without making the customer repeat everything.
</Callout>

Test one contact across web chat, email, and a required social channel. Check matching, assignment, tags, notes, attachments, search, and close rules. Make sure separate customers do not merge.

The chat box can match the brand. Test load speed, phone use, consent, blocked scripts, offline messages, and access by keyboard or screen reader.

## AI replies need a narrow role

Captain can draft answers from support knowledge. That may handle repeat questions and help agents reply faster. The packet did not include outside evidence on its accuracy.

Use a small approved knowledge set with known answers. Ask about a normal case, an exception, and a fact that is absent. The AI should hand off or admit uncertainty rather than invent a policy.

Review source files, retention, model providers, and training terms. Customer messages may include private account details. Do not turn on broad AI access before security approval.

Track corrections during the pilot. AI is useful when it reduces agent work without raising the number of wrong or risky replies.

## Self-hosting trades vendor work for internal work

Self-hosting can give a firm more control over where chats and customer data live. It also makes the firm own updates, security, email, channel links, storage, backups, and uptime.

Install the intended version in a test environment. Connect one channel, restore a backup, rotate a secret, and update the service. Measure staff time and server cost.

Channel firms still sit outside the server. WhatsApp, social media, email, and AI services have their own APIs and rules. Self-hosting Chatwoot does not make the full path private.

The hosted service is a better fit when the team wants the inbox without running it. Compare the live price with the full internal cost of self-hosting.

## Support and price need direct evidence

The packet did not include enough current price or outside support detail for an exact judgment. Guides exist, but a hard live issue may need more than a help page.

Open a real channel or migration question during the trial. Learn what support comes with the intended hosted plan and what self-hosted users can buy.

Export contacts, conversations, help articles, and attachments. Prove that a restore or move keeps links and time stamps understandable.

## Verdict

Chatwoot offers a capable open support stack with channels, live chat, a help center, AI, and deployment choice. It can fit a technical team that values control.

The limited outside evidence prevents a stronger recommendation. Self-hosting also creates a serious operating duty.

Run one real support queue and restore a test backup. Keep Chatwoot when channel records stay correct, AI hands off safely, and the team can own its hosting choice. Skip self-hosting when nobody is responsible for updates and recovery.
