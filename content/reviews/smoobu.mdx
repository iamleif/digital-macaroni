Smoobu is a property tool for vacation apartments and small hotels. It syncs calendars across booking sites and can also handle prices, direct booking, and a rental website. A 14-day trial is available without a card.

The basic value is easy to understand: one calendar can reduce double bookings and give the owner a clearer view. The concern is reliability. Accounts describe long outages and a website-builder update that forced a rebuild during peak season.

We recommend Smoobu only after a live channel and website pilot. It may suit a small rental business, but the owner needs a backup for bookings and guest contact when the service is unavailable.

## Calendar sync is the first feature to trust

One host said Smoobu made multi-channel calendars and prices easier to manage. That is the core job. A booking on one site should close the same dates everywhere else.

Connect one property during the card-free trial. Block a date, change a price, make a booking, modify it, and cancel it. Open every channel directly after each step.

<Callout>
Smoobu is useful only when its shared calendar matches what guests can actually book.
</Callout>

Record how long updates take. A short delay may be normal, while a long or missed sync can create a double booking. Keep alerts or a manual check during the first live weeks.

Export future bookings and guest details. The owner should have an up-to-date list available when the main screen cannot load.

## Website changes can become business changes

One long-term user said a new website builder forced a rebuild during peak season. The move led to downtime, lost bookings, and language display problems. Smoobu responded, but the customer still faced the disruption.

A built-in website is convenient because rates and open dates can connect with the booking system. It also ties a public sales page to product updates the owner does not control.

Build a test site before replacing the current one. Check every language, phone view, search page, booking step, payment, policy, and contact form. Keep a copy of all text, images, and domain settings.

Ask how long the old builder or design remains available after a major change. A forced migration needs notice, preview, support, and a safe rollback.

## Outages need a written fallback

Some accounts described outages lasting hours in June 2026, including one report of daily disruption over ten hours. The packet does not establish the full scale or how many customers were affected.

The operational risk is still clear. Staff need access to arrivals, guest phone numbers, door details, and open dates during an outage. Keep that day's essential information outside Smoobu.

Run the mobile and support paths during the trial. Learn how service status is reported and whether booking-channel links keep working when the dashboard is down.

Support accounts are mixed. Some users received useful guided setup, while a potential client with more than 50 properties described a missed sales call and no useful follow-up. Test the route included with the intended plan.

## Trial value depends on the full monthly cost

The 14-day no-card trial is a good way to test without an accidental renewal. The evidence did not include enough current price detail for an exact example.

Price properties, channels, website, direct booking, payments, users, and support. Include any fee tied to booking value. Compare the cost with the risk and time saved by shared calendars.

Use the trial during normal work, not only a quiet setup day. One real booking and one guest message reveal more than a polished sample account.

## Verdict

Smoobu can give a small rental operator one useful view of calendars, prices, and bookings. Guided setup and a no-card trial lower the barrier to testing it.

Outage and website-update accounts prevent a stronger recommendation. Both can affect bookings at the worst possible time.

Pilot one property, one live booking, and the public website. Keep Smoobu when every channel stays aligned and the outage fallback is workable. Skip it when the business cannot tolerate another forced website rebuild or long loss of access.
