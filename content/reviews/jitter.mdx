Jitter makes static designs move in a web browser. A designer can animate text, logos, charts, or app screens, then export a video, GIF, or small file for a website. We recommend Jitter to designers and small marketing teams that already work in Figma. It is a fast way to make short product clips and simple web motion without learning a large video package first.

Jitter makes the controls easier. The quality still depends on timing, sound, design, and editing judgment.

## Figma import is the main advantage

Jitter can import the separate layers from a Figma design. The designer can keep text, shapes, and screen parts editable instead of rebuilding the whole layout from flat images. That is useful for a product screen where a menu opens and a chart grows, or for a logo that needs a short motion. Presets and a visual timeline control when each part appears or moves.

Fast browser use, the Figma handoff, templates, and familiar design controls receive broad praise. Product Hunt had 48 favorable reviews in the evidence set, while other scored samples were much smaller.

The advantage is weaker when the work does not begin in Figma. Jitter can still build designs and import media, but the editable handoff is the feature that saves the most setup.

Skip Jitter when the job needs long video editing, complex effects, or detailed character work. Those projects need deeper control than this browser tool aims to provide.

## Free is enough to finish a real example

The Free plan included the main motion tools, more than 300 templates, Figma import, team work, and video, GIF, and Lottie export. Lottie is a small animation file used inside websites and apps.

A free workspace could hold three files. Exports were limited to 720p at 30 frames per second and carried a Jitter watermark. Those limits still allow a complete trial. Bring in a real Figma design, animate the part that matters, export it, and show it to someone who has not seen the static layout.

Pro removed the watermark and allowed unlimited files and folders. It raised export to 1080p at 60 frames per second and added ProRes and WebM files. Max added 4K, very high frame rates, clear backgrounds, and the option to save each frame. Ultra mainly increased AI use.

The official page offered monthly and yearly billing per editor with a 15% yearly saving. Verified numeric prices were missing from the page response, so check the live total before paying.

## Easy motion can still be poor motion

A preset can make an object slide onto the screen. It cannot decide whether that movement helps the viewer understand the message. In one Motion Design community discussion, people critiqued a marketing clip made with Jitter. Their concerns were pacing, sound, and editing choices rather than the software controls.

The designer still decides when motion starts, how long it lasts, and how much appears at once. Several smooth effects can compete for attention and make a simple message harder to follow.

Show the draft without explaining it. Ask the viewer what changed and where their eye went first. If the answer is wrong, remove motion before adding more.

A professional motion designer may still use Jitter for a quick product screen. The same person may return to After Effects or another full editor for work that needs deep control.

## Upgrade only when the output blocks delivery

Use a project the team already needs. Import the real design and add only the motion that helps explain it. Check the exported file in the place where it will be published. The viewer should understand the message without a tour. The export also needs the correct size, frame rate, background, and file type.

Upgrade to Pro when the watermark, 720p output, or three-file limit blocks delivery. Choose Max only when a client, editor, or publishing system requires 4K, a clear background, or separate frames.

High output numbers do not improve weak timing. Paying for 120 frames per second makes no sense when the final channel uses far less.

We recommend Jitter for short Figma-based product clips, logo motion, social graphics, and simple web animation. It removes a heavy setup from a small design job. Look elsewhere for a full marketing film, complex creative effects, or a shortcut to motion-design judgment. Jitter helps a designer make things move. The designer still has to know what should move and why.
