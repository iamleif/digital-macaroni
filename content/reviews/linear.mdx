Linear is a tool for planning software work. Teams use it to track bugs, plan short work cycles, and see what may ship next. Its main promise is simple: less clutter and more speed.

People often praise the clean screen, quick search, keyboard controls, and links to Slack and GitHub. Many of the happiest users work on small software teams.

## What people like

Speed is the clearest theme. People say it takes little effort to create an issue, find work, or update a task. They also like that Linear does not ask them to build a large system before they can begin.

That is a real benefit for small engineering teams. A project tool only works when people keep it up to date. If each update feels slow, the board soon stops showing the truth. Linear removes much of that small daily pain.

<Callout>
Linear's best feature is not one button. It is how little effort the whole tool asks from you.
</Callout>

People moving from Jira often find Linear easier to manage. The simpler setup can help a small team stay focused instead of turning project management into its own job.

## Where the limits show

The praise and the complaints come from the same choice. Linear gives teams a clear way to work. It does not offer endless control.

Teams often run into weak reports, fewer custom choices, and missing tools for complex work. Labels can become a way to patch missing structure. Linear may work well for one team and become harder to manage across a larger company.

This does not make Linear unfinished. It shows what the company values. Linear would rather give you one clear system than let you build any system you want.

Linear is also built for software teams. Marketing, sales, and other groups may find its words and workflow too narrow. A company that wants one shared tool for every group may need to look elsewhere.

## Support deserves more care

Product feedback is mostly warm. Service feedback is more mixed. Some customers report problems with account access, workspace data, leaving the service, and getting help.

These reports do not prove that most customers have the same trouble. They do show how serious the risk can feel when a project tool holds a team's work.

That is why the support score sits well below the product score. A polished app is not enough if people feel unsure about their account or data when something goes wrong.

## Who should use Linear?

Linear is a strong fit for small software teams that want a fast issue tracker. It is best when the team is happy to follow a clear system instead of building its own.

It is a weaker fit for large firms, teams with strict approval steps, or companies that want one project tool for every department. It may also disappoint leaders who need deep reports without sending data to another tool.

## Verdict

Linear earns much of its good name. It is fast, calm, and easy to keep current. But the same rules that make it simple can block complex teams. Support and account complaints also keep us from giving it a top score. For a small software team, it remains a strong choice. For everyone else, test the limits before moving your work.
