Amie combines a calendar, tasks, and meeting notes in one polished app. It connects with Google Calendar, Outlook, Slack, Notion, and task tools. It can also record and summarize online meetings without adding a visible bot to the call.

The design earns praise, and the combined daily view has real appeal. The product still has gaps. Accounts describe Things 3 tasks disappearing after completion, awkward alert controls, no natural-language event entry, and missing iPad landscape support.

We recommend Amie for someone who values a visual day plan and can test every task and calendar link. We would keep the old task source until two-way sync proves dependable.

## Calendar and tasks can share one daily view

Amie lets a user see meetings and tasks together. A task can be placed into an open block instead of living on a separate list with no time set aside.

That helps someone whose day mixes calls and focused work. A person with a stable calendar and short to-do list may not need another planning layer.

<Callout>
Amie is strongest when the combined day changes what the user actually finishes.
</Callout>

Connect one calendar and one task app first. Create, move, complete, reopen, and delete a task from both sides. Check due dates, time zones, repeat rules, and what stays in history.

One Things 3 user said completed tasks disappeared from Amie's calendar, leaving the two apps inconsistent. That may be a design choice or sync gap, but it matters to anyone who uses the calendar as a record of completed work.

## Meeting notes avoid a visible bot

Amie can record meetings across Zoom, Google Meet, Slack Huddles, and Teams without placing a bot participant in the room. It creates summaries and action items, while private notes can guide the result.

The discreet approach may feel better for internal calls. Recording still needs clear consent and a privacy check. Guests should know what is captured, where it is stored, and who can read it.

Test a meeting with known decisions, names, dates, and tasks. Compare the summary with the recording. A vendor customer story claims a large productivity gain, but that selected result is not a forecast for every team.

Check what happens when the call changes tools, uses a headset, or includes several speakers. Export notes and tasks so the meeting record does not depend on one app.

## Missing basics can matter more than design polish

Some users want natural-language event entry, such as typing a meeting and date in one line. The evidence says Amie lacked that ability at the time reviewed. A beautiful calendar can still feel slow when common entry takes too many controls.

One mobile user found the wheel control awkward for setting an alert one day before an event. Others asked for iPad and landscape support. Test the device used during travel or meetings, not only the desktop app.

Create ten normal events and tasks during the trial. Count taps and corrections. Check recurring events, all-day items, alerts, guests, video links, and time zones.

Design quality is valuable when it makes the day easier to read. It is cosmetic when the user keeps returning to another app for basic edits.

## Plan value depends on the integrations used

The evidence packet did not include enough current price detail for an exact example. One account mentioned Apple Calendar behind a paid plan, though the current status was unclear.

List every required calendar, task app, meeting tool, and device. Confirm which plan supports each one and whether sync works both ways. Start monthly until the integrations survive daily use.

Support evidence was limited, with one note of developer response to feedback. Send a real sync question during the trial and test account recovery.

## Verdict

Amie offers a distinctive and attractive way to combine calendar, tasks, and meeting notes. Bot-free summaries and time-blocked tasks can make a busy day easier to manage.

Sync and missing basics prevent a stronger recommendation. A planner must preserve task truth across every connected app.

Use Amie for two full weeks with the real calendar and task source. Keep it when completed work stays correct, notes capture decisions, and event entry is fast enough. Skip it when the design cannot offset daily sync repair.
