Crisp brings web chat, email, WhatsApp, and Messenger into one team inbox. It also keeps a customer history and offers AI tools. A 14-day trial does not require a card.

The range can help a small support team. The evidence raises hard concerns. They include sudden account bans, key free tools behind paid plans, a hard deletion path, and PDF text cut off during AI training.

We recommend Crisp only after testing the real business category, inbox, API, and AI source files. We would not make it the only sales chat until account approval and export are proven.

## One inbox can keep customer history together

A customer may start in website chat and return by email or WhatsApp. Crisp can put those messages beside the same profile so the next agent does not ask for the story again.

This matters when several people share support. A solo owner with one quiet channel may not need a full inbox and CRM.

<Callout>
Crisp is strongest when customer context follows the conversation across channels.
</Callout>

Test one contact across every needed channel. Check name matching, consent, staff ownership, internal notes, attachments, and search. Make sure two different people are not merged by mistake.

Use the API or webhooks only when they remove real copying. One developer praised active support while connecting an existing bot, even on a free plan. Run the same failure and retry checks used for any customer system.

## Account rules need approval before launch

One sexual-wellness store said the account was banned overnight after setup. The slow review led to lost sales. Some business types carry extra risk rules, even when the company believes it is valid.

Ask Crisp whether the real category and content are allowed. Use the real website and business details during the trial. Do not launch the chat widget across the store before approval is stable.

Keep another contact route visible. Export customer and conversation records on a schedule. A suspension should not remove the only way to reach active prospects.

Deletion also needs a test. Some users found that removing a workspace and account required confusing steps. Learn the order for export, cancellation, workspace removal, and account deletion.

## AI knowledge needs known-answer checks

One mobile-support provider said PDF content was silently cut off when used to train the AI. Wrong answers continued for months because the missing text was not clearly flagged. Crisp acknowledged the bug in the reported exchange.

Upload a small source with facts at the start, middle, and end. Ask questions with known answers. Check source links where available.

Repeat with a long PDF, table, and updated file. The system should warn when it cannot read all content. A confident reply from a partial file is dangerous for customer support.

Set a handoff rule for money, account access, legal issues, and any answer the AI cannot prove. Review actual chats during the pilot.

## Free access may not cover useful automation

One small-business user expected automated replies on the free plan and left when the feature required payment. A free inbox can still be useful, but buyers should map each required feature to the live plan.

Price channels, agents, automation, AI use, CRM needs, history, API access, and support. The evidence packet did not include enough current price detail for an exact example.

Start with the card-free trial and a monthly plan where possible. Test support with a real account or AI question before paying longer.

## Verdict

Crisp offers a broad shared inbox with customer history, channels, API access, and AI. It can reduce scattered support work for a small team.

Account access and silent AI source failure are serious risks. Both can hurt sales and customer trust.

Run the real business through approval, answer known questions from a full PDF, and export the inbox. Keep Crisp when every channel and source stays correct. Skip it when the business category remains uncertain or AI hides missing content.
