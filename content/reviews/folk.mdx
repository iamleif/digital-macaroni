folk is a light CRM for small teams that win work through people they know. It keeps people, companies, messages, meetings, notes, deal stages, and follow-up work in one shared place.

We recommend folk when a team has outgrown a spreadsheet and wants one clear next step for each person. It suits agencies, recruiters, partnership teams, and small sales groups.

The simple design is the reason to choose it. That same focus gets in the way when managers need deep forecasts, strict access rules, or many custom sales reports.

## folk turns a contact list into shared memory

A spreadsheet can store a name and deal stage. The email thread, last meeting, source of the relationship, and next reminder often stay in separate inboxes.

folk can connect email, calendar, and WhatsApp accounts so the contact record holds more of that history. Its browser tool can capture a person from LinkedIn or another page. Contact enrichment can add public details such as a work email, company, or job title.

People can be placed into groups and shown as a pipeline. A pipeline is a set of steps that a relationship moves through, from a new lead to a booked meeting or signed deal.

Quick setup, contact history, browser capture, and added contact data receive broad praise. The large G2 sample gives that pattern useful weight.

These tools matter when several teammates share relationships. A solo user with a clean address book may gain little. The team must actually add notes, assign an owner, and set the next action.

## Messy contacts are the real setup test

Saved time from added contact data depends on correct matches. Two people can share a name, someone may have changed jobs, and an email sync can create a second record for a person already imported.

Use a dirty sample during the trial. Import 100 contacts with duplicates, missing companies, old jobs, and several email addresses. Inspect every suggested match and look for messages attached to the wrong record.

Decide which source wins when details conflict. A title entered by the team may be newer than the one found by folk. A CRM loses value when people stop trusting its contact data.

Test the browser tool against existing records too. Capture someone who already exists and someone with two current roles. Check whether folk updates the right record or creates a copy.

Every CRM import needs cleanup. The trial should show how much work folk needs before the full address book moves.

## Shared email history needs access checks

A shared contact history helps another teammate understand the relationship. It can also expose a message that should stay private.

Standard included the last 30 interactions, while Premium included the full history. Enterprise added more controls for private messages and access by group.

Connect two test accounts before linking every inbox. Include a normal client thread and a sensitive internal message. Check what another member can see, remove access, and confirm what remains.

Outgoing email needs its own test. Standard supported campaigns and up to 2,000 messages per member each month. Premium raised that to 5,000 and added timed follow-up emails that can stop after a reply.

Send a small campaign to addresses the team controls. Try a reply and an unsubscribe, then add a bounced address and someone who answered through another channel. Make sure follow-ups stop at the right time.

folk now lists a mobile app, so old complaints about its absence are stale. The current test is whether someone can find a contact, add a note, move the deal, and set a reminder after a meeting.

## Premium carries many growing-team features

Standard cost $24 per member each month with yearly billing when the evidence was collected. Monthly billing was $30. It included pipelines, email campaigns, enrichment, AI fields, the browser tool, account sync, and integrations.

Premium cost $48 per member with yearly billing or $60 monthly. It added custom record types, email follow-ups, workspace roles, dashboards, API access, more credits, and full interaction history.

Enterprise started at $80 per member with yearly billing. It added deeper privacy controls, group permissions, guided setup, custom integrations, and priority support.

A five-person team paid $1,440 per year on Standard and $2,880 on Premium. That jump needs a real use for dashboards, roles, API access, full history, or custom records.

Usage limits matter alongside seats. Email-finding credits, AI credits, outgoing messages, and synced accounts vary by plan. Estimate those from the team's actual work.

We recommend folk to a small team that wins work through close ties. Skip it when the weekly sales meeting depends on deep forecasts, approvals, sales areas, or detailed reports.

Import the messy contact sample, connect two accounts with sensitive messages, send a small campaign, use the mobile app, and rebuild the weekly report. Choose folk when normal teammates keep records current and each relationship has a clear next step. Look elsewhere when managers rebuild the numbers outside the CRM or the team spends too much time fixing history.
