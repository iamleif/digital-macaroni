The method

Use it first.
Write second.

A good review needs a point of view, but it also needs receipts. This is the basic loop behind every Digital Macaroni verdict.

  1. 01

    Give it a real job.

    We start from scratch and use the product to get something done. No guided-demo theatre. No review assembled from a feature page.

  2. 02

    Follow the friction.

    We keep notes on setup, dead ends, unclear language, missing features, pricing, and the moments that are surprisingly good.

  3. 03

    Make the call.

    We compare the promise with the reality and write a verdict. Context matters more than a tidy spreadsheet average.

  4. 04

    Look beyond our own test.

    We research what other people have said across review platforms, customer communities, forums, and the wider web—looking for patterns our own time with the product might not reveal.

What we pay attention to

Four lenses.
Not four promises.

These keep reviews consistent. They are not a claim that we have seen every edge case, spoken to every support agent, or lived through a year of invoices.

01

Onboarding

Can you get anywhere useful?

We look at the path from first click to first result: setup, guidance, defaults, imports, and the amount of work required before the product earns its keep.

02

Product

Does the core job hold up?

This carries the most weight. We test the work the product says it is built for, then pay attention to reliability, speed, limitations, and everyday friction.

03

Support

What help is actually there?

We inspect the documentation and help experience. When we contact support, we say how and why. When we do not, we say that too.

04

Billing

Is the money part honest?

We check pricing clarity, plan boundaries, trial terms, upgrade pressure, and cancellation information. We do not pretend to have tested a charge or refund we never made.

About the score

A verdict.
Not a formula.

The four lenses inform the final score, but we do not average them mechanically. A serious product failure can matter more than four pleasant setup screens. The number is editorial judgment, made visible.

9.0–10Exceptional

A category standout. Easy to recommend.

8.0–8.9Great

Very good, with compromises worth knowing.

7.0–7.9Good

Useful, capable, and meaningfully imperfect.

6.0–6.9Mixed

The rough edges change the recommendation.

Below 6Hard to recommend

The product asks too much in return.

The important part

Certainty is cheap.
Specifics are useful.

We tell you what we used, what happened, and where our test stopped. If a company points out a factual error, we correct it. We do not negotiate the opinion.
