# Digital Macaroni review voice

The target voice is an informed person explaining software to another person. It is plain, connected, useful, and willing to say when a feature is only good enough.

## Target example

If you do not have a website, Lodgify's website builder is useful. It is limited, but it gets the job done. You can put your properties online and let guests book without going through Airbnb or Vrbo.

If you already have a website, this feature will not matter much. You can add Lodgify's booking form to the site you already have, but you probably do not need Lodgify to build the rest of it.

The bigger question is whether Lodgify can keep your Airbnb, Vrbo, and direct bookings on the same calendar. Some hosts had old bookings or blocked dates go missing while connecting their accounts. That creates a real risk of two guests booking the same property.

Why it works:

- It explains the feature before judging it.
- The judgment uses normal words: useful, limited, gets the job done.
- It tells two different readers whether the feature matters to them.
- It explains the unfamiliar part through an action, not a definition.
- The concern comes from a real customer event rather than a dramatic hypothetical.

## Avoid this voice

“The case is much weaker if your website is already built. At this point, you are buying Lodgify mainly for channel management, and this is the part of the product we trust least.”

Why it fails:

- “The case is weaker” sounds like an analyst presenting a recommendation.
- “At this point” narrates an internal decision framework.
- “Channel management” assumes the reader knows software marketing language.
- “The part we trust least” performs authority without explaining what went wrong.

Keep the analysis in the notebook. Put the explanation in the article.
