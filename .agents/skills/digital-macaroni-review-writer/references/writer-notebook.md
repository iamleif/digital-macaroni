# Writer's notebook schema

Build this notebook from the evidence packet and the underlying source material.

## Research scope

- Review type: research-based or hands-on
- Product access: what was used, if anything
- Sources opened and access dates
- Reviews actually read, separate from displayed platform totals
- Main research limits

## Reader and job

- Primary reader
- Business or team size
- What they are trying to get done
- What they use now
- What happens if the product fails

## Product facts

For each fact, record the statement, source, date checked, and any plan or version limit.

## Experience records

Create one record for each account that adds context:

- Source and date
- Positive, negative, or mixed
- User context
- Task attempted
- Product or connected service involved
- What happened
- Result
- Vendor response
- Useful customer wording
- Confidence and reason

Do not manufacture missing fields.

## Repeated patterns

For each pattern, record:

- What happened, in plain language
- Accounts that support it
- Accounts that conflict with it
- Which buyers, plans, services, or dates it applies to
- What it changes about the recommendation

## Feature translation

For every important feature or category term, record:

- Company or industry term
- What it actually lets the host do
- Who cares about it
- When it does not matter
- The simplest words you would use to explain it aloud

## Conflict ledger

Write each conflict as two claims that can both be true. Explain what condition resolves the conflict.

Example: “Setup help receives praise” and “urgent support follow-through is mixed” can coexist because they concern different stages of the customer relationship.

## Missing evidence

List unanswered questions that would materially change the verdict. Never turn an unanswered question into article texture.

## Spoken language bank

Save concrete nouns, verbs, and useful customer phrasing. Add plain replacements for jargon. Do not save promotional adjectives or analyst language for reuse.
